Register

points on a plate

You put a picture on the screen and mark exact places on it. The marks are stored as fractions of the picture — 0 to 1 across, 0 to 1 down — so they survive any size or crop. Then you hand the whole thing to Claude, and review what comes back.

Do this

  1. Get a picture in. Drag an image file onto the middle of the screen. (Or Image ▾ → choose a file.)
  2. Pick what you are marking. The list on the left is the layers. Click one — say structure — and everything you draw goes there.
  3. Draw. Click a tool at the top left. Pen clicks out a line, one point per click; press Enter to finish it, Esc to throw it away. Rect, Circle and Ellipse are drag-to-draw. Point drops a single numbered point.
  4. Fix it. Switch to Select. Every white square is a handle — drag it. Arrow keys nudge it one pixel of the original image at a time. The panel on the right lists the exact numbers; type into them.
  5. Hand it over. Ask Claude → say what you want → it copies one line. Paste that line into a Claude chat. When Claude answers, open the document again and you get a list of every single change, each one saying how far it moved. Click any of them to drop it. Keep saves what is left.

What is where

LeftTools, then the layers, then the preset settings and view options
MiddleThe picture and your marks. Scroll to pan, ⌘-scroll to zoom, 0 to fit
RightWhatever is selected, and its numbers — type exact values here

The strip along the bottom always shows where the cursor is, in both fractions and original pixels. The little square that follows your cursor is a ×6 magnifier — that is how you land on a one-pixel edge.

The layers

structure — lines you draw by hand from the picture. The drawing. trackers — the ten numbered points the reading fixes. mask keep — only what is inside this gets read at all. mask drop — cut this out (a watermark, a corner). noHatch — no shading in here. forceBand — shade this region at a set weight regardless. darkKey — where the drawing's own ink is traced into line. hatch plate — a region shaded at a fixed angle.

Only structure and trackers matter for most jobs. The rest are settings for the Mode Mode plate, and you can ignore them until you need them.

Preview

Preview runs the real Mode Mode pipeline on your picture and draws the actual plates over it — the same code the site uses, not an imitation. The picture fades so you can see them. Press it again to hide them. d hides your marks so you can judge the plate alone.
