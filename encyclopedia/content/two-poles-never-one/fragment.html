A magnet has two poles. The field lines leave the north pole and curve back into the south — every line closes. There is no end where they simply stop.

Cut a magnet and you get two magnets, each with its own north and south. Cut those and you get four. You never reach a single pole — that's ∇·B = 0, and it has no known exception.
