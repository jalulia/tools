Skin worlds / V06 / proposed Window 4 of 7

A skin changes how Mindgut looks. Never what it does.

Seven ways of knowing, each taken from a working practice and run as a live system on the same record. The window holds words. The record holds only marks. Type a line and it enters all seven.

I / The GardenerPoint to read a ring

A cut limb keeps every ring.

A gardener who takes off a limb reads the cut. Each exchange laid down one ring: open where you spoke, dense where Mir answered, wide when there was much to say. Only the outermost layer is alive. Everything inside it is record, and none of it is redrawn.After tree-ring reading (dendrochronology). Heartwood, sapwood, cambium.

II / The NavigatorMove across the water

You do not look at the chart. You feel the swell.

Marshallese navigators built charts of how islands bend the ocean swell, learned them ashore, and read the water with the body at sea. Here the swell is one grating and every island returns its own rings. Where they cross the water knots into a lattice, and that lattice is how an island is known before it is seen.After Marshallese wave charts (mattang, meddo, rebbelib). An outsider's reading: a chart was fully legible only to its maker.

III / The WeaverPoint at the cloth

Your words are the warp. Mir's are the weft.

A tartan is written down as a thread count: a letter for the color, a number for the threads. Here every word is one stripe, as wide as its letters and dyed by its channel. Your lines are warped up the cloth and Mir's replies are woven across it. Where the two of you land on the same channel, one dye crosses itself and the square comes out solid.After the thread-count notation of tartan setts, as kept by the Scottish Register of Tartans. Woven and stitched with the T-04 weave and satin embroidery effects.

IV / The AstronomerPoint at a strand

The record, printed by daylight.

John Herschel, an astronomer, worked out the cyanotype in 1842: lay things on sensitized paper, leave it in the sun, and whatever was covered stays white. Here the helix is laid on the sheet end-on, 42 strands to the turn, the first strand at the center. The threads laid across it are recall: from your latest line back to the first strand, to the three strands most like it, and to the three least like it.After Herschel's cyanotype, 1842. The record is simulated and the recall rule shown is a stand-in. Printed with the T-04 cyanotype effect.

V / The ComposerSelect a plate to sound it

Sand gathers where the plate holds still.

Chladni drew a bow down the edge of a plate strewn with sand, and the sand ran to the lines that were not moving. Each exchange in the window is one plate. Your line sets how many waves stand across it, Mir's reply sets how many stand down it, and the figure is where the two cancel. When the two of you are far apart, the sand has not settled.After Ernst Chladni, Entdeckungen über die Theorie des Klanges, 1787. Figures follow cos mπx cos nπy − cos nπx cos mπy. Grains set with the T-04 stipple effect.

VI / The PrinterDrag to pull the plates

Three plates. Register is the clash.

Color printing splits one image into plates and brings them back together on marks cut into the block. Each letter of the window prints on the plate of its own channel. The plates sit out of register by the measured distance between you and Mir.After the kento register marks of Japanese woodblock printing, and the rotated halftone screen

VII / The ArchivistOriginal order / by neighbor

Keep the order it came in. Then break it.

Archives keep records in the order they arrived, because the order is evidence. Warburg did the opposite: books shelved beside their good neighbor, images pinned to black panels by affinity. The same strands read both ways, as they came or by what they sit closest to.After the principle of original order, and Aby Warburg's Bilderatlas Mnemosyne, 1924–29
