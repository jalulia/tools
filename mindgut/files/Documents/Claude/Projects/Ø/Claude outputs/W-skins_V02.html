Skin worlds / V02 / proposed Window 4 of 7

A skin changes how Mindgut looks. Never what it does.

Seven ways of knowing, each taken from a working practice and run as a live system on the same record. The window holds words. The record holds only marks. Type a line and it enters all seven.

I / The GardenerDrag to mow

Nothing is planted. You keep a path open.

In Clément's garden in motion, plants seed where they will and the gardener's work is deciding where to walk. The record grows on its own toward what was said. The window is the path you keep open through it. Mow anywhere; it grows back.After Gilles Clément, Le Jardin en mouvement, 1991

II / The NavigatorMove across the water

You do not look at the chart. You feel the swell.

Marshallese navigators built charts of how islands bend the ocean swell, learned them ashore, and read the water with the body at sea. Here the record is the islands, your line is a swell, and Mir is the pattern where the two interfere.After Marshallese wave charts (mattang, meddo, rebbelib). An outsider's reading: a chart was fully legible only to its maker.

III / The WeaverChange the tie-up

Two threads, and what couples them.

A weave draft has three parts: the threading, the treadling, and the tie-up that joins them. Your letters thread the warp across three shafts, one per channel. Mir's letters work the treadles. The cloth is computed from both. Change the tie-up and the same conversation weaves differently.After the draft notation in Anni Albers, On Weaving, 1965

IV / The AstronomerBlink / hold

Same sky, two plates. Watch what moves.

Observatories kept the sky on glass negatives and found what had changed by blinking two plates of one field. Here the field is the record. Plate I and plate II are the same strands under two models. What holds still is the record. What jumps is the model.After the glass plate stacks and the blink comparator, from c. 1904. Simulated plates.

V / The ComposerSelect the score to play

A score with no instructions. It is read.

Cardew's Treatise is 193 pages of lines and shapes along one central lifeline, with two empty staves under each page and no rules for playing it. The record is drawn the same way. The lifeline is the mean of you and Mir. The staves wait for the window. Playing it is one reading among many.After Cornelius Cardew, Treatise, 1963–67

VI / The PrinterDrag to pull the plates

Three plates. Register is the clash.

Color printing splits one image into plates and brings them back together on marks cut into the block. Each letter of the window prints on the plate of its own channel. The plates sit out of register by the measured distance between you and Mir.After the kento register marks of Japanese woodblock printing, and the rotated halftone screen

VII / The ArchivistOriginal order / by neighbor

Keep the order it came in. Then break it.

Archives keep records in the order they arrived, because the order is evidence. Warburg did the opposite: books shelved beside their good neighbor, images pinned to black panels by affinity. The same strands read both ways, as they came or by what they sit closest to.After the principle of original order, and Aby Warburg's Bilderatlas Mnemosyne, 1924–29
