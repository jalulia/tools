Graphic Elements

The brand's visual vocabulary spans geometric forms, icons, grid patterns, and the Ø mark itself — deployed across dark and light surfaces with intentional restraint.

The Ø Mark as Graphic Element

Beyond the logo, the Ø functions as a compositional device — watermark, texture, structural anchor. Three deployment modes:

Wireframe (Stroke)

No fill. 4–12% opacity on dark, 4–8% on light. Anchors compositions as a watermark.

Filled (Background)

Solid fill at 3–8% opacity. Creates depth behind content. Never competes with foreground.

RIGHT-SIDE ANCHOR

Compositional Anchor

Large-scale Ø positioned off-edge (typically right). Creates asymmetric pull. 8–15% opacity.

Impøssible Øbjects (3D)

Retro-tech 3D renders with moody lighting. One per composition max. Dark backgrounds always. Below: wireframe placeholder — final assets are pre-rendered.

Wireframe Placeholder

Isometric wireframe geometry. Represents where pre-rendered 3D assets slot in.

Impossible Geometry

Penrose-inspired forms. The brand's conceptual core — negating impossibility — made visual.

PRE-RENDERED ASSETS
SLOT FOR FINAL RENDER

Asset Slot

3D objects are rendered externally, then composited. One per layout max. Moody, low-key lighting always.

Icon System

One unified system. All white, fine line, consistent 1.2px stroke weight. Dashed lines permitted at the same weight. Ranges from abstract geometry through data visualization to representational objects. Min size: 24px.

Abstract & Geometric

Composition & Layout

Every layout rests on six principles. Applied together, they produce the brand's signature feeling: sophisticated restraint with bold asymmetry.

01

Asymmetric Balance

Offset the primary element. Visual weight balanced without being symmetrical. Never center everything.

02

Generous Negative Space

30–40% breathing room. Premium through restraint. Don't fill every corner.

03

Anchor + Accent

ONE dominant element, 1–2 supporting accents. Never let accents compete with the anchor.

04

Layered Depth

2–3 visual planes: background (color/grid/wash), mid-ground (shapes, photos), foreground (type, data).

05

Edge Bleed

Shapes and patterns extend beyond the frame. The system is larger than any single layout.

06

Consistent Footer

Company name (left), date/section (right), thin rule above. Small ø mark at bottom-right.

Photography & Imagery

The brand does not use stock photography as-is. All imagery is stylized through brand treatments.

Treatments

B&W (light mode primary)

Full desaturation. High contrast. Paired with concentric circles and wireframes on cream.

Moody / Dramatic (dark mode primary)

Low-key lighting with deep shadows. Brand accent color cast for light spills.

Circular crop

Photography masked into circles — the brand's geometric signature. Paired with concentric rings.

Geometric overlay

Thin-line grids or circles superimposed at low opacity. Unifies photos with graphic language.

Subject Matter

Technology & craftWorkspaces, tools, prototypes
ArchitectureBuildings, interiors, structural forms
Abstract / macroTextures, light patterns, materials
Retro-tech3D rendered Impøssible Øbjects

Never

  • Use unfiltered stock photography
  • Use photos with busy, colorful backgrounds
  • Place full-color photos on dark bg without treatment
  • Use photography without at least one brand graphic element
  • Use generic corporate photography (handshakes, teams)

Voice & Tone

Writing Principles

Declarative over tentative.

Say "We build" not "We aim to build."

Concrete over abstract.

Name the thing: brands, boxes, bottles, buildings.

Short over long.

Sentences are punchy. Paragraphs are tight.

Technical confidence.

Speak about technology without dumbing it down.

Warm ambition.

Bold but not arrogant. Confident but not cold.

Rhythmic repetition.

Concrete nouns in parallel structure. Alliteration and cadence are on-brand. "Brands, boxes, bottles, and buildings."

Embrace paradox.

The brand lives in contradiction — negation as invitation, impossibility as doorway. "What seems blocked might actually contain the way through."

Do's & Don'ts

Do

  • Always use the ø character in the company name
  • Default to dark (#0A0A0A) OR warm cream (#F5F0E8) — both primary
  • Use the 4-tier typography system consistently
  • Treat color as accent (≤15% of surface area)
  • Use /01 /02 format for section numbering
  • Keep copy declarative, short, rhythmic, and active voice
  • Mix dark and light slides in long presentations
  • Use line-art wireframes and icon clusters on light backgrounds
  • Apply B&W treatment and circular crops to photography
  • Embrace paradox in voice — negation as invitation

Don't

  • Write "Impossible" without the ø
  • Use colored backgrounds as the default
  • Use generic blues (#0000FF, #007BFF)
  • Set body text in the display font
  • Apply drop shadows or glows to the logo
  • Describe ø co. as an "ad agency"
  • Use ∅ (null set) instead of ø in the name
  • Use pure white (#FFF) as light bg — prefer warm cream
  • Use unfiltered stock photography without brand treatment
  • Over-use gradients — they're for hero moments, not defaults

A Quick Note on Our Name

I chose it to reframe the word.

"Impossible" usually ends the conversation. It signals that something cannot be done. A shutdown. A hard stop.

But there are many ways to do something extraordinary. And I often find "impossible" to be the wrong stopping point. Premature.

By replacing the O with Ø, I wanted to open the word again. The word impossible now has a null in the middle of it. We're negating impossibility.

Not to deny difficulty, but to welcome complexity. To embrace challenge.

The Ø isn't just a letter swap. It's a fold, as we call it in PFD. It signals that something contradictory might still be coherent. That what seems blocked might actually contain the way through.

Kind of like a doorknob. Which can both lock you out, and let you in.

The logo is inspired by the doorknob in Alice in Wonderland (thanks, Gaby). That strange little character who tells Alice she's too big to fit. She'll need to drink something odd, become something smaller, change how she sees in order to enter.

And only when she does, does the world open.

That's how I see this whole endeavor. Not as a perfect map, but as a door. Not a company that says "trust me," but a paradox that says "turn this until it clicks."

The Ø in the logo marks the place where negation becomes invitation. A reminder that real breakthroughs often come disguised as contradictions — often in unlikely hands.

And that folding those contradictions, not avoiding them, is what lets us move forward.

This is a door we all have the key to.
You just have to unlock your mind to find it.*
