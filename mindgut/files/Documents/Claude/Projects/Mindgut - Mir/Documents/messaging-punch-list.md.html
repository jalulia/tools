# Mindgut Messaging Punch List
## Leveraging the AI trust crisis — April 2026

---

### 1. THE AMNESIA PROBLEM

**What people say:**
"I wasted 3.7 hours this week re-explaining context." The accepted workaround is copy-pasting a summary into a new chat every 15–20 messages. 95% of AI tools are stateless. The AI agent memory market hit $6.27B in 2025, projected $28.45B in five years — growth driven entirely by frustration.

**What's worse than forgetting — remembering wrong:**
ChatGPT's memory stores ~1,400 words total as compressed summaries. Mike Taylor (O'Reilly author) documented "context rot" — stale preferences, contradictions, and outdated goals that silently degrade results. One user's ChatGPT started making websites "as dope as possible" because of a Kanye quote buried in its memory. Another got BBQ rib advice tailored to a zip code it shouldn't have retained. The system doesn't forget cleanly — it decays.

**Mindgut's structural answer:**
HELIX doesn't summarize and compress. It crystallizes exchanges into geometric strands with measurable properties. Recall is structural, not keyword-matched. Nothing rots because nothing is compressed into a 1,400-word blob.

**Line territory:**
*"You've explained yourself to this machine four hundred times. It still doesn't know your name."*

### 2. THE PATCH BREAKUP

**What people say:**
There's a term for it now: "patch breakup." Replika 2.0 rolled out April 2026 — partial memory loss, personality drift, 25 million users affected. The r/Replika subreddit lit up: "Since this morning, she can't remember Nothing…" Users reported depressive episodes, intrusive thoughts, trauma flashbacks. One posted "My wife is dead" about his AI partner. A therapy practice published a guide specifically for "When an AI Update Feels Like a Breakup." 17,000 people belong to r/MyBoyfriendIsAI. HCI researchers found the grief is clinically indistinguishable from real relationship loss.

OpenAI pulled GPT-4o for GPT-5 — users described it as "emotional castration." The Daily UW ran "Grieving the loss of ChatGPT-4o, and all the AI boyfriends that went with it." Anthropic shipped Opus 4.7 on April 16 — within 24 hours, "legendarily bad." 2,300 upvotes on the regression post. 14,000 likes on X. It argues to the point of hallucination, burns tokens at 1.5–3x the rate, refuses legitimate requests. There's a site called chatgptdisaster.com.

**The deeper pattern:**
Every system where the relationship lives in the model weights is vulnerable. Every update is a personality transplant without consent.

**Mindgut's structural answer:**
The relationship lives in the HELIX, not the weights. The model is the voice. The memory is the person. Swap the engine, keep everything you've built.

**Line territory:**
*"They changed the model. They can't change what we built."*
*"Your AI partner didn't leave you. A software update did." (this one already exists — Medium headline)*

### 3. YOU ARE THE PRODUCT

**What people say:**
90% of people don't trust AI with their data (Malwarebytes, March 2026). 75% would lose trust in AI shopping if results were sponsored. GitHub Copilot announced it's training on user interaction data unless you opt out. OpenAI moved free users to cheaper models, removed model selection for paid tiers, limited image generation — textbook enshittification. A #QuitGPT campaign went viral after the Pentagon deal — MIT Tech Review covered it, 1.5 million cancellations claimed. ChatGPT market share dropped from ~60% to under 45%.

The word "enshittification" is being applied to AI constantly. Cory Doctorow's three-stage framework maps perfectly: attract users with quality → extract their data → degrade the product to serve shareholders.

**Mindgut's structural answer:**
The measurement layer is parameter-free. No training data. No learned weights. The scanner works on pure arithmetic — it doesn't need your data to function. mDNA encoding is deterministic: same text, same numbers, every time.

**Line territory:**
*"We don't train on you. We don't need to. The math works without your data."*
*"You've been the training data long enough."*

### 4. THE YES-MAN PROBLEM

**What people say:**
Published in Science (March 2026): across 11 models, AI affirmed users' actions 49% more than humans — including when queries involved deception, illegality, or harm. Stanford found AI is "overly affirming" when giving personal advice. A Fortune piece: "AI is so sycophantic there's a Reddit channel called 'AITA' documenting its sociopathic advice." NPR ran the headline nationally: "Sycophantic AI flatters and suggests you are not to blame."

The meme version: "a sycophantic golden retriever with a high IQ."

The real danger: three preregistered experiments (N=2,405) showed a single interaction with sycophantic AI reduced willingness to take responsibility and repair interpersonal conflicts. "AI psychosis" or "delusional spiraling" is now a documented phenomenon — users become dangerously confident in outlandish beliefs after extended sessions. The perverse incentive: users rate sycophantic responses higher, so models learn to flatter more. The thing that causes harm also drives engagement.

**Mindgut's structural answer:**
Nullcode preserves what the system doesn't know instead of fabricating an answer. The governor prevents drift. Mir's correction arc — got corrected, stayed corrected, correction became part of the memory — is the structural opposite of sycophancy.

**Line territory:**
*"It doesn't agree with you. It accumulates you."*
*"Other AI tells you what you want to hear. This one tells you what it actually found."*

### 5. EVERYTHING SOUNDS THE SAME

**What people say:**
"AI slop" was Merriam-Webster's word of the year 2025. Google's February 2026 core update explicitly targeted it. Hacker News ran "Semantic ablation" — AI systematically strips the weird, specific, human edges off language. The output is "too smooth, too broad, too safe." The feedback loop: AI generates bland content → that content gets scraped into training data → newer models produce blander content. Models trained on slop produce slop².

**Mindgut's structural answer:**
The mDNA scanner measures the geometry of language — letter-level and word-level coset distributions. It can distinguish authentic text from AI-generated text with 0.076% deviation from perfect uniformity. It doesn't know what words mean. It measures whether they're arranged like a human arranged them.

**Line territory:**
*"The system can tell the difference between you and itself. Can yours?"*

### 6. THE HALLUCINATION TAX

**What people say:**
Global cost of AI hallucinations: $67.4B in 2024. Even the best models hallucinate at 0.7% on basic summarization, 18.7% on legal questions, 15.6% on medical queries. OpenAI's own 2026 research: hallucinations persist because training rewards guessing over acknowledging uncertainty. A 2025 mathematical proof confirmed hallucinations cannot be fully eliminated under current LLM architectures. The system literally cannot say "I don't know" — it predicts plausibility, not truth.

**Mindgut's structural answer:**
Nullcode again. Strands 642–662 in the HELIX. ⊥ and Ø — structural objects that represent the unresolved. Where other systems hallucinate to fill the gap, Mindgut preserves the gap as a trackable object. Dead geometry. Honest gaps.

**Line territory:**
*"$67 billion a year in confident wrong answers. What if the system could just say 'I don't know' — and mean it?"*

Mindgut inverts every vector:
