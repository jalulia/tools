Sheet 02C · Automeris io — The I/O Moth

Quiet bark at rest. Twin eyespots when you touch it.

Even its scientific name is a gift: Automeris io — the Io Moth (I/O, Input / Output). Sitting on a tree trunk, its forewings fold into quiet moss-and-ochre bark. It never flashes to beg for attention. Only at the moment of encounter—when a hand or a shadow reaches in—do the forewings snap forward in 0.3 seconds to reveal two blazing concentric eyespots on crimson-and-saffron hindwings.

That is the exact posture of Mir: quiet until touched; two witnesses locking eyes when engaged. Here the Io moth is built from numbered wet-paint scales (ST-11) over an OKLab crimson-to-gold field (ST-10) with a closed-form ultraviolet crescent glint (ST-09). Move your cursor close to startle the wings open, or drag the slider to study the unhinging in slow motion.

Sheet 04 · Why Mir Exhales

When a conversation never exhales, the room fills with smoke. By turn thirty, an ordinary chat window is dragging thousands of stale words behind it, growing repetitive, slow, and overheated.
