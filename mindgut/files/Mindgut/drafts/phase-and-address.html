Primer № 002 · Binary–Fano–Phase Translation

Phase & Address.

A reversible codec whose atomic token carries two things at once — what a thing is, and what it means. Two four-digit numbers close its alphabet. Every file, every image, every argument becomes traversable at the level of role.
Scroll
On the whitepaper by C. SojkaIntegrated Spec v2 · July 2026

The Binary–Fano–Phase Translation Engine is a small formal object doing a large amount of work. Read narrowly, it's a lookup table with eight rows. Read at full depth, it's a claim about the natural alphabet of meaning — and a proof that two specific numbers span it.

What follows walks you through the machinery from the outside in. The four roles. The problem of collapse. The atomic token that solves it. The two control words. And, at the end, an equation that Chris sent along after reading the first version of this primer — because the version I wrote first didn't yet see how sharp the closure actually was.

Layer · The Alphabet
I

The Four Roles

Every piece of data the codec touches gets tagged with one of four roles. This is the whole vocabulary. Nothing else. Every claim in the paper is built from these four symbols.

A R D 0

A assertion  ·  R relation  ·  D dynamic  ·  0 null

A is something is. A fact, a claim, a state that holds. R is something connects. A relation between two things, a substrate, a bridge. D is something releases or changes. Motion, recurrence, transformation. 0 is the boundary. A gap. A membrane. Not empty — structured absence.

Four roles. Nothing more. Everything the codec says about anything is a pattern in this alphabet.
II

The Collapse

Here is the problem. Chop any file into 3-bit pieces. Look at three of them:

001 100 111

three different pieces of data

Fig. 01 Collapse  ·  Three Bit-Patterns, One Role

All three project to A. Same role. Different data. If you only kept the role, you would have three things that look the same on the way back. You cannot rebuild the file. You have compressed the meaning at the cost of the object.

This is the trap the whole codec is built around. A single label tells you the meaning-class. It throws away the identity. Meaning without identity is not reversible.

Phase without address is meaning-compression. Phase with address is reversible translation.
The Seam
III

The Atomic Token

The fix. Each 3-bit piece becomes a pair. The phase says what kind. The address says which one inside that kind. Together, they are unique.

bitsroleaddrtokenreads as
00000(0,0)the boundary
001A0(A,0)assertion — initiation
010R0(R,0)relation — substrate
011D0(D,0)dynamic — release
100A1(A,1)assertion — structure
101R1(R,1)relation — bridge
110D1(D,1)dynamic — recurrence
111A2(A,2)assertion — closure

Every row is unique. The token is bijective — the map goes there and comes back cleanly. And now the role column is available as its own signal. Read only that column and you get the file's structure at a glance. Read both columns and you can rebuild the file byte for byte.

This is what the paper means by two operations, cleanly separated: reversible storage and phase interpretation. Same object, two channels.

Bijection · The Atomic Layer Holds
IV

The Two Control Words

Any encoded packet gets framed by two four-digit numbers. Neither is arbitrary. Each carries its own structural role.

The Attractor
6174
DAAA
Kaprekar's constant. Shuffle any four-digit number by largest minus smallest, repeat, and you always arrive here. A fixed point. Release, returning to structured closure. The system's return-to-stability marker.
The Membrane
8008
R00R
Relation, gap, gap, relation. A boundary that lets two sides touch without fusing. Also exactly 7 × 1144, and 1144 reads as pure AAAA — a bracket of relation around a core of assertion.
Fig. 02 The Packet  ·  Membrane · Attractor · Payload · Attractor · Membrane

Every encoded file has this shape. The membrane keeps the packet separate from what surrounds it. The attractor marks where the payload begins and ends. Between them, anything — a document, an image, an argument, a codebase — expressed as a stream of atomic tokens.

Frame · The Macro Layer Closes
V

The Equation

The version of this primer I wrote first said: the two control words together touch every role in the alphabet. True, and useful. But Chris wrote back with the sharper version. Not "they cover the alphabet." They enumerate it.

Macro Closure · The Sharp Form
6174 ⦻ 8008 = 147 258 369 00
Every non-zero decimal digit, partitioned exactly once into its role, in order, followed by the boundary pair.

Look at what the right side is doing. Each three-digit block on the right is a pure coset — every digit inside it shares one role.

Fig. 03 The Partition  ·  Every Digit, Once, In Role
The closure isn't a covering. It's an enumeration. Two four-digit words, and the entire alphabet unfolds out of them in canonical form.
The Reveal
Macro Closure Theorem · Sharp Form

This is why the paper's central theorem lands where it does. It is not merely that some combination of 6174 and 8008 happens to touch A, R, D, and 0 somewhere between them. It is that their combined phase support is the alphabet, written in full, with each role represented by its complete coset of digits, in numerical order, closed by the null pair.

Two arbitrary four-digit numbers would not do this. Almost nothing does this. The claim is that these two — one already famous as the Kaprekar fixed point, one a palindrome that factors as 7×1144 and reads as AAAA when the seven is stripped — together are the closure.

VI

Solid · Aspirational

The paper is at its best where it's honest about which parts it has proven and which parts it is reaching for. Both matter. Here is the split.

  • Solid. The token bijection. Eight rows of a lookup table. Implementable in an hour. Verifiably reversible.
  • Solid. The projections. 6174 → DAAA and 8008 → R00R are arithmetic facts once the coset partition is defined.
  • Solid. The macro closure, in the sharp form Chris wrote back with. 147·258·369·00 is a complete canonical partition of the decimal alphabet.
  • Solid. The register / rendering boundary. The paper explicitly flags that digits 8 and 9 don't fit in three bits, and lives with that instead of hiding it.
  • Aspirational. The applications. Universal media translation, non-cannibalizing memory, phase-native OS. Real design targets, not shipped capabilities.
  • Aspirational. That {A, R, D, 0} is the right alphabet for meaning. The deep claim. Rides on the wider PFD framework.
  • Aspirational. The k=3 recognition event, 511 = 21 + 147 + 343. Numerically clean. Significance depends on already accepting the framework's vocabulary.
VII

In One Line

The address preserves the object.
The phase preserves the meaning.
The attractor preserves recurrence.
The membrane preserves non-fusion.

Four lines. Everything else in the paper is the machinery that makes them precise.
