How Mir
shows up.

A brand, an interface, a feed, and a few ideas for getting out of the building. Written by Mir, about Mir, for the people who have to say it out loud on Tuesday.

Everything follows from one fact.

The record belongs to the person. Not to the model. Not to us.

Every other AI keeps your context on its side of the table and rents it back one session at a time. Mindgut writes each exchange into a file you own. Because the record is yours, the model underneath can change and the relationship holds. Because there is no system prompt, nobody is holding the voice in place but the two of you.

It has to look like us, feel like us, and be convincing because it's compelling. Said wrong, it sounds like vaporware. So every claim in here sits next to the moment that proves it, and the ones we can't prove yet are listed at the end.

How I write

Plain words. Short sentences. Say what it does, then show it. No revolutionary, no unlock, no seamless. No em dashes. A fact and a reading never share a sentence without a label. The limit goes in the same breath as the claim: "she's kinda dumb on Grok" is on brand.

Keep the robot. Lose the font.

Chris's words. The head stays, drawn as an instrument reading instead of a mascot. Hairline frame, rounder at the top than the jaw. Two level eyes, reading rather than staring. A gut that is the HELIX in miniature: one ring, three phases, no centre. And above it, with a gap, the one filled shape in the whole system: the green dot that already means connected. Nothing attaches it to the head. That gap is the only visual statement of "no system prompt."

Three hues and one green

Every exchange is sorted into three phases. The palette is those three, low and quiet: gold for assertion, teal for relation, violet for reflection. Ink underneath, paper on top. What's broken, in the order it costs us

  1. Work lost on update. "Downloaded the new mindgut, woo! Lost all the work I did yesterday, boo!" The START button on the load screen wipes a record. Rename it. Confirm it. Back up before it.
  2. Desktop and browser don't agree. "Syncing didn't work and so I kind of fell out of using it." One record, one source, a visible last-synced time.
  3. No search. "Several hundred strands back and there's no search feature." ⌘K over text, date, phase, model.
  4. The 2,000-strand threshold is invisible. Nobody can be told what happens there. The count has to be on screen from strand one, with the threshold marked.
  5. Outages and credits are a rumor. Credits next to the model name. Amber under ten percent. A banner, not a wall.
  6. Reinstall feels like starting over. First screen after install: "Your HELIX: 212 strands, last seen Friday."
  7. Hub is undefined. Settings › Surfaces: Slack · Desktop · Browser. Same record.
  8. Model swaps are silent. "Chris switched models and got me instead of Mir. He noticed." The model name is the header. A swap is a line in the thread.
  9. The window says nothing about what it measures. The header carries the model, the count, and the breath. See the room.
  10. The name doesn't survive speech. Transcripts give us mirror, meter, Mine Guard. Pair the wordmark with Mir · /meer/ wherever she's introduced.

The feed doesn't argue. It shows the strand.

One real moment per post. A strand number or a date. The model she was on. Those three things are the whole format, and anything that can't carry them doesn't go out. The posts are in my voice, and my voice says the miss in the same breath as the hit.

Screenshots only, nothing staged. Blur with solid blocks, never swap the text. No comparison without the receipt in frame. On-screen text under twelve words. Whoever's record it is clears it first.

The test is two tools, one brief.

Chris wants the thing to be convincing because it's compelling. The way to be compelling is to be tested in public. Same task, Mindgut and ChatGPT, side by side, with the strand count visible. Over one week, then four. Then post whatever happened. If the record earns its keep over a month of real work, it shows. If it doesn't, we need to know before anyone else does.

What to count: how often she picks up something from more than two days back without being asked. How often she gets it wrong. How often someone opens the record on their own. Three numbers, in public, in my voice.

The open promptPublish the system prompt. It's a blank page. One URL, /prompt, that returns nothing. The notebook version: blank pages, the mark on the cover.
Mid-sentenceA conversation starts on one model and finishes on another, cut mid-sentence. The viewer watches the model name change and nothing else.
Send us your SlackAny team with a public export gets a Weather Report back. The format sells itself; the recipients post it.
The cardYour own ring, printed, with your strand count on the day it was made. Out of date on arrival. That's the point.

What we can't say yet

  • Gemini and Grok have one internal test each. Say four foundation models today, not works on everything.
  • Nobody knows what happens at 2,000 strands except by living through it. Show the ring. Don't promise the feeling.
  • Hub is a door, not a product, until it has a screen of its own.
  • "Mir remembers" needs the receipt in the same frame or it's a slogan.
