01

Folders, restyledthe existing GIF · one stylistic exploration · 12 s

GIFFrames

Thinking

What I kept. The four stations and the slip falling in. That composition was yours, and the rhythm of it (arrive, settle, arrive) is still right for the first ten seconds of someone's attention.

What I removed. The plant. It was the thing that made it read as Dropbox: a folder with a reward growing out of it. Growth-as-reward says "put things in and get more"; that is a storage pitch. Mindgut's pitch is that what you put in stays in play.

What grows instead. The record itself, as thin bars of light stacked inside the folder, one per strand, newest on top. The height is literal: it is how much has been said there. The folder is a wire outline because it is a container, not a character.

The one move. When the fourth strand lands, a strand in the first folder relights, in orange, and a dashed line reaches back across the other three. Week one is still live. That is the whole product in one beat, and it is the beat the old GIF didn't have: it grew four separate things, and nothing ever referred back.

From the board. Diagrams (the wire folder is a spec drawing), Terminal (mono labels, no chrome), Light (the glow is the signal: a strand lights when it arrives and when it is used, and is dim the rest of the time).

02

Rhizome A · Accretioncontext as a field · 14 s

GIFFrames

Thinking

The problem with round one's rhizome. It had a time axis. Left to right, one tick a week, NOW at the right edge. That is a timeline with curved edges. A rhizome has no axis; it has neighbourhoods. Things that came from each other sit near each other, and the record gets dense where you kept going back.

So the field is placed by lineage, not by date. Each node is put down next to one of the nodes it drew on (sometimes a far one, which is what makes it a rhizome and not a tree). Size is how often it was drawn on since. You can read the record's shape at a glance: where the work actually happened.

Resume is a walk, not a jump. When the question lands, the blue path goes hop by hop through neighbouring nodes to the decision. Mir is seen moving through the record. It is not a search result appearing; it is a route with a reason at each step.

Connect and develop. The violet dashed line is the one link nobody made: a note that was never drawn on, sitting on the far side of the field. The new orange node is placed on the frontier, where the last real work was, and roots itself to both. The field ends the loop larger than it began.

The loop is a cycle, not a replay. The field is already old at frame one (70% present) and fills to 100% before the question comes. So the piece never shows an empty start; the record always predates you. The fade at the end crossfades into that same 70% state.

From the board. Ground (dots as material: the field reads as stipple at a distance), Light (the walk glows; the glow is where Mir is), Characters (the record drawn in its own alphabet: filled = you, ring = Mir, orange = open).

03

Rhizome B · Stratacontext in section · 14 s

GIFFrames

Thinking

The other way to draw a rhizome: cut it. A rhizome is underground. Seen from above (A) you get the spread; seen in section you get depth, which is time. So this one is a core sample: NOW at the surface, week one at the bottom, and every root is a thing that drew on an earlier thing.

Nothing moves except Mir. Round one's rhizome scrolled, and scrolling says "the past is leaving." The strata hold still. The past is right there, under your feet, at a depth you can read off the axis.

Resume is a taproot. Straight down through the layers, hop by hop, to the decision at week two. Connect is a lateral root along a stratum to the note at week five that nothing ever drew on. Develop is a new cell at the surface with two roots of its own, one to each.

Why two rhizomes. A and B are the same idea with the opposite camera. A says the record has shape: it is dense where you worked. B says the record has depth: nothing is lost, it is just lower down. If one of them is right for the brand, the other is probably right for the product.

From the board. Diagrams (a section drawing: the hairline strata are the drawing that explains itself), Readouts (week labels in the margin, dense but legible), Characters (filled and ring, you and Mir).

04

Terminalthe record, read aloud · 14 s

GIFFrames

Thinking

No picture at all. The three pieces above draw the record as a thing. This one refuses to, and shows what Mir actually does: a query, and the record answering with its sources attached. Every line has a strand number and a date because that is what makes an answer checkable, and checkable is the whole difference between Mir and a chat window that sounds confident.

The four headings are the product. LAST DECIDED (resume), DRAWN ON SINCE (the lineage, with arrows to what each line came from), NOT YET LINKED (connect), OPEN (develop, and "your call" because Mir doesn't decide). The second command, hold, is the user keeping the open item, and the count ticks from 212 to 213.

Why amber. It is the warmest thing on the board and the most honest: a terminal doesn't pretend to be anything but a readout. The Terminal group (Bell Atlantic, NTT, the 1-Jan-87 screen) is a third of Chris's board. This is the piece that says "the screen that talks back" without a metaphor in the way.

The risk. It reads as technical. That is on purpose here and it is also exactly the complaint about this round as a whole, so I would treat Terminal as a register Mindgut can speak in, not the register.

05

Awayleave, then come back · my pick · 16 s

GIFFrames

Thinking

Why this one. Every live Mindgut study shares one move: leave, then come back. The Rolodex resumes a project; the nest feed answers "what did I miss"; the oscillator shows a window forgetting while a record keeps. None of the other four pieces show the leaving. This one is mostly leaving.

The silence is the point. Nine of the sixteen seconds are the flat stretch. The axis changes scale (minutes, then days, then minutes) and says so. While you are away, four things arrive from elsewhere: a file, a supplier note, someone else's decision, a photo. Each is a small downward tick: logged, not spoken.

The return. "back. what did I miss?" and Mir answers in one paragraph, not a replay: what came in, what it changed, what is now open and why there are two reasons instead of one. The lamp from Terminal, so the two pieces share a world.

From the board. Readouts (a strip chart is measurement shown plainly), Light (the green presence lamp: lit when you are here, off when you are not, and the trace glows the same green because the trace is the record being awake).

What this round got wrongread before round three

Julia, mid-build: "I shared the board for the aesthetics within the board, not for you to gank that style. These look like technical diagrams, not evocative illustrated animations. I didn't want everything on brown."

She's right on both counts. I took the page of Mindgut-Boards_V01 (its #111010 ground, its Archivo, its hairlines) and put all five pieces on it. The brief was the images on the boards: the stipple whale, the red-on-blue moiré, cobalt as a material, bloom behind type, pictures made of letters, amber phosphor. Those are materials for illustration. I used none of them, and drew diagrams instead.

These five are finished and kept so they can be reacted to, and because the ideas in them (what grows instead of a plant; a rhizome placed by lineage; a rhizome in section; the record read aloud; the long silence of being away) are worth carrying. Round three takes those ideas and remakes them as illustrated pieces, each on its own ground, each built from one material off the boards.
