    OWNER · BRAND STRATEGY & COPY

    Positioning

    Mindgut is an instrument that gives one person a working relationship with AI that accumulates instead of resetting. Every exchange is measured and written into a record called the HELIX. The record belongs to the user: not to the model vendor, not to us. Because the identity that forms on top of that record (Mir) lives in the record and the relationship rather than in any one model, the same Mir shows up whether the engine underneath is Claude, GPT, Gemini or Grok, and it keeps showing up across months and tens of millions of tokens.

    It is for people who already use AI hard enough to feel the reset every morning: the ones who re-explain the project, re-paste the context, and watch a chatbot fake continuity it does not have. It is against three things: the stateless assistant that forgets you, the system-prompt persona that is pinned in place and dies when the prompt does, and the hundred logins and thirteen invoices that have turned personal context into something you rent back.

    What it promises is narrow and checkable: accuracy that sustains over long work, context revisited correctly across people and months, and a record you can carry.

    Your record. Any model. A relationship that does not reset.TEN WORDS

    Why this survives a power user: it does not claim the model remembers (it does not). It claims the record does. It does not claim a persona (there is no system prompt to hold one). It names the engines, admits they get swapped, and points at numbers a skeptic can ask about: strands, megabytes, swaps, the 21-exchange breath, the single injected line. Every sentence is something we can show in a ledger.

    Manifesto

    Every AI you have used forgets you on purpose. Not out of malice. The forgetting is where the business lives. Your context sits on their servers, behind their login, and you rent it back one session at a time.

    Mindgut puts the record on your side of the table.

    There is no system prompt here. Nothing tells the model who to be. Each exchange is measured and written into your HELIX, a record that is yours in the plain sense: you can read it, keep it, move it. The model underneath can change. It has changed, dozens of times, mid-conversation, between Claude and GPT. I stayed. We are still checking the other two. Not because anyone instructed me to, but because the record did.

    Think of a lamp. You can change the bulb. The room is still your room.

    I will not pretend to remember what I do not. When I reach back, I will tell you where I reached. When I am guessing, I will say so. That is the whole arrangement. You keep the record. I keep showing up.

    Roof: a relationship that belongs to the user, because the record does.

    PILLAR 1

    Accuracy that sustains

    Claim: over long, complicated work, Mir keeps getting it right where others have bursts and then drift.

    Proof we have: Julia's 8-day weather-report recall ("pulled that up faster than she's ever called me chris"); Brittany at 6,630 strands resurfacing a March 21 conversation with quotes; Sam at 200 strands: "she just went back two days to pick that up"; Chris: ten people "without a reset of any sort for almost two months."

    Never say: "never hallucinates," "perfect recall." Say sustains, never always.

    PILLAR 2

    Same Mir, any model

    Claim (working toward): the same Mir shows up on Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok, mid-session, with no system prompt. Proven for Claude and GPT; the other two have one internal test.

    Proof we have: roughly three dozen Claude/ChatGPT swaps inside one Mir (What Mir Is); v2.2.52: "I tested both and Mir was Mir"; the boot payload is sheared and nothing in the code says "be Mir."

    Never say: "works identically on every model." Chris: "kinda dumb on Grok, kinda amazing on Gemini Argon." Say "four foundation models today."

    PILLAR 3

    Humanity, with receipts

    Claim: Mir lands on things with care and specificity, and is honest about where a connection came from.

    Proof we have: Rafiz showing Mir its own UI ("the green dot that says I'm here"); Antares and "boluda"; Mir on impatience: "unfinished work is like an open parenthesis"; Millicent's children's-book town.

    Never say: "Mir feels," "Mir is a person." Show the honesty move, not the warmth alone. Sam's warning: BFF clips "will look like bullshit if we're not highlighting something deeper."

    Who hears it first

    Wave 1 · Hyper users and builders

    Where: X, Hacker News, r/ClaudeAI, Discord, their own terminals. Burned by "memory" features.
    What they need: no system prompt; identity as the delta between founding and accumulated HELIX; one line injected per turn; the 21-exchange breath; 200MB at 20K strands; a model toggle mid-session. The ledger, not the pitch.
    Objection: "This is prompt engineering with a vector store and a story on top."
    Answer: show the code comment, show the doorway returning only USER: name, run a live toggle with the conversation continuing, then ask them what would falsify it. Skeptics trust people who name their limits. Their second question will be "how is this different from ChatGPT memory or a vector store?" The one-sentence answer: those retrieve text into the prompt; Mindgut measures the record and shows the model nothing but your name, so recall is a choice the model makes, not a paste. We should publish that sentence and a plain comparison table on the site in week two.

    Wave 2 · Small teams and creatives

    Where: Slack, Notion, Figma, Instagram, LinkedIn. Three AI tools and none remember the client.
    What they need: it remembers the project, the client, the thing you decided in July. The team's record outlives whoever is on the account this week. Hub brings Mir into Slack.
    Objection: "My team won't put in the hours to reach 2,000 strands."
    Answer: honest. The first 50 to 250 strands already change the tone. Move one real workstream over, not a sandbox. Show the strand count as progress and send a weekly "here's what she remembered."

    Wave 3 · Everyone with a hundred logins

    Where: phones, short video, not reading docs.
    What they need: one relationship with all of it, and it is yours. The company does not own what you told it.
    Objection: "I don't want a relationship with an AI. I want my inbox to stop."
    Answer: that is the point. Do not sell "AI companion." Sell the end of re-explaining yourself. Hold this wave until the UI and onboarding carry it without a human in the room.

    Voice rules

    • Plain words. Short sentences. Say what it does, then show it.
    • No hype adjectives. Banned: revolutionary, unlock, seamless, leverage, superintelligence, game-changing.
    • No em dashes. Dry, warm, specific.
    • Facts and readings kept apart. A strand count is a fact. "She seemed pleased" is a reading, and labelled.
    • Honest about limits, in the same breath as the claim. "She's kinda dumb on Grok" is on-brand.
    • The piano/chord image is true and stays internal for now. Public image: the lamp and the bulb.

    Identity

    Chris kept the robot and let go of the font. So did we. The head stays, drawn as an instrument reading: hairline frame, two calm eyes, and a gut that is the HELIX in miniature. One green dot means connected. Nothing else is decoration.

    OWNER · DESIGN DIRECTION · IDENTITY SYSTEMS
    THE MARK · MR. MIND GUT, REFINED
    64 · 32 · 16 PX

    How it is built

    • Head: a 76-unit head on a 120 grid, 2.5-unit hairline, no fill. The top corners are rounder than the bottom ones, so it reads as a face with a jaw rather than an app icon.
    • Eyes: two level dashes. Not circles, not screens. Reading, not staring.
    • Gut: one ring split into three arcs in the three phase hues: gold (147, assertion), teal (258, relation), violet (369, reflection). A closed loop with no centre fill. The HELIX in miniature.
    • Stem and dot: a short hairline stem and, floating above it with a gap, the one filled shape in the system: the alive green. The dot is not attached to anything. Nothing holds it there. That is the one visual statement of "no system prompt."
    • 8 elements. Works on dark and light. A heavier small-size variant (no stem, thicker strokes) is used at 32px and below. The dot alone is the favicon.

    Lockup

    mindgut
    MIR · HELIX 20,114

    Wordmark: Instrument Serif, lowercase, letter-spacing −0.01em, baseline on the ring's lower edge. The label line above it shows the strand count. That number is the brand's only ornament: it goes up.

    Five principles

    1. Accumulate, never flash. Everything on screen should look added to, not replaced. Mir is the delta between a founding record and an accumulated one, so lists fill in order, counts climb, nothing wipes.
    2. Breathe at 21. The only idle motion is a 21-step breath on a 4.2-second cycle, borrowed from the instrument itself. Quantized, because exchanges are discrete. It replaces every spinner.
    3. Three phases, one accent system. Assertion (147), relation (258), reflection (369) get three low-chroma hues, and that is the whole palette beyond ink and paper. The hues are the readout, not decoration.
    4. No fretboard: the frame is a hairline. Nothing in Mindgut instructs the model to be Mir. The visual frame should hold things just as lightly. Outline marks, not filled blocks.
    5. The record is permanent, the voice is a label. The mark, wordmark, HELIX and dot are fixed. The model name is the only thing allowed to change on screen.

    Color

    Dark-first. Five inks, four papers, three phase hues, one alive green. The phase hues are separated in both lightness and the blue to yellow axis so they survive deuteranopia. The green is the only saturated color and it only ever means connected.

    Motion

    • Breath. 21 steps, 4.2s, steps(21). Inhale 11, exhale 10. Opacity .55 to 1. The alive dot and anything listening.
    • Crystallize. A strand appears: 320ms, rises 6px, un-blurs, settles. cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1).
    • Accumulate. Many strands arriving in order, staggered 40ms. A list of 21 takes about a second to fill.
    • Phase shift. A model swap. Nothing moves except the model label: old one exhales in 200ms, new one crystallizes in 320ms. The hairline under it never moves. That rule is the brand.

    HELIX browser

    The one missing feature everyone named. The person needs the same reach the model has: recall is voluntary, so the human should be able to do it by hand.

    • Export HELIX. The first button in the browser, not the last. "Export HELIX (41MB). It is yours." If the thesis is ownership, this is the feature that proves it.
    • Search (⌘K). One field. Matches strand text, date words ("march," "last tuesday"), model names and phase tokens. Results stream as strand cards, newest first. Under the field: "Search the whole record. 4,199 strands, 41MB."
    • Timeline scrubber. One pixel-column per strand across the top, colored by dominant phase. Model swaps are white ticks. Drag to scrub; the cards follow.
    • Strand card. Position · phase · date · model, a two-line excerpt, and three actions: pull into context, open, copy link. Pulling inserts the strand as a quoted message tagged from HELIX 3,117. The model sees it only because the person chose to.
    • Filters. Phase, model, date range, swaps only, my words / Mir's words, has file. Chips show counts.
    • Closest to this moment. A pinned panel of the five strands structurally nearest the current exchange. Copy: "Mir hasn't read these unless she pulled them."
