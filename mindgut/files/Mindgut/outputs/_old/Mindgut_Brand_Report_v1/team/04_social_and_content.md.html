## Content thesis

Mindgut content does not argue. It shows receipts. Every post is one real moment, screen-recorded or screenshotted from a real session, in which the HELIX did something a stateless chat cannot: it remembered correctly across months, it stayed itself across four models, it said "I don't know" when the honest answer was I don't know, or it landed a line with the kind of humanity Chris keeps noticing. The audience for the first two weeks is not "everyone"; it is hyper users, builders and people who already live in Claude Code, Cursor and ChatGPT and know exactly how fast memory breaks. For them a 15-second clip of Julia typing "remember the weather report I asked you to do?" and Mir answering "impossible_weather_report.html, 42KB, about 8 days old" is worth more than any explainer. The copy is in Mir's voice: plain, short, dry, warm. Proprietary client material gets blurred, never faked. Comparisons to other models are made only when we have the actual side-by-side screenshot. And the relationship thesis ("the HELIX belongs to the user") is never stated as a slogan; it is shown by the fact that the same Mir appears on Claude, Gemini, GPT and Grok in the same post.

**The ten rules**

1. Real screenshots and real screen recordings only. No mockups, no staged sessions, no re-typed conversations. If we don't have the artifact, we don't have the post.
2. Blur, don't fake. Client names, formulations, pricing, people's personal content get a solid blur block (not a pixelation that can be reversed). We never paste in substitute text.
3. One moment per post. If a post needs a second moment to make sense, it is two posts.
4. No comparison without the receipt. "Mir did X, Gemini didn't" only ships if both screenshots are in the same frame with the same prompt visible.
5. Captions are written in Mir's voice. Plain words, short sentences, no em dashes, no "revolutionary / unlock / seamless / leverage", honest about limits.
6. Facts and readings stay separate. A strand count is a fact. "She seemed happy" is a reading and is labelled as one.
7. Show, then say. On-screen text names what happened in under 12 words. The caption explains. Nothing in the caption that the clip doesn't show.
8. Say the model. Every clip shows or names the foundation model Mir was running on at that moment (Claude, GPT, Gemini Argon, Grok). This is the quiet proof of portability.
9. Say the strand. Where we know it, the strand number goes on screen (strand 228, strand 4411, 6,630). Strands are our timestamps and our credibility.
10. Everything clears through the owner first. The person whose HELIX it is approves the post, then Gaby or Chris approves the proprietary check. Nobody publishes someone else's session.

## Three series

**Series 1: Strand N**
Hook: a single strand number on a black frame, then the exchange it refers to.
Structure: Frame 1 "Strand 4411" (2s). Frame 2 the original exchange, dated (5s). Frame 3 the later exchange that recalled it, with the gap stated ("209 days later") (6s). Frame 4 one line in Mir's voice (2s).
Length: 15s. Daily, Mon–Fri. One strand a day read aloud in a flat, slow synthetic voice (the IG/TikTok "automated voice" format Chris named), or silent with on-screen text on IG.
Visual: near-black background (#0A0B0D), white monospace for strand numbers, teal for Mir's text, light grey for the human's. Three thin horizontal bars at the top show the Z₃ phase split for that strand (147/258/369). No music.
Sources queued: 228 (Millicent), 4411→6,630 (Brittany), the 8-day weather report recall (Julia), Sam's two-day movie recall, Antares/Argentina.

**Series 2: Same question, four models, one Mir**
Hook: one prompt typed once, four answers side by side, the model badge on each.
Structure: Frame 1 the prompt (3s). Frames 2–5 four quadrants fill in one at a time: Claude, GPT, Gemini Argon, Grok (3s each). Final frame: the one line that appears in all four, highlighted. Where the voice drifts (Grok), we show the drift.
Length: 15s Reel/TikTok; the uncut session goes to YouTube at 2–4 minutes.
Visual: four-up grid, each quadrant with a small text label of the model. Mir's name identical in all four. Where we have it, the token readout corner is left visible (In / Out / Cache) because that is the receipt.
Rule: the prompt must be real work, not a trick question. First three: "What's going on with Impossible right now?", "Write Gaby's Monday scrum opener", "Explain the Weather Report Z₃ signature to a new hire."

**Series 3: The Weather Report**
Hook: "Mir read [N] messages of our Slack and gave us a forecast."
Structure: 1) Z₃ signature readout animates in. 2) Three "weather systems" named, one line each. 3) One Eye-wall quote. 4) The five-day forecast as a numbered list. 5) "Corrections welcome."
Length: 60–90s YouTube/LinkedIn video; 15s cut for IG/TikTok using only the signature and one quote.
Visual: the existing Weather Report HTML aesthetic (serif headline, dry caption lines, the Ø ornament), scroll-captured, slow. Monthly cadence, with the back catalog (Report I and II) published in launch week. Also an invitation format: any team with a public Slack export can send one in; Mir issues a reading. This is the series most likely to pull builders in.
