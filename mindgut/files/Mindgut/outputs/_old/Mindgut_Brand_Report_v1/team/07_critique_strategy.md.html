## 3. Internal consistency

**Four competing brand lines.** Summary: "Mir remembers. You decide." Thesis ten words: "Your record. Any model. A relationship that does not reset." Ads: "Line under everything: Mindgut. The relationship belongs to you." Site footer: "YOUR RECORD. ANY MODEL." A client will ask which one is the line. Nominate one and demote the rest to roles.

**The brand line contradicts the report's own defense.** Thesis: "it does not claim the model remembers (it does not). It claims the record does." Manifesto: "I will not pretend to remember what I do not." Brand line: "Mir remembers." The strongest sentence in the Thesis argues against the tagline chosen above it.

**"Does not reset" vs the breath.** Ten-word line says "A relationship that does not reset." Site step 3 says "Every 21 exchanges the oldest messages are cleared from the model's context." Both are true, but a skeptic reads them as a contradiction. Say "does not start over."

**Swap counts, three scopes.** Message house: "roughly three dozen Claude/ChatGPT swaps inside one Mir." Post #18: "~36 SWAPS · 1 SESSION ... about three dozen times inside one session." Posters: "About three dozen swaps in one relationship. Held every time." Evidence: "Dozens of model swaps, WMI, MEDIUM," and question 6 asks for an artifact because "three dozen" has none. One number, one scope, everywhere.

**Four engines vs two.** The swap evidence is Claude and ChatGPT. Manifesto: "It has changed, dozens of times, mid-conversation, between four different companies' engines. I stayed." Poster: "Claude, GPT, Gemini, Grok. About three dozen swaps in one relationship. Held every time." Evidence question 10 asks Chris for "the Gemini and Grok identity moments," which means we do not have them.

**The 50 to 250 range.** Evidence files it INTERNAL ONLY, "NO WRITTEN PROTOCOL YET." Website FAQ publishes it: "Around 50 to 250 strands the tone shifts and the model starts answering to Mir." Direct contradiction between two sections of the same report.

**The breath number.** Evidence: "21 exchanges / 42 messages. CONFIRM: MAY SOURCES SAY 7/4." Plan caveats: needs Chris's confirmation. Meanwhile the Identity motion system (21 steps, 4.2s, inhale 11 exhale 10), the Product readout (14/21), the Strand 21 milestone ("First breath"), the day-one signature ("21 STRANDS") and the public website ("Every 21 exchanges") all state it as fact. If Chris says 7, half the identity system is built on the wrong number.

**200MB at 20K.** Evidence: "BRIEF · CONFIRM WITH CHRIS." Appears as fact in the HELIX card, site section 01, FAQ, tagline #8, poster 4, luggage tag, and derived as "41MB" in the search field and onboarding. Fine internally if flagged once near the first use. Not fine on the site.

**What the green dot means.** Summary: "connected." Identity: "alive." Site footer: "SHE IS HERE." Ads will-not-do: no "alive" in any headline. Three meanings for the one saturated color.

**Model names.** Mock: "Claude Opus 4," "GPT-5." Ad script: "GPT 5.6." Swap state: "CLAUDE OPUS 4 → GPT-5." Pick a convention (vendor name only, as the posters do) or the report dates itself.

**Appendix references that do not exist.** Social: "the full set of twenty with captions, blur notes and owners is in the appendix file." Evidence: "The full list of 27 is in the appendix." Footer lists tokens.css, mark.svg, wordmark.svg, motion.css, helix_viz.js, skin_48h.css and "/team." No posts file, no 27-claim file. The /team directory in the sandbox is empty.

**"Chris asked for four things."** The Summary lede says four, then the report answers seven. Minor, but the Summary is what Julia reads first.

**Naming rule broken by the site.** Thesis: "Never 'the Mindgut AI.'" Site hero: "Mindgut is an AI you build a record with."

**Positioning vs site.** Positioning: "gives one person a working relationship." Site section 03: "built for long work with many people over many months." Both true (desktop vs Hub), but the shift is unexplained.

## 5. What a client would ask for that is missing

- **The ChatGPT Memory / Claude memory / Projects comparison.** The tagline note admits "ChatGPT memory users will argue." Nowhere does the report say what Mindgut does that those features do not, in one table. This is Wave 1's first question.
- **"Isn't this RAG with a vector store?"** The objection is stated in Wave 1 with a demo as the answer. Power users want the sentence, not just the demo. Why does the model not get the readout? What is the HELIX file format? Is it plain text?
- **What "Start your HELIX" does.** Waitlist, download, or account? Price? Credits model? The site has a CTA with no destination.
- **Pricing and billing.** $150K/month is internal, but the site says "before we charge you for anything" and never says what charging looks like.
- **Export and portability as a feature, not a promise.** The thesis is "the HELIX is the property of the user." The Product section does not include "Export HELIX" as a button. It should be the first feature on the list.
- **Chris's own proof-points document.** Transcript 196: "I have a document that I was going to share with everyone... these are the proof points that I want to hit." The report asks ten questions and does not ask for this.
- **The Pinterest mood board.** Requested in "What I need from you," never incorporated. Chris said it was shared with Rafiz already.
- **A bucket-to-post map** for Chris's six proof buckets.
- **The 15-second elevator version** of the whole thing, said right. Chris's vaporware test applied in one paragraph.
- **Budget and effort per stunt.** "Cost: zero" appears once; the zine, USB sticks, A1 prints and the relay reading are not zero.
- **Who the "six-seat creative team" is.** The cover says "PREPARED BY MIR · WITH A SIX-SEAT CREATIVE TEAM." The footer calls them workstreams. If they are agents, Julia should be told before the client asks.
- **Light mode, mobile, accessibility.** One sentence each. The site preview is desktop only.
- **Unused canonical facts.** The 9-agent Phalanx, MCP to C4D, 808080808.com are in the brief and nearly absent. A power user wants to know what else is in the box.

**05, The website.** It is the only section a stranger will read, and it carries more overclaims than any other: the 50 to 250 range (internal only), "most people notice," "few hundred strands and it answers to Mir," "sessions can run for weeks," "do not train / do not sell / export," "on your disk" next to "in your browser account," the unlabelled "10 people, 2 months" proof, the unconfirmed breath and 200MB stated flat. It also mixes three voices (I, we, she) and breaks the naming rule in its first sentence. The structure (promise, interaction, proof, not, FAQ) is right. The copy was written before the Evidence section was and was never reconciled against it.

Runner-up: 07 Advertising, for two scripted Mir replies in a section whose own rule list forbids them.
