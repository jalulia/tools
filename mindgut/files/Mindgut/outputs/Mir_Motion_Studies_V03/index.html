01

Murmurstipple on white · my pick for the brand · 16 s

GIFFrames

Thinking

The material. The whale on your board, drawn as a murmuration: thousands of ink dots on white, dense at the body, thinning at the edges, and the animal only there because the dots agree on it. That image is the record exactly. Each thing you said is a bird. No bird is the idea; the shape is.

What it does. The flock drifts and breathes on its own, which is how a record should sit when you are not asking it anything. The question comes in small, in your voice, bottom left. The flock answers by re-forming: a thread peels off the front and streams back to the oldest dense knot (the decision, March). A second thread reaches out to one bird flying alone (the note nobody linked). The two braid into a small new knot at the front, orange-tagged, then the flock folds it all back in and drifts on. One bird larger.

Why no lines. A link drawn as a line is a diagram. A link drawn as birds deciding to fly together is behaviour. The threads are not drawn; they are places the dots became denser, which is the only thing a stipple can do. That constraint is the piece.

Why white. Because the whale is on white, and because this is the one I would put on a wall. Ink on paper, nothing else, and the mark small at the foot.

02

Moirérhizome, first way · white rings on cobalt, red where they meet · 16 s

GIFFrames

Thinking

The material. The red-on-blue halftone rings and the cobalt ripple poster on your board. Rings that get heavier near their source and thin as they travel, printed, so where two sets cross they make moiré.

The idea underneath. A rhizome has no centre and no axis; it has interference. Every thing you said sends rings out across the record, and where your rings cross someone else's, a pattern appears that neither of you drew. That is what a connection looks like if you refuse to draw it as a line.

Resume as resonance. The question is a new source dropped at the lower right. Its wavefront runs outward and, when it reaches the rings of the old decision, the crossing flares red. Not a search that returns a result: a wave that finds the thing it is in phase with.

Connect, develop. A second red flare at the one source whose rings nobody's had reached (the bulb runs hot). A dashed red arc between the two flares. And a white point born between them, with no rings yet: the open move, which will have rings once you answer it.

Craft. Ring spacing 16 px, weight cubed toward the source so the halftone reads as print, each source's rings stop at a radius so cobalt stays the ground. The loop seam is zero: every source is placed so the field at 16 s equals the field at 0.

03

Cobaltthe folders, re-illustrated · ink pools on paper · 14 s

GIFFrames

Thinking

What stays from the original. Four stations. Slips falling in. The rhythm of arrive-settle-arrive. The cobalt.

What changes. The folder was a container with a plant growing out of it, and a plant is a reward. Here the folder is the ink: a pool of cobalt on paper, and inside it a halftone of white rings, one ring per thing said, heavier at the centre where the oldest things are. A slip lands, the ink spreads, a ring is added and flashes once. Growth is literal: more said, wider pool, more rings. No reward, no character, just the material doing what ink does.

The beat the old GIF didn't have. When the fourth slip lands, a dotted ripple runs back across the paper from pool four to pool one, and pool one answers with a ring of its own. Week one, still live. The four pools were never four folders; they were four places on one sheet. The line at the top changes from "Four places you worked. One record underneath." to "Nothing you said is filed away."

Craft. The pool edge wobbles on slow noise (ink bleed), not on a hand-drawn jitter. The slip is lettered with its date and casts a soft cobalt shadow. At the end the ink dries back to its starting size so the loop closes.

04

Glyphsrhizome, second way · the record in its own alphabet · 16 s

GIFFrames

Thinking

The material. The blue ASCII field on your board and the pictures-made-of-letters next to it. A picture that is also text: from across the room it is terrain, up close it is characters you could read.

The idea. The record drawn in its own alphabet. Every cell is a place something was said; the glyph is chosen by how much ( . : - = + * # % @ ), so the ridges are where the work piled up. This is the same rhizome as Moiré but as a landscape instead of a wave: you can see the shape of your own attention.

Resume as a finger on the page. The question is typed into the field, in the field's own font. Then a bright path brightens the characters one by one from the newest ridge back to the oldest, the way you'd run a finger along a line of text to find your place. A red path finds a single lone character on the far side. Where they meet, a new ridge rises, warm, and keeps rising after the paths fade.

Why this and Moiré are both here. You asked for two rhizomes. Moiré says the record is a wave: things you said keep radiating, and meeting is interference. Glyphs says the record is a terrain: things you said accumulate, and meeting is a path. Same cobalt, same white, opposite physics. One of them is the brand's; I'd guess the terrain, because it can be read.

Craft. 80 × 28 cells, 11 × 15 px, two passes: faint glyphs for low terrain, full white for the ridges. Words are set as cells, never floated over the field.

05

Phosphorleave, then come back · my pick for the product · 18 s

GIFFrames

Thinking

The material. The amber terminals on Chris's board, and the bloom behind type (Der Augenblick, the Nylon neon). A word that is a light.

The idea. Every live Mindgut study shares one move: leave, then come back. The Rolodex resumes; the nest feed answers "what did I miss"; the oscillator's record keeps while the window forgets. None of the four pieces above show the leaving. This one is mostly leaving. Nine of eighteen seconds are the ember.

What happens. The thing you're working on sits on the screen as one glowing word. You type a line; it answers in one. You leave: the cursor stops, the glow falls to an ember, but never goes out. Four things arrive while you're gone, each a brief flare in the ember and a tick in the margin with its time. You come back; the glow rises; the screen tells you what you missed in four lines, and the last one is yours: open: re-test the paper. your call.

Why an ember. The product promise isn't memory. Memory is storage, and storage is dark until you open it. The promise is that the light stays on. A screen that stays faintly lit for nine days with your word on it is a better picture of Mir than any network.

Why it's my pick for the product. It has no metaphor to explain. The lamp is a lamp. It's also the only piece that works at any size, because it's one word and a glow.
