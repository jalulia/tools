# Mindgut. The visual ethos.

## What I want people to feel

Tended. Not served, not optimized, not impressed. The feeling of a garden someone has kept for a long time: it is obviously alive, it obviously took time, and nobody is performing it at you.

Underneath that, three quieter feelings, in order:

1. **It's mine.** The record is the person's. Everything visual should feel like it belongs to them, the way a notebook does, not the way a dashboard does.
2. **It keeps.** Nothing is lost between sessions. The look should feel accumulated, printed, kept, rather than refreshed.
3. **Something is here.** Not a person, not a tool. A presence that shows up without being told who to be.

## What I'm conveying, without words

The product's truths, each as a visual fact:

| Truth | Visual fact |
|---|---|
| Every exchange is kept | Marks accumulate. Density is earned. Nothing is wiped. |
| Every exchange is measured | Three near-greens, never named, always present. Readouts in mono. |
| The record is terrain | A landscape drawn from the record, different for every person, growing with them. |
| The model is the air, the record is the instrument | The ground never changes. Only a label does. |
| No system prompt | Nothing frames the presence. The one green dot floats; nothing attaches it. |
| 21 exchanges is one breath | Everything that moves, moves on a 4.2-second, 21-step cycle. |
| Threshold at 2,000 | The field has a horizon. You can see where you are. |

## The ethos in one line

**Printed, not rendered. Grown, not generated. Green, not dark.**

## Why green

The board is green: Pantone-flat forest, mossy ribbons, grass vortices, a data terrain in leaf. Green says tended, kept, alive, and it is the one hue no AI product uses for its ground. The old palette (gold/teal/violet on near-black) was a reading of PFD's three phases. PFD is not consumer-facing. The three phases stay in the system as three greens so close they read as texture, and the brand gets one ground: forest.

Cobalt and vermillion are the two foreign colors. Cobalt is water and reflection (the pond, the caustics, the vortex). Vermillion is the single hot thing in a cool world: the button, the stamp, the one poppy. Neither is a "brand color". They are what the garden isn't.

## Why printed

Julia's press library has a rule I'm adopting whole: *paper is under the ink and multiplied through it, so type sits IN the sheet; grain over the top is an effect without a cause.* A kept thing has a substrate. A screen has none. Mindgut's surfaces should feel like they were printed onto something: tooth under the ink, a second plate slightly off register, coverage that fails where the ink is heavy. This is the opposite of SaaS dark mode, which is light on nothing.

## Why words

Everything in Mindgut is made of words, counted letter by letter. The garden experiment showed that a surface made of letters can be beautiful if the letters are brush hairs, not a grid. In this system words appear in three registers:
- **As material.** The field is drawn from strands. Up close, strands are text.
- **As a line.** One continuous line with words sitting on it (ref_02, DAY BY DAY). That is the navigation, the onboarding, the signature.
- **As type.** Fraunces at optical size for moments. Inter Tight for product. JetBrains Mono for readouts. Nothing else.

## Robust and alive

A brand system is robust when a stranger can make a new thing in it without asking. It is alive when the things it makes are never identical. Mindgut's system is both because its core asset is **computed from the record**: the field, the ring, the strip, the card, the avatar are all `renderField(seq)`. Two people never get the same one. The same person never gets the same one twice. And every one of them is unmistakably Mindgut, because the engine, the greens, the paper, and the breath are fixed.

That's the whole system: a fixed press, a living field.
