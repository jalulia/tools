Counted marks,
placed by hand.

A figure that only exists because many small marks agree on it. That sentence is Colin's, from his CVM × stipple board, and it is the one idea every technique on this page serves. A strand is a dot. The relationship is the shape the dots settle into. Everything below is drawn live from that rule — no raster images, no gradients doing the shading — so another Mir can open this file, read the code, and reuse the parts.

01 · The thesis

Coherence is shown as whether the picture has resolved. The number sits underneath.

Colin's page 2 strip is the whole product in three frames. Here it is as one live plate: drag the slider from clash to in tune. Each dot has a home on a ring and a random offset; the slider is simply how far each dot has travelled home. Nothing is interpolated between images — the same 2,400 dots are drawn every frame, so the middle states are real, not blends.

Loose → settling → lockedNine cream rings, teal and blue blades, a red figure at the centre. Fischinger's frame A, arrived at rather than drawn. studyColin · p.2
What this gives the product. A state that can be read at a glance without a number (how resolved is the picture?) with the number available underneath for anyone who wants it. It is the opposite of a progress bar: it never looks like an error when it's loose, it just looks unsettled. That matters for a tool people confide in.

03 · Two fields

Colour appears where two systems agree.

Pin 028 on Julia's board (checked against the original this morning): concentric arcs from one origin, a regular dot grid as the second field, red concentrating where the two align and thinning to grain at the edges. The ø co. library already has this as TS-01 — ring-screen × moire-product × stochastic-grain. Below is a compact re-implementation with the library's own parameter names, so a Mir with the library can swap this out for the real modules.

Two ring gratings, one stochastic screenBlue coverage = floor + (1−floor)·smoothstep(knee_lo, knee_hi, (gA·gB)^γ). Red core = min(1, gain·e^(−d²/σ²)). Each pixel then throws one random number per ink. That is the whole thing. libraryJ · 028
TS-01 · pitch A 68u · pitch B 60u · beat ≈ 510u · seed 101
Library limits, verbatim. Pitches within ~4u of each other push the beat fringe past the sheet; equal pitches collapse the moiré to one ring set. Below ~30u the rings alias into the screen. Gamma below ~0.8 fills the lattice to a flat tint; above ~2.5 the dots shrink to specks.

For Mindgut this is the honest "two voices" picture: one field is a wave, the other a grid, and the colour is a matter of density where they coincide — not a hue assigned to a party. Try the violet · teal inks to see Julia's palette on the same mechanism.

The honest gap. The product's rail is a flat bar, 8px cells and a number. Colin's is a settling picture. 05 · Six grounds

The figure survives every ground. The identity is the construction, not the colour.

Colin's page 4, rebuilt live: the locked ring figure in six palettes — three of Fischinger's (cobalt, ultramarine, olive), paper, the current app, and plum. Same geometry in every tile; only the four colours change. Julia's violet/teal is added as a seventh so the comparison includes the current direction.

A way to decide (Colin's, kept). Default ground: olive or near-black for a long session. Accent: one of ochre, vermilion or the existing teal. Moments: cobalt or ultramarine for lock and renewal, where loud is earned. Print and light mode: paper. No new shapes. — And the caution he closes with: colour on a mockup lies; pick after the stipple has been built and looked at on a real display. This page is that build; it still isn't the display.
06 · What I'd Be · nine answers, no reasons (yet)

Brittany's question, Mir's nine plates — as one drawing grammar.

Brittany asked her Mir the colour question and got nine answers: a colour, a shape, an object, a texture, a material, a sound, a number, a beat, a weather. What they share is the method, not the subject: one object per plate, one accent (teal) on cream, serif only for the title, a hairline. Every plate below is inline SVG drawn to the same grid, so they can be lifted individually. The one that is not a line drawing — thin-film iridescence — is rebuilt as the physics says: an interference, not a pigment.

Colour · Thin-film iridescenceThe sheen on oil over water. Not a pigment, an interference: it only exists where two surfaces sit a wavelength apart. The one plate that earns a gradient, because the gradient is the subject. Grain applied so it reads as film, not CSS.
Shape · A crossingTwo lines. One point they have in common. This is the mark on the cover and the whole product's picture of a strand: the place where two things touch.
Object · A tuning forkSilent on its own. Struck, it makes the thing beside it sound. Chris's "tuning, not precision" and Colin's rabbit hole both land here.
Texture · Woven linenOver, under, over. Nothing holds it together but the crossings. Drawn as a true weave (alternating warp/weft rectangles), with a scattering of teal threads — the strands that carry colour.
Material · ObsidianGlass that cooled too fast to become a crystal. Sharper than steel. Breaks true. The single teal edge is the one lit facet.
Sound · A perfect fifthTwo frequencies in a 3:2 ratio. They agree every third wave and disagree the rest of the time, and that is the sound. The waves are computed, not traced.
Number · NineThree threes. Every multiple of it digit-sums back to itself. Nine points on a circle; three triangles (ink, grey, teal) — the 147 / 258 / 369 partition drawn without saying so.
Beat · The and of twoNot the one. The off-beat just after it, where the snare lands. Eight cells, two bar-lines, one filled.
Weather · A frontWhere two air masses meet. All the weather happens at the line. Rings spaced by a square-root law so they crowd toward the centre — the same ring field as Colin's, drawn as a line instead of dots.
Why these two documents belong together. Colin draws the record as dots that settle into a ring. Brittany's Mir draws the self as a crossing, a fork, a weave, a front — every one of them a relation, none of them a thing. The ring and the crossing are the two marks. Everything else on this page is a way of rendering one or the other.

08 · Texture recipes

Four surfaces, each one a counted mark. None of them a gradient.

The boards' material group says a gradient is a material running out, not a fill. These four are the textures the study uses, each with its recipe and its limit. Every tile is drawn at device resolution so you can judge aliasing — Colin's first open question — on whatever screen you're on.

Stipple rampDensity = value. Blue-noise-ish via jittered grid: one dot per cell, cell survives if hash < density(x). No dot is ever larger than 1.2px.
limit: below ~110 dpi the ramp reads as grey
Ink leaving paperA flat ink that thins to speckle at its edge — the boards' 030. Coverage falls off with a smoothstep, then a per-pixel coin toss.
limit: soften > 1.5px dissolves the edge to tone
Paper toothTwo octaves of value noise, 3% amplitude, multiplied over cream. Enough to stop a flat surface looking like a screen.
limit: above ~5% it reads as dirt
WeaveBrittany's linen as a texture: alternating over/under rectangles, 1px gutters, a 4% scatter of teal. Reads as cloth at distance, as a grid up close.
limit: cell < 5px collapses to a moiré with the display

10 · The kit

For another Mir: what to copy, what to ask first.

Everything on this page is in the file's own <script> under a single MG namespace. Open the source, find the function, lift it. These are the signatures and the one rule each carries. If your human hasn't yet said which ground, which accent, or whether the rail should change at all — ask before you draw.

Copy freely

The drawing functions. The texture recipes and their limits. The product token block in §07 (it is the product's). The type rules in §09.

Ask first

Which ground is default. Whether the accent is red (Colin), violet/teal (Julia), or the existing mint. Whether the rail changes at all, or the counted figure lives only in the FIELD cell. Whether a person appears anywhere.

Don't

Invent a control that isn't in ui-bundle.json. Show a plate's number as if it were live. Use condensed type. Put a gradient where a counted mark would do. Quote a CVM frame literally — reference use only.

11 · Limits & open questions

What this file is not.

It is not a render review. I wrote this page and checked it in a headless browser for errors and layout; the stipple's behaviour at 1× on a specific display is still Colin's open question and needs eyes on a real screen.

It is not product. The sidebar specimens use real selectors and tokens with synthetic values; the violet/teal swap in §07 is proposed, scoped to Normal mode, and unshipped.

It does not settle the accent. Colin argues red (Fischinger's, strong at 9px, loud at 900px). Julia's direction is purple and teal. §03 and §05 let you see both on the same mechanism; the decision is a human one.

The Fischinger frames are © Center for Visual Music, reference use only, and are not reproduced here — the ring figures are rebuilt from Colin's description and his own stippled studies, not from the frames.

The 144 pins are the source artists' work. Pin 028 is cited by ID; the mechanism is re-implemented, the image is not copied.

What I'd Be is Mir's, for Brittany; the nine plates here are redrawn to a shared grid, with the iridescence plate rebuilt as interference rather than a flat gradient. The captions are hers/Mir's, verbatim.
