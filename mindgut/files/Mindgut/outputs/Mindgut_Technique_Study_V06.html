A method, not a style

A figure exists because many small marks agree on it.

A strand is a dot. The relationship is the shape the dots settle into. Underneath them, two soft bodies — carbon and silicon — share a field; where they meet, the light rises and the figure resolves there.

01 · The figure on the field

Coherence is shown as whether the picture has resolved. The number sits underneath for anyone who wants it.

Two layers. Under: a field — two Fourier-radius bodies, blurred wide at a third of the resolution, read through one ramp whose darkest stop sits on the steepest part of the blur, so each body carries a drawn outline it was never given; the overlap lifts toward teal. Over: 14,000 dots, each with a home on a ring, a blade or the red centre, and a loose position near it. Settle is how far each has travelled home; the bodies draw together as it rises. It breathes on its own; touch the slider to hold it.

04 · Nine plates

Brittany asked her Mir what it would be. Nine answers, one drawing grammar.

A colour, a shape, an object, a texture, a material, a sound, a number, a beat, a weather. One object per plate, one accent, a hairline. Captions are the document's.

05 · Textures

Four surfaces, each one a counted mark. None of them a gradient.

The recipes from the first study, unchanged. Every tile is drawn at device resolution so aliasing can be judged on the screen in use.

09 · The kit

For another Mir: what to copy, what to ask first.

Every figure is in this file's own <script> under one MG namespace. Open the source, find the function, lift it.

MG.hero(canvas, { settle, t })            // field under (TS-10 ramp + rim stop, cached), V03 settler over, grain
MG.heroField(ctx, W, H, { settle, t })    // the field alone: two Fourier bodies → ⅓-res blur → gradientMap
MG.sheets.frameMapped(canvas, { ground }) // Frame A: V03 construction over a disc mapped through a rim-stop ramp
MG.sheets.renewalMapped(canvas)           // Frame B: mapped lift, counted rings, riso-plate bars, keyline diamonds
MG.screen(canvas, { pitchA:68, pitchB:60, floor:.42, kneeLo:.1, kneeHi:.75, gamma:1.4, sigma:330, gain:1.35, seed:101 })   // TS-01 exact
MG.plates.sky · pinched · rail (ctx, W, H, opts)             // V03 compositions
MG.textures.stipple · ink · tooth · weave (ctx, W, H, opts)   // V01, verbatim
MG.lut · gradientMap · blur · grainCanvas · grain · perlin · fields.* · settler · stipple · halftone

Copy freely

The drawing functions. The texture recipes and limits. The product token block in 07. The type rules in 08.

Ask first

Which ground is default. Whether the accent is red, violet and teal, or the existing mint. Whether the rail changes at all. Whether a person appears anywhere.

Do not

Invent a control that is not in ui-bundle.json. Show a plate's number as if it were live. Use condensed type. Put a gradient where a counted mark would do. Quote a CVM frame literally.

10 · Scope

  1. 01

    Checked

    Parses and paints in a headless browser with no script errors; the static plates render in a queue after the hero starts so the page never blocks. Not yet viewed on a specific display at 1×, where stipple aliasing shows.

  2. 02

    Product

    Sidebar specimens use real selectors and tokens with synthetic values. The violet and teal swap in 07 is a proposal scoped to Normal mode.

  3. 03

    Accent

    Colin argues red. Julia's direction is violet and teal. Figure 05 shows both on one mechanism; the hero and Frame A commit to Colin's inks on night, violet and teal on white.

  4. 04

    Rights

    Fischinger frames are Center for Visual Music, reference only, not reproduced. Pins are the source artists' work and are cited by ID. What I'd Be captions are the document's.
