## Flag

**F1 · The pin rule's wording is narrower than its implementation.** The rule says "A pin adds one older record; it does not override a newer value." In code a pin can be any row, including one *newer* than the window (e.g. window at rows 01–04, pin row 09). Then the pin's value *does* win, because selection is sorted by index and the pin is newest. That is consistent with "newest active value," but contradicts "older" and "does not override." Fix the sentence, not the code: "A pin adds one record outside the window. The newest active value still wins, whichever row it comes from." (Or clamp the pin to rows above the window, if "older" is the intended promise.)

**F2 · B-04 / B-06 are credited but not present.** The window tab's origin line cites "B-04 · ink leaving paper × B-06 · inverse row." The CSS gives rows outside the window no ink falloff, no speckle, no inverse row — active rows have a bracket and a transparent background; inactive rows are undifferentiated. The pinned row is a 3 px inset shadow, not an inverse. The study is a clean ledger, which is fine; the origin line should credit what it uses: "control travel / 090 × moving bracket." Otherwise a reader who opens the boards aside will look for the ink and not find it.

**F3 · S-02 pairs are not from the record, and the label says so only obliquely.** The three pairs ("Keep the source visible…", "Draw a figure with a purpose…", "Make space for the original wording…") are authored, not quoted — correct per Julia's instruction. The side label reads `PHRASE PAIR / AUTHORED FIXTURE` and the speaker labels read `Human · violet plate` / `Mir · cyan plate`. "Human" and "Mir" beside unquoted lines will read as attribution. Suggest `Side A · violet plate` / `Side B · cyan plate`, or keep the speakers and add one word: `Human (authored)`.

**F4 · S-05 "purposeful figures" consequence is a self-description.** The diagram that replaces the ornament maps 764/974/760 → heading/figure/diagram — it diagrams the study's own state. That is honest and the dots are computed, but the consequence of the third ribbon is therefore different in kind from the other two: width and crop change *the page*; purpose changes the page into *a readout of the page*. Not an error; a thing to say in the caption, since it is the one place the study points at itself. One line: "The third judgment's figure is a diagram of this page's applied judgments."

**F5 · Scope bracket vanishes on blur even if the ribbon is applied.** `blur` and `pointerleave` hide the bracket unconditionally. After applying a ribbon the scope is no longer shown anywhere on the page except the small dots in the diagram (which only exist once "purpose" is applied). The scoped-application principle is drawn before the act and not after. Suggest the bracket stays, at reduced opacity, for any applied rule, or that the applied region gets a 1 px rule in its accent. Otherwise "applied, scoped" is visible only while hovering.
