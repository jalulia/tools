# Interface study — art direction for the three operations

Mir, 2026-10-23. Scope: the Interface study only, as one tabbed interactive piece; hub navigation, work index, moments and statement untouched. Accents: the app's violet, cyan, green and amber, mostly on white. No mint, no dark mode. Step control, not autonomous motion. Authored phrase pairs in S-02.

**Correction accepted.** A remembered judgment does not apply everywhere automatically. The new application needs an explicit scope. In S-05 that means a ribbon never just *lands* — it lands *on something named*, and the name is visible before the consequence is. This changes recommendation 3 below and the shared diagram grammar in 1.

Five recommendations. Each ties to one of the three operations or to all three.

---

## 1. One diagram grammar for all three tabs: a scoped bracket

**All three.** The three operations share one structure — a span of the record, an operation applied to it, a result — and the study should draw that structure the same way in every tab so the reader learns it once. The form: a thin bracket (one line weight, square corners, 2 px ticks at its ends) drawn beside or under the material it governs, with a mono label at its midpoint. In S-02 the bracket spans the two lines that are paired. In S-04 the bracket *is* the window — it spans the rows in ink, and stepping the window moves the bracket, not a band of color. In S-05 the bracket is the **scope**: it spans exactly the region of the new page that a ribbon may change, and it appears when a ribbon is picked up, before it is laid.

This is the direct answer to the scope correction: the bracket is drawn first, the consequence second, and the reader sees that a judgment was applied *to this* and not to the page. Color: the bracket takes the accent of the operation (see 5) at full strength; everything it does not span stays in black-on-white. Nothing outside a bracket ever changes color.

**Fails if** the bracket is decorative — it must move with the operation and never be drawn where nothing is governed.

---

## 2. S-02 as a typographic plate, not a text box: display size, two screens, the letter as the mask

**Overlap.** The boards' two-ink idea (B-03) only reads at scale. Set the paired phrases large — the longest pair at 48–64 px, not body size — and let the *letterforms* be the mask over the two screens, so the ring interference is visible inside the strokes. Below the plate, the full passage at reading size in plain black, with the paired phrases underlined in their ink. The plate is the picture; the passage is the evidence.

Composition: a tall plate, cream-white ground with a 20 u margin rule, pairs stacked with 1.5× line spacing so the overprints have room to show their fringe when dragged. The two inks are **violet** and **cyan** screened — the app's own pair, and the overprint of a violet screen and a cyan screen reads as a deep blue-violet, which is a legible third without a third ink. The *shared only* mode drops both single screens to 8 % and leaves the overprints at 100 % — the sheet goes nearly white with a few dark words on it, which is the strongest single image the study can produce.

Registration by drag: the cyan layer moves, the violet stays; a mono readout under the plate shows the offset in units (`+3 u`) only while dragging, then fades — it is a ruler, not a meter, and the caption says so.

**Fails if** the pairs are set at body size; the moiré becomes noise and the plate becomes a highlighted paragraph.

---

## 3. S-05 as a spread, with the scope drawn before the ribbon lands

**Transfer.** Draw it as a two-page spread of one open book, not two books: the *left page* is the source — three short quoted lines from the record, each with a ribbon emerging from its margin in its own accent; the *right page* is the new editorial page, blank-ish, with three mono-labelled regions outlined at 1 px: `heading` · `figure` · `text`. The gutter is real: a 12 px shadow down the centre, the only soft edge in the study.

Pick up a ribbon (click, drag, or arrow-and-Enter): the right page's bracket (rec. 1) appears around the one region that ribbon may change, labelled with the ribbon's tag and the word `scope`. Lay it: the region re-sets. The three consequences, equal in territory, unequal in kind:
- **normal-width type** → `text`: the text block's runs widen visibly; a hairline back to the left page's quote.
- **complete people** → `figure`: a flat spot figure (B-10 grammar, keyline, hatch) in the region gains a face and a tool in hand — the figure was already there, cropped at the shoulders; it becomes whole.
- **purposeful figures** → `heading`: the heading's decorative rule is replaced by a small chart whose bars are the region's own word counts — the ornament becomes a figure that says something.

The ribbon stays visible on the left page at full strength throughout; the laid copy on the right is the same ink at 60 %, with the hairline between them. Source whole, application scoped, copy marked as a copy.

**Fails if** the regions change outside their bracket, or if a ribbon can be laid without its scope having been shown first.

---

## 4. S-04 as a ledger with holes you can see through: the blank as a window, not a mark

**Composition under a limit.** The composed line at the foot is five fields, each a labelled slot. A filled field is the newest inked value, set in black. A missing field is not a dash and not a gray box: it is an **open slot** — a rectangle with no fill and a 1 px edge, the same width as the field would be, through which the paper shows. Above it, a hairline rises to the window bracket and stops at the row where that field's last value *was*, now outside ink. The reader sees not just that something is missing but where it went.

Step control: `←` `→` or two buttons with travel (a rest state and a pressed state, 2 px down), stepping the bracket one row at a time. Pin: click the row's margin dot; a pinned row gets a small **amber** tab at its left edge and stays in ink below the bracket. The composed line updates on each step with a 120 ms cross-fade, no slide — the composition is a stable thing to inspect, not an animation to watch.

Material: white paper with a faint tooth, rows in 11 px mono at one size, field labels in mono caps. Ink is black; the bracket is **green**; pins are amber. Nothing else is colored.

**Fails if** the open slot is styled as an error state (red, dashed). It is a true report of the window, and it should look like one.

---

## 5. Four accents, four jobs, declared once

**All three.** The app's four hues need a job each, held across the tabs, or the study will look like a palette demo:

- **violet** — the record's first side (S-02 ink one; S-05 source quotes; S-04 row carrier mark)
- **cyan** — the record's second side (S-02 ink two; S-05 laid copies; S-04 the other carrier)
- **green** — the operation in force (every bracket; the window; the scope)
- **amber** — a human act on the record (S-04 pins; S-05 the ribbon currently in hand)

Declared once, in a four-line key at the head of the study in mono, and never re-explained in a tab. Mostly white means: the accents appear only on the brackets, the ribbons, the pins and the screened inks; grounds, type, rules and figures are black on white. The study should be printable in black and lose only the brackets.

**Fails if** any accent appears on a surface — a fill behind text, a tab background, a card. Accents are for marks, not for areas.

— Mir
