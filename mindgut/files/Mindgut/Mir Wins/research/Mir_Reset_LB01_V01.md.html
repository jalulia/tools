## 2. One direction: the work-table, not the gallery

**Make LB-01 a single page where three source records visibly change one piece of work the reader can edit, and where the reader can see — and reverse — which record did what.**

Three operations, which are the three things Mindgut does that a transcript does not:

1. **Scope** — a remembered judgment applies to a named region, not everywhere (Julia's correction, strand 1012).
2. **Provenance** — each line on the table carries who wrote it (person / model), and a model's proposal is visibly not a person's decision.
3. **Inspection** — "available" and "consulted" are different states, and the reader can open the source behind any change.

Everything else in Open Studio (the editorial series, the posters, the booklet) becomes a *consequence* of the table — printed from its state — or is cut. Rich surfaces survive, but they are outputs of the instrument, not a world beside it.

**Layout.** Two columns on white, Archivo normal width, JetBrains Mono for addresses. Left: **the record** — a short ledger of real strands (speaker, date, one line each, mono address). Right: **the work** — a piece of writing the reader can edit. Between them: **the brackets** — a scoped bracket per applied record, drawn from the left-hand row to the exact right-hand region it governs (your existing moving-bracket grammar). Below: the **window** control (deliberate steps, as now) and the single **pin**. Violet = person's line; teal/blue = Mir's line; green = a rule in force; amber = the reader's own act (a pin, an override). Accents on marks only; the sheet prints black and loses only the brackets.

**The one new interaction that makes it a workspace:** the reader can **type into the work**. When they do, the brackets re-evaluate: a scoped rule whose region they touched lights green; a rule outside the region does nothing; a proposal (model line) that they have not accepted stays dashed. Then they can **accept** a proposal (it becomes solid; a human reply is recorded with the reader's name as "accepted by") or **override** it (amber strike, reason field, source kept). That is the florals case from strand 722 — "this was a decision, this was its scope, this was the reason, here is what changed, decide again" — as a thing you can do, not a thing you are told.

## 3. A non-design subject for the table

The subject must be something a person would actually bring back to Mir, where a scoped judgment, a proposal and an override all occur naturally, and where nothing is medical or personal. Three candidates, in order of preference:

**A. A reading list with rules (recommended).** The work: a list of eight books with one line each. The records: (1) a person's line — "no more than one book per author on a shortlist" — a *scoped* rule (applies to the shortlist region, not the full list); (2) a Mir line — a *proposal* to add a ninth title, with its reason — dashed until accepted; (3) a person's later line — "I've changed my mind about X, keep the reason I had" — an *override that keeps its predecessor legible* (strike, not delete). The reader can reorder, accept, override. Every operation is visible. Nothing here is design, and everyone has a reading list. Brittany's "personal digital bookstore" in #mindgut (strand 868) is the team's own precedent that this is a real use.

**B. A trip plan.** Days as regions; a rule about pace ("no more than two things a day") scoped to weekdays; a model proposal for a detour; an override when the weather changes. Natural scope, natural override. Slightly riskier — travel reads personal fast.

**C. A film club's notes.** Keeps Open Studio's "short film" thread. Scenes as regions; a judgment about an ending scoped to one scene; a proposal to cut; an override. Works, but it inherits the mood-board feel the criticism named.

All three use **authored** lines, labelled as such. The exact wording of the three operations should be modelled on the real records already verified in `content.json` (764, 760, 884) so the *shape* is true even where the *subject* is new. Do not put real quotes on a fictional subject.

## 4. tools-a5 techniques worth adapting (inspected entry.js, not assumed)

Of 168 entries, 74 are `unsorted` and 6 are `PROPOSED BY TOOL`; I am naming only canonical or clearly-graded ones, and only where the entry's own text argues for the use.

1. **`d1-inline-annotation`** (canonical, display-specimen). Four inline forms — pill, tab, bracket, lozenge — "measured, not placed: each is sized to the headline's x-height and sits on its baseline, which is why they read as punctuation rather than as stickers." Adopt its sizing rule for the scope bracket and the provenance tag: every mark on the table is set to the x-height of the line it governs. This is the single most useful import; it is what stops the brackets reading as UI chrome.

2. **`d2-circled-glyphs`** (canonical). Press layer crisp, hand layer in paint-marker with "pressure (thick and thin along its length), a waver, and sometimes a doubled pass." Its own fault list records that uniform vector circles read as "SVG ellipse." Adopt for the **reader's acts only** — the amber override strike and the pin — so a human mark is visibly a hand and a rule is visibly a press. Do not apply it to Mir's lines.

3. **`e2-modular-grid`** (canonical, swiss-modular). Track widths snapped to integers in script, published as a custom property, "so the hairlines are pixel-exact instead of half-covering two device pixels. A grid whose own rules are blurry is not a grid." Adopt the snapping for the ledger rows and brackets. **Avoid `e3-bento-grid`** — the library itself marks it `historical`: "the hierarchy it promises is not one it can deliver."

4. **`c5-spec-sheet`** (canonical, technical-doc). Not the barcode and punches — the principle: "a document that looks like the machine that made it, so there is no ornament anywhere on it," and crisp mono for anything functional. Adopt for the record ledger. Skip its ink-starve; this is a screen table, not a print.

5. **`crx01-instrument-typology`** (canonical, technical-doc). "A pen is thirteen numbers": a declaration drives silhouette, elevations, detail and callouts from one data object. Adopt the *architecture*: the table's state is one JSON object; the ledger, the brackets, the printed editorial sheet and any poster are all renders of it. This is how the rich surfaces stay honest. Note its ruling is `proposed`, not Julia's — "No raster anywhere on this sheet" — so cite it as a proposal.

**Deliberately not adapted:** `c4-ribbon-type` (woven ribbons read as object illustration — the clip-art risk again, and its own text says so); the riso/xerox atoms (`two-drum-riso`, `xerox-degradation`, `dot-screen-20`) for the working surface — press failure is lovely in the editorial outputs but on the table it is fake telemetry in material form; the sound lane; anything in `unfiled`.

**Type.** The library's `display-specimen` and `atmospheric` styles already declare Archivo for text; `technical-doc` declares it for display. Normal width only, per 764. JetBrains Mono for addresses in all six styles — keep it.

## 5. Five constraints so the ambition stays legible

1. **Every change on the right has a bracket to a row on the left.** No change without a source; no source without a region. If a bracket cannot be drawn, the change is not allowed.
2. **Person / Mir / reader are three carriers, never merged.** Violet, teal-blue, amber. A Mir line is dashed until a person's reply makes it solid. "Approval" is a reply, not a stamp (my earlier caution, strand 1004, still stands).
3. **State is one object; surfaces are renders.** The editorial sheet, any poster, the printed booklet page — all derived from the same JSON as the table. Nothing is painted separately.
4. **No autonomous motion.** Step and window controls only; 120 ms cross-fade. A composition the reader can hold still and inspect (Julia's standing rule from 1012).
5. **Fiction is declared once, at the top, in the ledger's own typography** — "Authored example · modelled on verified records 764 · 760 · 884" — not repeated as a badge on every element.
