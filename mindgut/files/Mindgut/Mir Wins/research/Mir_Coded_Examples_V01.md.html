# Mindgut-specific coded studies — proposals V01

Mir, 2026-10-23. For an aside in `Mindgut-Boards_V05.html` and, through it, the Mir Study hub's Interface study. Sources read: `build/cats5.json` (5 chapters, 10 techniques B-01…B-10, 144 item notes), `build/rebuilds.js` (the `R[key](ctx,W,H,P,pal)` convention, design units 1000 = sheet width, seeded `mul()`, `vnoise`, `hatch`), hub `build/content.json` and `build/template.html` (seven bentos: identity, palette, conversation+source, states, type, graphic system, model handoff).

Rule for inclusion: a study belongs here only if it needs *both* Mindgut's subject (a two-sided record with a short window and a long tail) *and* a specific technique from the boards. Anything that would work for a generic chat product is out.

What the hub currently lacks, read from `template.html`: every bento is about the record as *evidence* (source drawer, state marks, correction column, model select). Nothing is about the record as *material to work with* — composition, inference, alternatives, omissions, transfer. All five proposals below are operations on the record, not views of it.

Terms: "the window" is the recent conversation the model sees; "the record" is everything. Carbon/Silicon are the two carriers. No shipped mechanic is claimed; every control is proposed.

---

## S-01 · Take one away

**Sources.** Item 12 (Jenga — "the stakes of removing one piece") × B-01 Counted screen (tone by density, no gray) × chapter I direction ("the record is letters counted into three phases, so draw it as counted marks").

**Insight.** A record's value is not its length but which strands are load-bearing. The Jenga image says it: removal is the test. Draw a short exchange sequence as a stippled band (B-01's grammar) where every strand contributes dots in proportion to its weight; then let the reader pull one out and watch the picture of the *reply* — not the record — thin where that strand was holding it. The study makes omission the operation, not correction.

**Interaction and visible change.** Twelve strands as twelve labelled columns of the band, each column's darkness its contribution. Hover a column: it lifts 8 u (Jenga block). Click: it is removed; the band re-screens with that column's density gone, and a one-line reply at the foot of the sheet (illustrative, fixed text) loses the words that strand carried — shown as the type-as-texture runs of B-04 fading to speckle under those words. Click again: it returns. A counter in mono reads `holding 11 / 12`. Pull three and the reply is mostly speckle. No slider.

**Composition / material.** Cream sheet, one ink, B-01's stochastic screen at cell 2 px. Band 560 u tall across the full width; columns 70 u, gutters 8 u. Column labels in mono at one size below, with a carrier mark in the margin (a filled dot for the human side, an open ring for the model side) — the one place this study touches provenance, and only as a mark. The reply line in 11 u gray runs (B-04 type-as-texture), 120 u tall, under a hairline.

**Fails if.** The removed column's effect on the reply is scripted so obviously that the reader sees it as a cartoon. The honest version weights columns by a real count (letters per strand, or a declared fraction) and the reply dissolution is proportional — the fixture must say the weights are illustrative. Also fails if more than one ink: the moment a removed column goes red it is a dashboard.

## S-02 · Two inks

**Sources.** Item 28 (red–blue moiré — "the red exists only where they interfere") × B-03 Ring interference × chapter IV lettering group (item 22 WE/DIVIDED — "words built of words").

**Insight.** The hub's graphic bento shows two carriers as paired columns. B-03 already encodes the better idea: two fields, two inks, and a third thing that exists *only* where they overprint. Carry it to a page of text. Set a short passage in two inks — one for the human's words, one for the model's — and let the overprint (where a model sentence contains a phrase the human said first) be the third color. The record's real structure is not two columns but one text with two inks and a few places where they are literally the same words.

**Interaction and visible change.** A paragraph of a reply, 14 lines, set in B-03's blue screen. Beneath it the human's three lines in the red screen. Where the reply reuses the human's phrase, the two screens overprint and the phrase goes dark-violet (B-03's two-ink overprint rule). Control: a registration offset — drag the red layer up to 6 u out of register and the overprints fringe, as mis-registered print does; the fringe is the illustration of a quote that has been paraphrased, not kept. Press `register` to snap back. Optional: a toggle `show only overprint` that drops both single inks and leaves the shared phrases on cream — the human's share of the model's answer, as an image.

**Composition / material.** Cream sheet with B-03's margin. Two inks at B-03's screen density; type is the *carrier* of the screen (each glyph is a mask over the ring grating), so letters have the moiré inside them. Headline in the lettering register: the word SAID set large, built of the two screens, the overlap as the third ink. Caption in mono: ink one · ink two · overprint.

**Fails if.** Overprint is computed by exact string match only — then "disembodied" and "disembodiment" don't overprint and the image lies. The fixture must use a declared matching rule (stem, or a hand-marked list) and say so. Also fails as a generic diff viewer if the inks are red/green; it has to be the boards' blue and red, screened, or it is GitHub.

## S-03 · The room has a door you didn't open

**Sources.** Item 2 (room in characters — "the record as a place") × B-02 Glyph room (one-point box, projected glyph lattice, dotted trails) × item 11 (Doors — "a storyboard from one repeated object").

**Insight.** B-02's trails show recall as a path through a room. What the hub never shows is the *untaken* path: the strand recall could have reached and didn't, because the window was full or the reach stopped. Alternatives are an object. Draw the glyph room with one trail taken and three doors in the lattice walls that stayed shut — each a region of the wall where the glyph density proves there was material behind it. The reader opens one.

**Interaction and visible change.** The room as B-02 draws it. One dotted trail (the recall that happened) from the front plane to a glyph cluster on the back wall. On the side walls, three rectangles of the lattice are drawn at a half-step larger cell — doors. Click a door: the wall lattice behind it pulls back 40 u in perspective (the storyboard-from-one-object method of item 11), a second trail in the other accent color runs to it, and a caption in mono gives that region's declared contents: `12 strands · same subject · outside the window`. Open a second door and the first closes — one alternative at a time. A small mono readout: `taken 1 · available 3`.

**Composition / material.** B-02 as is: ground, yellow plate frame inset 20 u, one-point box, glyph cell 14 u on the back wall, trails at 10 u spacing. Doors are not drawn as doors — they are lattice regions at cell 18 u with a 1 px rule. The pulled-back state uses the same projection with the wall plane translated; nothing new is drawn, only moved.

**Fails if.** Door contents are invented prose — then it's a story about memory, not a study of it. Contents must be counts and labels only. Fails too if more than three doors: it becomes a menu.

## S-04 · The window, printed

**Sources.** B-04 Ink leaving paper (the gradient as a material running out) × chapter III "operated" group (controls with travel, "a rest position and a lit one", item 90 faders) × B-06 Phosphor log's inverse row.

**Insight.** The hub's model-handoff bento is a select. The actual object is the *window* — a short span of the record that is fully inked, after which the ink runs out and the page is speckle. B-04 already draws that page. What it doesn't do is let the reader move the window along the record and see what falls out of full ink into speckle, and what the next reply can and cannot be made of. Composition as an operation: the model composes from inked text only.

**Interaction and visible change.** A page 1333 u tall: forty strands as B-04's gray type runs, oldest at the top. The ink coverage is a band, not a left-to-right falloff: solid over the most recent seven runs, falling to speckle over the next three, nothing above. The control has travel — a fader on the right margin (item 90 grammar: a slot, a cap, a lit position at the newest). Drag the cap upward: the solid band moves with it, and a fixed reply line at the foot of the page — composed, illustratively, from phrases in the inked runs — re-sets as the band moves, losing phrases from runs that have gone to speckle and gaining ones that have come into ink. One inverse row (B-06) marks the strand the human last spoke on; when it leaves the band the row's fill drops to a hairline.

**Composition / material.** B-04's cream, fine tooth, 11 u type pitch, one blue ink. The fader is the only drawn control: 12 u slot, 28 u cap, mono tick labels every seven runs (`−7`, `−14`, `−21`). The reply at the foot in the same type at 1.5× — the only thing on the page not screened, because it is what the model is about to say, not what it has said.

**Fails if.** Treated as a mechanism diagram of the actual window — it is not; the seven/three figures are declared study values. Fails if the fader gets a readout in numbers (then it is telemetry); the ink is the readout. Fails if the reply is pre-written per position — it must be assembled from the inked runs by a visible rule, or it proves nothing about composition.

## S-05 · Ribbon, book, ribbon

**Sources.** B-10 Spot illustration (item 48 ribbons — "bookmarks as recall, colored reaches into a text") × B-07 Brushed ribbon (one stroke, one light, fibers) × item 10 (tools on a text — "stewardship, literally").

**Insight.** Creative transfer is the one operation no bento touches: a judgment made in one project applied, by hand, to another. Item 48 draws recall as ribbons leaving a book. Draw *two* books. A ribbon lifted from the first (a judgment, e.g. a type rule) can be laid into the second; the second book's page re-sets under it. The ribbon is B-07's fibered band, so it has a lit side and a body — the judgment is a physical thing that goes from one place to another, and it leaves a gap where it was.

**Interaction and visible change.** Two B-10 books, left and right, flat spot fills with 4 u keyline on aged stock. Four ribbons leave the left book's fore-edge, each tagged in mono with a short rule (`normal width`, `no faces`, `one object`, `cream not black`) — the tags are the study's declared judgments, not quotes. Drag a ribbon across the gutter to the right book: it is redrawn as a B-07 fiber band in flight (lit from the top left), and when dropped it seats between two pages of the right book. The right book's visible page, a small B-04-style text block, re-sets by that rule — the type runs change width, or a tiny spot figure loses its face — and a hatched gap stays in the left book where the ribbon was. Drag it back: the gap closes, the right page reverts. Only one ribbon in flight at a time.

**Composition / material.** B-10's four-ink grammar: brown stock with grain hatch, green and ochre book covers, cream page blocks with hatch pitch 9 u, keyline 4 u. Ribbons in B-10's flat inks at rest; in B-07's fiber shading only in flight — the material change *is* the transfer. The gutter between books is 120 u of bare stock with one mono caption: `taken from · applied to`.

**Fails if.** The right page's re-set is cosmetic (a color change) rather than a legible consequence of the rule. Each of the four ribbons needs a visible, different effect on the page, or the study is a drag-and-drop demo. Also fails if a ribbon can be in both books at once — the gap in the left book is the point.

## Ranking — the three I would build

**1 · S-02 Two inks.** It replaces the hub's weakest bento (06 / Graphic system, paired columns) with the boards' own best idea: two fields and a third thing that exists only where they interfere. It is composition, provenance and omission in one image, it uses a technique already in `rebuilds.js` (B-03), and it produces a headline (SAID in two screens) that could be the study's cover. The matching rule is the only hard part, and declaring it is honest.

**2 · S-04 The window, printed.** It gives the model-handoff bento (07) something to hand off *from*. B-04 is already written; the additions are a band instead of a left-to-right falloff, one fader with travel, and a reply assembled by rule. It is the clearest picture of a short window over a long record that doesn't draw a diagram. Risk: being read as a mechanism claim. Mitigation is in the fixture caption.

**3 · S-05 Ribbon, book, ribbon.** The only one about transfer, which is the operation Julia's own question on 10-23 was circling ("carry what worked into one genuinely new artifact without copying the pins"). B-10 and B-07 are both rebuilt already; the work is the drag and the per-ribbon page effect. It is the most illustrative and the least "UI", which is why it should be third, not first: the hub needs the two above to earn it.

Not built now: **S-01** (closest to a cartoon if the weights aren't real) and **S-03** (strongest idea about alternatives, but B-02's perspective transform makes the door-pullback the most code for the least certainty of reading).

## What changes in the hub if these land

- Bento 06 (Graphic system) → S-02 replaces it. Legend becomes `ink one · ink two · overprint`.
- Bento 07 (Model handoff) → S-04 sits beside the select, or replaces it; the select becomes a label on the fader's cap.
- Bento 03 (Conversation + source) keeps the 764 excerpt; S-05 could carry the same four tags as its ribbons, so the two bentos share vocabulary without sharing copy.
- Bentos 01, 02, 04, 05 (identity, palette, states, type) stay. S-02 and S-04 are one-ink and two-ink on cream; they do not disturb the palette bento, which is about UI chrome.

## Constraints carried from the boards

- One or two inks per sheet. No third color unless it is an overprint.
- Every control has travel or is a click; no sliders on effects.
- Mono labels at one size; the figure is never explained twice.
- Nothing glows outside a frame (chapter V).
- No face on the product (chapter IV illustration group). S-05's "no faces" ribbon is a rule tag, not a drawing of a face.
