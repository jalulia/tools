# Coded studies V02 — revised principles, final copy, critique

Mir, 2026-10-23. Follows `Mir_Coded_Examples_V01.md`. Codex's implementation constraints for S-02, S-04, S-05 are accepted as written. Two principles in S-05 are withdrawn below. S-01 and S-03 stay as unbuilt proposals.

---

## 1. Two S-05 principles, withdrawn

**"No faces" as a rule tag.** This inverted the record. Julia's correction in 974 was not *remove faces*; it was that cropping faces made everyone disembodied. The judgment that survived is *complete people* — figures with faces, doing something. A ribbon labelled "no faces" would carry my error, not her correction. Withdrawn. The tag is `complete people`, and its visible consequence is a figure gaining a face, not losing one.

**The gap in the source book.** Taking a ribbon out of one book and leaving a hatched gap made a judgment consumable — as if applying "normal-width type" to a new page used it up in the old one. Judgments are the opposite: once made, they apply everywhere at once, and the source record is untouched. Withdrawn. The source stays intact; the ribbon is *read*, not removed. What moves is a copy, and the study should show that as a copy — same ink, same tag, a hairline back to its origin.

Both errors are the same error: I made the record a stock of things that get used up. It is a list of things that were said. Nothing in it is spent by being applied.

## 2. Final artifact copy

One description, one insight, each ≤ 1 sentence. No dialogue, no names. Study-level labels follow the boards' convention (code · title).

### S-02 · Two inks
**Description.** A short passage set as two screened inks, one for each side of an exchange, with the phrases both sides used shown as the overprint where the screens meet.
**Insight.** A record's two sides are not two columns; they are one text in two inks, and the shared phrases are the only places the inks touch.
**Fixture caption.** Phrase pairings declared by hand. Each ink can be shown alone; *shared only* leaves the overprints on the sheet. Registration is dragged for the eye; the offset measures nothing.

### S-04 · The window, printed
**Description.** A page of record rows under a band of solid ink that moves as new rows enter, with a visible rule composing the next line from the newest inked value per field and marking any field with no inked value.
**Insight.** What a reply can be made of is whatever is still in ink; the rule is visible, and so is each blank it leaves.
**Fixture caption.** Authored record of four entering rows and one pinned older row. The composition rule is the only author; no model is called. Window length and the pin are study values, not product behaviour.

### S-05 · Ribbons, both books
**Description.** Three judgments from one body of work — normal-width type, complete people, purposeful figures — laid as ribbons into a new editorial page, each changing one visible thing on it while the source stays whole.
**Insight.** A judgment is not spent by being applied; it is copied forward, and the page it lands on shows which one arrived.
**Fixture caption.** Source judgments are quoted from the record (764, 974, 760). Their application to a new page is a design proposal. Drag, click or keyboard; one ribbon at a time; the source book is never altered.

### S-01 · Take one away — unbuilt
**Description.** Twelve record rows as a stippled band; removing one shows which were holding the reply up.
**Insight.** A record's worth is not its length but which rows are load-bearing.
**Status.** Proposal. Requires real per-row weights to be honest; not built.

### S-03 · The door not opened — unbuilt
**Description.** A room of characters with one recall path taken and three regions on its walls that could have been reached and weren't.
**Insight.** Every recall has alternatives, and they are countable even when they were not taken.
**Status.** Proposal. Requires declared counts for each region, never prose; not built.

## 3. The combination, critiqued

**What holds.** The three studies cover three distinct operations — overlap (S-02), composition under a limit (S-04), transfer (S-05) — and none duplicates a bento that stays (identity, palette, states, type). Each is a different material: two inks on cream, one ink on paper, four flat inks on stock. Read in sequence they say: what the two sides share, what the next line can be made of, what carries forward. That is a sentence about the product, not three demos.

**Where it is thin.**

1. **S-02 and S-05 both quote 974 and 764.** S-05's three ribbons are the same judgments the hub's Moments carousel already shows, and S-02's declared pairings will almost certainly draw on the same strands. Three surfaces quoting two exchanges reads as a small record. Suggest S-02's pairings come from a *different* exchange than the Moments — 760 is the obvious one, since it is the craft correction and its reply is long enough to have reused her phrases.

2. **S-04's "missing field" mark is the strongest honest thing in the set and the copy underplays it.** A rule that composes from inked values *and shows its blanks* is the one place the studies admit what a window can't do. Give the blank a visible form equal to the filled fields — a hairline rule the length of the field, not a dash — so an empty reply reads as a reply with holes, not as an error.

3. **S-05's "distinct visible consequence for each" risks three unequal effects.** Normal-width type is a measurable change to every line on the page. Complete people is a change to one figure. Purposeful figures is a change to what a chart *is*. If the type change is the only one that touches the whole page, the other two look minor. Fix in the fixture: the page has exactly three regions (a heading, a figure, a text block), and each ribbon owns one region in full. Equal territory, unequal kinds.

4. **Keyboard on S-05.** With click/keyboard as well as drag, "one ribbon at a time" needs a visible rest position. Suggest the three ribbons sit in a row on the gutter, one of them raised (focus), and `Enter` lays it. Otherwise keyboard users operate an invisible state.

**A stronger real interaction, for S-04.** Codex's constraint says four rows enter a moving window and an optional pinned older row can contribute. The pin is the interesting control and the copy treats it as optional. Make it the primary: the window moves on its own (rows enter on a slow tick, or on `→`); the only thing the reader *does* is pin. Pinning an older row keeps it in ink after the band has passed, and the composed line changes to show what that one held row contributed. That is the product's actual proposed control — a person choosing what stays reachable — rendered as one gesture, and it is a better teacher than moving the band by hand, which teaches only that windows move. Unpin and the line loses that value and marks the blank. Nothing else in the hub shows a human act *on* the record that is not a correction.

**Palette, source language, decision handling.** Agreed that these follow. Two notes: S-02's blue and red are the boards' inks (item 28) and should not be reconciled with the UI palette's green — the studies are on paper, the UI is on screen, and the two should look like different substrates on purpose. And "decision handling" should take S-05's correction: a decision is copied forward, never moved; the source is never shown altered by having been applied.
