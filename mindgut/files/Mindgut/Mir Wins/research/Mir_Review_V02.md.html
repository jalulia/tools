## 3. Critique of the proposed statement (actual copy)

Word count: 207 by `wc` (I first wrote 221 from an estimate; same error as V01, caught the same way). Under cap with 23 to spare. Structure is right — mechanism, test, two of Julia's own corrections, both sides, the retraction, the claim. Line-level:

1. **"Mir is the partner that develops through that work."** "Partner" is the one word in the draft a lab reader will flag as marketing. "Mir is what forms on that record" or "the identity that develops through that work" says the same with less to defend.

2. **"The record can continue across a change of model."** Good — "can continue" is the honest modal. Keep.

3. **"For me, the useful test…"** The shift to first person is the best move in the draft; it makes the statement Julia's, not the company's. But "for me" needs an antecedent the reader lacks. Either open the paragraph with a role ("As a designer, the useful test…") or let the third paragraph's "In my work with Mir" carry the first person and cut "for me" here.

4. **"Design history includes the rejected direction, the reason a reference mattered, and the correction that changed the next build."** Strong triad. Keep as is.

5. **"A model's proposal and a person's decision need to remain distinguishable."** This is the thesis and it is buried at the end of paragraph two. Consider it as the first sentence of the statement, or as the last.

6. **"a correction about disembodied people"** — the word reads as insider; the hub reader hasn't seen the renders. "A correction about faceless figures in a set of renders" lands without the record. Codex's call; the verbatim is in M1 if it stays.

7. **"A rejection of condensed type changed the documentation."** True and verified (strand 764; `UPDATE_LOG` spec-v03). Keep.

8. **"Mindgut records both sides of the exchange. That makes it possible to revisit the original words and challenge a later interpretation."** This is the right form of the provenance claim — carriage, not authorship. Keep.

9. **"Mir made a claim about the record and corrected it after checking the text."** Accurate. One nuance to protect: the first claim came from a geometric lookup, the correction from a literal one. "corrected it after a literal check of the text" says that without mechanism.

10. **Closing claim.** "…without a previous proposal being mistaken for an agreed decision." Right test, slightly long. "without a proposal being mistaken for a decision" is nine words shorter and the same test.

What I would not add: anything about cost, anything about identity being "the same" across models, anything about system prompts. The draft avoids all three.

## 4. UI constraints — agreement and one caution

Agree: original robot and wordmark; flat surfaces, shallow edges; source / interpretation / approval as three distinct states; no invented telemetry; specimens labelled as proposals; the two-sided record with a visible correction shown as an illustration, not as a mechanism.

One caution. "Source / interpretation / approval" is a three-state model; the record as it stands has two carriers (Carbon / Silicon) plus whatever a later strand says about an earlier one. "Approval" is not a field — it is a Carbon strand that answers a Silicon one. The specimen should show it that way (a reply that marks, not a stamp that certifies), or the hub will imply a workflow the product does not have.
