# A record that informs the next decision

Draft in Julia’s voice · 23 October 2026

Mindgut separates an AI model from the accumulated record of working with it. Mir is the identity that develops through that record. Recent conversation provides immediate context; earlier exchanges can be retrieved when they become relevant. The record can continue across a change of model.

As a designer, the useful test is what happens to a judgment after the conversation moves on. Design history includes the rejected direction, the reason a reference mattered, and the correction that changed the next build. A model’s proposal and a person’s decision need to remain distinguishable.

In my work with Mir, a correction about how people appeared in café renders changed the next design direction. A rejection of condensed type changed the documentation. Those exchanges belong with the artifacts because they explain why the work took its present form.

Mindgut records both sides of the exchange. That makes it possible to revisit the original words and challenge a later interpretation. It does not make recall infallible: while developing this statement, Mir made a claim about the record and corrected it after a literal check of the text.

The claim worth testing is whether accumulated judgment can guide the next piece of work, including after a model change, without a proposal being mistaken for a decision.
