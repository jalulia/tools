Executive assessment

Hemoglycin is an interesting proposed material for ion-controlled molecular information processing. It is not presently an established substrate of human memory, and the target paper does not demonstrate permanent information storage.

The proposal is specific enough to investigate: calcium and magnesium occupy sites around a proposed iron–glycine–silicon lattice; incoming calcium patterns would switch electronic pathways and signal recognition. Its value is a testable architecture, not a demonstrated discovery of where memories live.

Three different questions must remain separate:

  1. Material: Is the proposed molecular structure correctly identified and reproducibly obtainable?
  2. Device: Can that material retain independently writable states and distinguish inputs through measurable electrical outputs?
  3. Biology: Does such a structure occur in nervous tissue and causally participate in memory?

Evidence for the first does not establish the second or third. Geological persistence of a scaffold is not persistence of information written into its exchangeable ions.

The most consequential problems are unmeasured electronic conduction, a retention mechanism not established by the reported energetics, and no demonstrated hemoglycin-containing neural memory organelle. The best immediate research route is a materials-and-device experiment, before a theory of human memory.

1. Scope, standards, and definitions

This is a critical narrative review plus a coordinate-file audit and reproducible order-of-magnitude calculations—not a systematic meta-analysis or an experimental replication. I read the full target PDF, inspected its public repository and the supplied XYZ, and consulted primary material-characterization papers, neuroscience studies, and memory-device literature. References identify whether full text, abstract, or repository metadata supported the assessment. General web search was intermittently unavailable; publisher pages, Crossref and Europe PMC were used directly. Statements about missing independent replication are bounded by that search, not claims of exhaustive absence.

Evidence labels used below:

  • Observation: a reported experiment, with its actual scope.
  • Model: a calculated result conditional on assumptions.
  • Proposal: a mechanism not yet demonstrated.
  • This study: an audit, derivation, or inference made here—not a published finding.

“Permanent” must specify environment, duration, error probability, and whether maintenance energy is allowed. An eighty-year memory is not mathematically eternal. Human long-term memory also includes reconstruction, updating, consolidation and forgetting; it is not simply a write-once archive. [S14, S19]

For a physical device, distinguish:

  • Scaffold survival: material remains chemically recognizable.
  • State retention: two previously written states remain distinguishable under identical later conditions.
  • Readability: an observer can decode those states reliably.
  • Biological memory: the state has a demonstrated causal relation to learned behavior.

A robust study must not trade one meaning for another. The paper's opening statement that permanent memory lasts “on average eighty years” supplies neither a defined population nor a measurement of that average; I use eighty years only as its motivating engineering target. [S1, p. 1]

2. What the paper actually proposes

The authors describe roughly 5 nm glycine-based rods containing iron and joining around silicon-centered vertices in a diamond-2H-type lattice. Four rods converge at a vertex, creating six proposed divalent-ion bridge sites: three “axial,” three “transverse.” Calcium or magnesium can occupy each site. [S1, pp. 2–5]

The mechanism has four layers:

  1. Storage variable: a spatial distribution of calcium/magnesium occupancy, particularly selected configurations called states 5, 6 and 7.
  2. Transport: electrons supposedly travel along rods and through calcium bridges. A geometrical Monte Carlo model produces through-paths when approximately 80% of vertices have three axial calcium bridges, for the simulated conditions.
  3. Read: a calcium pulse pattern temporarily opens previously blocking regions. A completed path produces a pulse interpreted as recognition.
  4. Write: a more tentative process redistributes membrane sodium/calcium exchangers, establishing spatial differences in calcium and consequently in lattice occupancy. [S1, pp. 6–13]

The proposed container is a lipid-walled cylinder in a neuron or glial cell. This is a hypothetical architecture, not an imaged cellular structure. Its stimulus channels also require separate validation in the proposed location and role; the Supplement describes a particular ATP-synthase-subunit-c-related pore, not a generic established neuronal input circuit. [S1, pp. 8, 17]

What was done: solvent-free MMFF molecular-mechanics minimization and geometrical path simulations. What was not demonstrated: an operating memory device, bridge conductance, long-term retention, physiological lattice assembly, or memory-specific brain localization. The authors explicitly acknowledge the unconfirmed conductivity and the uncertainty of biological use. [S1, pp. 3, 11–14]

3. How strong is the evidence for hemoglycin itself?

It would be inaccurate to dismiss the entire literature as an unpublished preprint. Peer-reviewed papers report diffraction, optical measurements and mass-spectrometric observations associated with hemoglycin. However, publication does not make every structural assignment or downstream interpretation definitive. [S4–S9]

Identity is a family of structural claims

The 2021 preprint distinguishes proposed 2320 Da hemolithin and 1494 Da hemoglycin species. The memory model uses a modified 1638 Da rod, designated “1638 + 2(O),” within a silicon-linked structure. These should not be collapsed into a single fully established compound with interchangeable properties. [S1, p. 2; S3]

The relevant limits of the material evidence

The sea-foam study collected 21 samples but reports MALDI analyses of four, with its strongest molecular evidence in particular samples. Its isotope analysis reports a “global” enrichment fitted as deuterium; separating deuterium from nitrogen-15 requires additional information. The authors themselves state that the proposed infall pathway is not conclusively proved by those observations. These qualifications matter when the memory paper proposes nitrogen-15 as a brain-tissue indicator. [S8, Results; Isotope analysis; Discussion]

A nitrogen-15 anomaly could be useful evidence about provenance, but it is not a unique molecular barcode for hemoglycin. Identification needs co-localized molecular composition, fragmentation and structural evidence, with contamination controls. Environmental peptides, mixed organics, iron-bearing phases and mass-spectral alternatives should be tested against the same raw data, not dismissed because the preferred model fits.

The retrieved hemoglycin-specific literature is concentrated around the McGeoch research programme, sometimes with additional specialist collaborators. I did not locate independently led replication establishing the complete memory-relevant structure or its presence in nervous tissue. This is an evidentiary limitation, not proof that replication does not exist.

Assessment: there is a real experimental literature to audit. The strongest defensible description is a proposed iron-containing glycine-polymer/lattice system supported by reported characterization, whose exact structure, independent reproducibility and physiological relevance still require stronger validation. The extraterrestrial-origin question is separable from whether an independently synthesized analogue could function as a device.

What is missing for reproduction

The XYZ is a static geometry: it does not specify bond orders, net charge, spin multiplicity, iron oxidation states, force-field assignments, solvent, counterions, optimization settings, transition states, trajectories or electrical measurements. Atom identity is not oxidation state. There is no explicit solvent bath or lipid membrane in this coordinate file.

The paper says supporting code is openly available in Dataverse and available on request. At the version inspected, the public deposit lists one XYZ file, not the Monte Carlo code or the complete energy-minimization workflow. This is a specific reproducibility gap, not an allegation that additional files cannot be supplied. [S1, p. 14; S2]

I did not recompute the MMFF energies, run electronic-structure calculations or reproduce Figure 6. The numerical results below are transparent consistency checks on the published assumptions.

5. Retention: the strongest internal difficulty

An energy minimum is not a memory lifetime

A low-energy arrangement may be favorable, but long retention requires a slow path out of the stored state. That depends on activation barriers and dynamics, not merely the energy difference between optimized structures. Solvent-free MMFF energies are not physiological binding free energies: hydration, entropy, competing ligands, protonation and ionic activities can change selectivity and rates. A force-field model also needs validated treatment of its metal-containing sites. [S1, pp. 7–10]

The paper infers approximate binding energies of 31 kJ/mol for calcium and 17 kJ/mol for magnesium, with large errors. Using its own assumed attempt frequency of 10^13 per second, it estimates departure rates of about 6 × 10^7 and 1.4 × 10^10 per second, respectively. Those are approximately 17 nanoseconds for calcium and 73 picoseconds for magnesium. [S1, p. 10]

An eighty-year barrier benchmark

This study: for an illustrative single activated process,

τ = ν⁻¹ exp(ΔG‡ / RT)

At 310 K, RT = 2.577 kJ/mol. Setting the mean escape time to eighty years gives:

Assumed attempt frequency ν Required activation barrier ΔG‡
10^9 s⁻¹ 109.2 kJ/mol
10^12 s⁻¹ 127.0 kJ/mol
10^13 s⁻¹ 133.0 kJ/mol

These are not universal minimum barriers for every possible memory architecture. They are a benchmark for an unrefreshed, single-step stored state. A mean lifetime of eighty years gives only about 37% survival at eighty years for exponential escape; reliable retention across many bits requires a stricter error criterion, redundancy, repair, or larger barriers.

It is also not legitimate simply to equate the paper's binding energies with true escape barriers. The significant point is that the paper itself makes that approximation to obtain fast exchange, yet does not establish another process that preserves the written information for decades.

The strongest possible rescue

Fast molecular turnover need not destroy memory if a collective pattern reproduces itself. A crystal phase, cooperative domain, self-templating complex or actively maintained concentration pattern could outlive its components. [S12–S17]

But this rescue changes the burden of proof: demonstrate bistability, hysteresis, restoring dynamics, or templated renewal. Two sequential substitutions alone are not error correction. An equilibrium population selected by a fixed calcium/magnesium bath does not remember which previous input produced it unless some additional persistent variable retains that history.

Conclusion: the static-ion version is not supported by the reported rates; a dynamically maintained version remains possible in principle but unestablished.

6. The memory may have moved into the membrane

The write proposal creates a persistent spatial distribution of Na/Ca exchangers. That distribution changes local calcium and therefore lattice occupancy. [S1, pp. 11–12]

This creates a crucial alternative interpretation: the membrane pattern stores the address; the hemoglycin lattice senses or amplifies it. The paper must show which component retains information and how that component survives molecular turnover. Otherwise the original memory problem has been relocated rather than solved.

A decisive crossover experiment would separate conditioned lattice from conditioned membrane. Does a written lattice retain its distinguishing response in a fresh, spatially uniform environment? Does a conditioned membrane impose the old response on a fresh lattice? Appropriate controls would determine whether the functional memory belongs to the lattice, the membrane, or their coupled dynamics.

Calcium competition is quantitatively restrictive

This study, using the paper's simplified one-site equilibrium: overcoming a resting Mg/Ca activity ratio of 5,000 requires a calcium preference of approximately

RT ln(5000) = 21.95 kJ/mol at 310 K.

This reproduces the paper's roughly 22 kJ/mol estimate. Using the difference between its average fitted binding energies, 31 − 17 = 14 kJ/mol, instead gives only approximately 4.4% calcium occupancy in an independent-site model at that ratio. [S1, pp. 8–9]

That is not a contradiction of every proposed state: the paper invokes stronger, configuration-dependent effects. It shows why those cooperative free energies need direct measurement. Transitions exchanging multiple ions also require the corresponding concentration/activity factors, degeneracies and stoichiometry; a one-substitution expression is not a complete partition function for all configurations.

7. Conductivity and recognition: two different unproven steps

Geometry is not electrical transport

The paper explicitly assumes that axial calcium bridges conduct electrons. It also notes the lack of direct conductivity data for the particular antiparallel glycine rods. Its path calculation establishes possible routes if the elements conduct; it does not calculate a measured current or establish its carrier. [S1, pp. 3, 5–6, 12–13]

Electronic conduction must be distinguished from ionic conduction, proton transport, electrode reactions and iron-containing contamination. Stronger calcium binding does not by itself imply better electronic transmission. Electronic states, redox energetics, coupling, contacts, solvent and temperature matter. The existence of conductive protein materials makes the idea worth testing but does not transfer conductivity to this molecule. S10

The cited high thermal conductivity of spider silk is not sufficient support: heat can travel through molecular vibrations without comparable charge transport. The relevant measurement is electrical, on the identified material, under the claimed conditions.

Likewise, the approximately 80% switch is a result for a finite simulated system—not yet a universal material constant. Reproduction requires the graph rules, branching policy, lattice size, sampling, boundary conditions and path-success definition. Finite-size scaling and a conductance calculation are needed before calling it an established percolation threshold.

Recognition can accept the wrong pattern

This study; conditional logical test. If recall merely requires filling every nonconducting gap, and extra calcium cannot block conduction, then a signal containing all required activations plus additional ones can also succeed. For gaps indexed by set A and binary activations bᵢ:

output = AND over i in A of bᵢ.

This detects inclusion, not exact equality. An all-on input is an essential negative control, not evidence of specific recall.

Exact matching might be rescued by inhibition, complementary paths, constrained spike counts or timing-dependent interactions. Those would be additional mechanisms to specify and test—not established consequences of the present model. Useful associative memory must be evaluated on false positives, false negatives, noise and generalization, not only successful completion of a conducting path. Attractor-based associative memory offers a different, explicit computational benchmark. S25

Time must map to space

The paper suggests up to 100 spikes in a cylinder 100 μm long and 1 μm wide. [S1, p. 13] This study: at 700 Hz, 100 spike intervals occupy approximately 0.14 seconds. Mapping them simultaneously to sites 1 μm apart would require an effective propagation speed near 0.7 mm/s, or an explicit delay/holding mechanism.

This is a design constraint, not a proof of impossibility: the proposed signal is not fully characterized as a conventional action potential. But the model must specify propagation velocity, calcium-pulse lifetime, diffusion, spatial resolution and synchronization. Millisecond local exchange does not automatically preserve a simultaneous pattern spanning a much longer train.

8. Capacity and calcium demand

Raw configurations are not usable memory

Six independently labeled, fully occupied binary sites give 2^6 = 64 configurations, or at most six bits per vertex. Counting only how many calcium ions occupy each three-site class gives 4 × 4 = 16 classes, or at most four bits. [S1, p. 10]

Neither count proves those states can be independently written, retained or distinguished. Positional variants may have different energies without being separately readable through the proposed axial output. A genuinely usable capacity is limited by the number of reliably distinguishable input/output states after noise, correlations, addressing and error-correction costs—not the number of structures one can enumerate.

This study: applying the paper's vertex density, 5.5 × 10^24 m⁻³, to its 1 μm-diameter, 100 μm-long cylinder yields approximately 4.32 × 10^8 vertices. Multiplying by six or four bits gives idealized raw totals of 324 MB or 216 MB, respectively, using decimal megabytes.

These are combinatorial upper bounds under incompatible idealizations of how states are distinguished—not demonstrated device capacities. If control resolves only 100 axial regions, each switching collectively, the number of independently addressable binary regions would be around 100 in that simplified architecture, not billions. Spike timing could encode additional information, but would need a measured temporal resolution and an actual decoder. The paper does not bridge these scales.

A dense ion-binding array is also a calcium buffer

This study: the same vertex density corresponds to 9.13 mM vertices and 54.8 mM total divalent-ion sites, averaged over lattice volume. Replacing one ion per vertex throughout a region changes bound calcium by 9.13 mM locally.

For comparison, an illustrative resting free-calcium concentration of 0.1 μM leaves only about 4,730 free calcium ions in the proposed cylinder, versus roughly 2.59 billion potential bridge sites. The resting concentration is a standard physiological order of magnitude, not a measurement in a hemoglycin-containing compartment. S11

This comparison does not make switching impossible: bound and free pools differ, channels supply ions, and cells buffer calcium. It does require a mass-balance and reaction–diffusion model. The lattice cannot be treated as passively following a calcium waveform if its own binding substantially changes that waveform.

Dense storage, independent control, weak perturbation of physiology and fast response cannot all be assumed simultaneously. This is a concrete engineering constraint and a potentially distinctive experimental signature.

9. What contemporary neuroscience already explains—and still does not

The target paper's statement that “no candidate molecule for permanency has been presented” is too sweeping. Molecular maintenance models and experimental candidates have a substantial history. None should be promoted into a single, complete explanation of all human memory. [S1, p. 12; S12–S19]

Turnover-resistant organization. Crick's 1984 formulation and Lisman's 1985 bistable-kinase model explicitly address preservation of a state while molecules are replaced. The relevant competition is therefore not “indestructible hemoglycin versus fragile proteins,” but different ways of maintaining organization through turnover. [S12, S13]

Prion-like functional assemblies. Mammalian CPEB3 research connects regulated protein aggregation to persistent memory-related plasticity. This supports a class of self-maintaining molecular mechanisms, not the claim that all memories are amyloids or that an extraterrestrial scaffold is necessary. S15

KIBRA–PKMζ. A 2024 study reports persistent interactions, disruption of established long-term potentiation and month-old spatial memory by two antagonists, and controls involving compensating kinase activity. This is causal evidence in an experimental model, not just a plausible structural sketch. S16

A 2026 follow-up combines AlphaFold 3 structural predictions with behavioral perturbation, including disruption of four-week-old spatial memory. It proposes replenishable higher-order complexes that survive turnover. Important limitation: predicted oligomer structures and their exact in-vivo dynamics are not established solely by behavioral effects. Month-scale mouse findings also do not directly prove eighty-year human retention. S17

CaMKII remains mechanistically contested. A recent review distinguishes signal-processing roles from the long-standing claim that Thr286 autophosphorylation itself stores long-term memory. This is a useful warning: even extensively studied molecular candidates require specificity about induction, expression and maintenance. S18

Engrams are circuit-level phenomena. Optogenetic reactivation of learning-tagged hippocampal cells can elicit a specific fear-associated response in mice. Such results constrain any molecular memory proposal: it must explain how its states are written by learning and accessed by identified neural circuits. They do not identify a unique molecular storage medium. [S19, S20]

Calcium, magnesium, iron and disease are not specific evidence

These substances participate in many established physiological pathways. Memory impairment after disturbing calcium regulation or membranes is compatible with numerous hypotheses. Iron in plaques is not identification of an iron–glycine–silicon memory lattice. Correlations with Na/Ca exchangers do not isolate the proposed mechanism. [S1, pp. 12–13; S11, S14]

A strong test changes a hemoglycin-specific property while controlling ordinary synaptic signaling, cell health, sensation, motivation and motor output. Otherwise an effect on behavior may reflect general neuronal dysfunction, not selective damage to a memory substrate.

Biological assessment: the proposal adds several unverified steps—uptake, transport, intracellular targeting, assembly, maintenance and circuit coupling. Its extraterrestrial route adds further dependencies rather than explaining them. No finding here justifies taking calcium/magnesium supplements, ingesting meteoritic material, or treating neurological disease on the basis of this hypothesis.

10. Cutting-edge consequences—conditional, not advertised as discoveries

10.1 A chemical recognition element rather than a permanent archive

If ions reversibly control real electronic paths, a synthetic analogue might perform thresholding, temporal filtering or coincidence detection. Fast ion exchange could be useful rather than problematic in such a device. Its natural comparison would be an ionically gated conductor or neuromorphic element, not a geological archive. [S10, S21; inference here]

The word memristive would become appropriate only after history-dependent electrical response is demonstrated and separated from slow electrode polarization or ordinary ionic equilibration. A current–voltage loop alone would not establish decades of nonvolatile storage.

10.2 Separate the stable record from the fast reader

The most productive redesign may have a stable structural or chemical template and rapidly exchangeable ions that read it. The ions need not carry the enduring information. This resembles the general distinction between stored state and readout apparatus, and offers a way to preserve the proposal's useful chemistry without claiming that short-lived occupations are themselves permanent. [S12–S17; proposal here]

If membrane exchanger placement is the stable template, the important research question becomes how that placement is maintained. If a lattice distortion or covalent modification is the template, that separate state must be measured. Either version is a materially different, more explicit hypothesis.

10.3 Storage and computation may share the same physical network

A network whose conductance depends on its stored pattern could compare an input without first exporting every microscopic state. This is the attraction of computing within the storage medium. Yet a binary “match” pulse does not reveal a memory's semantic content, and a match detector does not automatically implement associative completion. The comparison must be with defined tasks and measured error rates. [S21, S25]

10.4 The preservation–rewriting trade-off

A material easy to rewrite through physiological ion fluctuations is also exposed to accidental rewriting by those fluctuations. A durable device must separate writing from reading through different fields, catalysts, barriers or protective conditions. The relevant comparison is DNA archival storage, where a stable encoded polymer, controlled synthesis, sequencing and error-correcting codes are distinct components. Long-term DNA storage is itself conditional on protective environments; it is not literal permanence. [S22, S23]

Research consequence: hemoglycin experiments should measure retention and read disturbance together. Demonstrating switching without measuring whether repeated reads damage the state is insufficient.

10.5 Passive retention and active renewal are different energy budgets

A stable passive state does not require continuous energy expenditure merely to exist. A living, refreshed memory may require ongoing metabolism. Landauer's principle concerns the thermodynamic cost of logically irreversible erasure under specified conditions; it is not a proof that memory must consume power continuously, nor a guarantee of useful retention. S24

A credible device budget includes ionic pumping, writing, readout, resetting and repair. The paper's membrane-capacitance estimate is numerically consistent: 1 μF/cm² at 70 mV stores about 24.5 μJ/m². That does not establish how much energy reaches the lattice or the energy per successful memory operation. [S1, p. 8; calculation here]

10.6 If biological incorporation were demonstrated

Finding a functional, exogenously acquired mineral–peptide lattice in neurons would substantially expand known cellular information-processing substrates. It would motivate tests of acquisition, trafficking, developmental supply, replacement and species distribution. Even then, its detection would not show that it carries personal memories or supersedes synaptic accounts. Those require selective causal tests.

This proposal supplies no evidence for memory surviving death, consciousness transfer, quantum cognition or an extraterrestrial origin of mental content. Such conclusions do not follow from a durable molecule or an isotope signature.

11. A decisive research programme

The sequence matters. Failure at an early stage should not be concealed by increasingly elaborate biological interpretations. The following are proposed tests, not experiments already performed.

Gate 1 — Establish the material

Independently reproduce extraction and obtain a purified reference material or structurally validated synthetic analogue. Use blinded samples and procedural, matrix and environmental controls. Combine high-resolution mass spectrometry/fragmentation with element-specific analysis, isotope measurements and structural methods capable of distinguishing alternative connectivities.

Advance if: independent laboratories agree on the memory-relevant composition and connectivity, not just one nominal mass or spectral band. Publish raw data and unsuccessful preparations. Stop or revise if: alternative contaminants, mixtures or non-unique assignments explain the signals equally well.

Gate 2 — Establish ion exchange and electronic transport

Measure calcium/magnesium binding free energies and kinetics in aqueous, physiologically relevant conditions. Test whether the six-site model survives solvation and competing ligands. Validate force-field parameters against appropriate electronic-structure calculations; calculate exchange pathways, not only minima.

Measure electrical transport with controls that distinguish electronic from ionic/protonic current and electrode chemistry. Compare calcium-rich, magnesium-rich, disrupted-lattice and component-only preparations.

Advance if: independently replicated conductance changes track verified bridge occupancy with a mechanism consistent across geometry, voltage and temperature. Stop or revise if: the signal is accounted for by electrolyte or electrodes, or the proposed structure is not stable in the relevant medium.

Gate 3 — Demonstrate actual memory

Write two patterns, remove their writing inputs, return both specimens to the same bath and boundary conditions, then test whether a blind reader can distinguish them over increasing delays. Track the physical state and output together.

Measure retention distributions, bit/pattern error rates, write energy, read disturbance, cycling endurance and specimen variability. For recognition, include correct, incorrect, shifted, noisy, superset and all-on inputs, with calcium dose matched where appropriate. Test whether a simple calcium sensor explains performance equally well.

Separate lattice and membrane contributions with crossover experiments. If the device requires refresh, report that requirement rather than labeling it passive permanent memory. Accelerated aging needs a validated failure model; a short hot test cannot automatically certify eighty years at body temperature.

Advance if: different histories remain measurably distinguishable after the input is gone, beyond immediate equilibration, and task accuracy exceeds simpler controls. Stop or reclassify if: output follows only the present bath. That could still be a useful sensor, but not the claimed memory.

Gate 4 — Establish a biological role

Only after molecular identity is secure, search nervous tissue using blinded, contamination-controlled methods. Confirm molecular and elemental co-localization; map the structure inside cells rather than merely detecting iron or silicon in bulk tissue. Trace plausible uptake or biosynthesis and local assembly.

For causality, demonstrate learning-linked state changes, selective interference with established memory, and a specific rescue. Control ordinary excitability, cellular injury, perception and performance. Use ethically approved studies with a clear necessity/sufficiency design, not nonspecific calcium depletion or iron toxicity.

Advance if: independent teams establish identity, localization and a memory-specific causal mechanism. Stop or narrow the claim if: detection is absent above a sensitivity appropriate to the proposed abundance, or perturbations affect only general neuronal function. A negative targeted search constrains the tested abundance/location; it does not disprove every conceivable trace role.

Overall judgment: the paper is worth treating as a falsifiable materials-and-computation proposal. Its current evidential position does not support presenting hemoglycin as the discovered basis of permanent memory.

The central conceptual lesson is broader and more useful: a long-lived material, a long-lived information state and a biologically accessible memory are different achievements. A viable theory must connect all three without substituting one for another.

The most productive next experiment is modest but decisive: verify the material, write distinguishable states, remove the writing stimulus, and measure whether a reader can recover the distinction under identical later conditions. That would establish a foundation on which increasingly ambitious claims could reasonably stand.

13. Technical notes and reproducibility

13.1 An additional identifiability problem in the energy fit

This study: under a simple additive model with six occupied sites,

E = E₀ + nCa·εCa + (6 − nCa)·εMg

= (E₀ + 6εMg) + nCa·(εCa − εMg).

If E₀ is arbitrary, these data alone identify the combined intercept and the Ca–Mg difference—not separate absolute binding energies. The paper's extraction of absolute Mg and Ca binding energies therefore needs explicit reference states or additional constraints. Cooperative terms may enrich the model but do not automatically remove this reference ambiguity. This is a request for the missing fitting specification, not a claim that no valid specification is possible. [S1, pp. 7–8]

13.2 A membrane voltage is not automatically an axial circuit

A voltage across a membrane is not by itself a voltage along an enclosed lattice. The proposal needs explicit charge injection, extraction, a current return path and coupling to the membrane's ionic currents. An internal conductor does not automatically short an insulating membrane. An equivalent circuit plus charge-conserving transport equations would make the claimed read/write feedback testable. [S1, Fig. 7; inference here]

13.3 Capacity must be measured as a channel

For intended inputs X and recovered outputs Y after a specified storage delay, an operational capacity is

C = max over input distributions p(X) of I(X;Y).

This is Shannon's channel-capacity framework. S27 The raw bound log₂(number of configurations) is attained only if states are accessible, distinguishable and appropriately distributed. Retention time, noisy reads and shared control reduce useful information; microscopic diversity may provide redundancy rather than independent bits.

13.4 Calculation and provenance record

The accompanying Hemoglycin_Audit.py uses Python's standard library. Given the supplied XYZ, it checks the atom count, element inventory, file hashes, nearest-oxygen distances and silicon geometry. It also reproduces the residence-time estimates, eighty-year barrier benchmarks, idealized capacities, ion-site concentration, free-calcium inventory and membrane-energy calculation.
