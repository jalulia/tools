01 / The proposed mechanism

Six positions.
One very large assumption.

Four rods meet at a junction. The paper proposes six calcium-or-magnesium bridges between them. Their occupancy would control electronic paths. [1, pp. 2–6]

26 = 64 possible arrangements

  1. Write

    Change an ion pattern, potentially through membrane exchanger placement.

  2. Retain

    Keep a distinguishable state after the input disappears.

  3. Read

    A matching calcium signal completes a conducting path.

The critical bridge is still missing: neither the proposed electronic conduction nor a complete write–retain–read cycle has been measured here.

A completed path
can recognize too much.

If extra calcium cannot close a path, “contains the required pattern” is not the same as “matches exactly.” Try the inputs.

02 / The persistence problem

The atoms can change.
The distinction must not.

A scaffold can survive while its ion pattern equilibrates away. Conversely, a collective pattern can persist while its components are replaced. Retention belongs to the information-bearing state—not necessarily to the same atoms. [4]

Replacement need
not mean forgetting.

Current memory research investigates persistent organization through molecular renewal. KIBRA–PKMζ studies include month-scale mouse memory experiments; the 2026 oligomer account combines structural predictions with behavioral tests—not proof of lifelong human retention. [5] [6]

The quantitative retention benchmark

For one unrefreshed activated escape process, τ = ν⁻¹ exp(ΔG‡/RT). At 310 K, an eighty-year mean escape time requires about 109–133 kJ/mol when ν ranges from 10⁹ to 10¹³ s⁻¹. Mean lifetime is not a reliability guarantee. This is an illustrative model, not a measured hemoglycin barrier. [3, §5]

What our earlier bridges do—and do not—add

The earlier material-response discussion emphasized intervention over resemblance: perturb a proposed interaction and test what changes. The coherent-ionics comparison distinguished a driven collective mode from a retained state. Neither analogy demonstrates hemoglycin conduction or permanent memory. The supplied bridge about a ferrofluid interface is relevant to presentation craft only, not molecular evidence.

PFD also supplies restrictions that are more specific than “coupling matters”: its stated stable interior potential branch has V″V⁗/(V‴)² between 0 and 1/3, invariant under linear coordinate and energy rescaling. A real application would first need a justified physical coordinate and effective potential. This ratio has not been measured for hemoglycin. [P2, strand 316]

04 / Locating the record

Does the lattice remember?
Or does its environment?

The paper’s tentative writing mechanism patterns membrane exchangers. They may preserve the spatial instructions while the lattice responds to them. A swap experiment separates the possibilities. [1, pp. 11–12]

05 / The decisive experiment

Remove the cause.
Can you still read the difference?

Audited here

683 atoms; checksum matched to the public deposit. No measured retention or conductivity in that file. [2]

Reported in the literature

Material characterization.

Mass spectra, diffraction and optical findings, mainly within the same research programme. An independent 2023 abstract reports non-replication of earlier Allende polymer detections. [7] [8] [9]

Still assumed

A functioning memory element.

Selective writing, hydrated electronic transport, durable retention and reliable recognition remain to be demonstrated. [1]

Not established

A role in neural memory.

Detection, intracellular location and learning-specific causality require separate evidence. Calcium or iron alone is not identification.

The most productive next claim

Start with a memory element.
Not a theory of all memory.

Ion-controlled temporal filtering or chemical recognition may be useful even if permanent storage fails. The result should determine the category—not the title of the proposal.

06 / What the primer changes

The material claim
has a contrary result.

The supplied primer adds a consequential source missing from the earlier audit: an independent attempt to reproduce the Allende polymer detections.

Origins 2023 · primary conference abstract · p. 143

“none matched those reported”

Parker and colleagues report that their Allende MALDI/TOF-MS analysis found large species, but not the previously reported polymer assignments. The abstract also raises blank/control concerns. It is independent contrary evidence—not a full analytical adjudication and not proof of absence. [9]

Review of the supplied primer: keep, strengthen, replace

Keep: the distinction between material, state, information and biological memory; the audited XYZ view; the separation of configurations from readable capacity; and explicit labels on hypothetical calculations.

Strengthen: the independent contrary result should affect confidence in the material identification before discussing its possible neural role. The new report makes that challenge visible in the evidence chain.

Replace: the primer’s hypothetical-feedback demo retains a chosen 70% contrast. That is openly labeled illustration, but not a derived retention law. Here the PFD comparison uses its declared equations and reproduces its numerical release states. It remains a model—not a mapping to hemoglycin.

Add: the membrane/lattice crossover test and the missing physical mapping to PFD. A proposed “read” must include both its measured output and its effect on the state.
