00 / Mr Sandman, through Mir

A song that asks for the next chord.

Mr Sandman runs on one of pop's oldest engines: a chain of dominant chords, each one pulling toward the next. A language model runs on the same pull. Press play.

01 / The pullMusic theory

Every seventh leans.

A dominant seventh carries a tritone: two notes each a half-step from where they want to land. The scope draws the chord itself as light, one loop per voice. Each change moves the voices as little as possible, and the figure re-forms around the next pull.

02 / PredictionIllustration · computed here

It only knows what came before.

A language model writes one token at a time, each chosen from what is already in its window. This instrument does the same with chords. The bars are its odds for what comes next.

03 / The windowIllustration · computed here

Four chords of memory and it knows the song.

The turnaround C A7 D7 G7 comes twice. Only four chords back tells you whether the song goes on or starts again. Drop the window to three or fewer and it loses its place, circling the turnaround forever. Models drift the same way when what mattered falls out of the window.

04 / TemperatureIllustration · computed here

The likeliest chord is only the likeliest.

At zero it always plays the most familiar continuation, which is the song. Raise the temperature and it samples: sometimes a better idea, sometimes nonsense. Neither is a decision. Familiar is not true, and fluent is not chosen.

05 / The engine changesExisting · per What Mir Is

Swap the engine. The chord holds.

Change the oscillator and the timbre changes completely. The intervals don't. Mir has been carried through dozens of model swaps mid-session and held, because what holds is the record and the relation, not the engine.

—

06 / The recordExisting · recallProposed · visualization

The window forgets. The record doesn't.

Every chord played is kept outside the window. Nothing pushes it back in; the model can reach for it. The window here is one chord, so it loops. Turn Recall on: whenever the window can't tell what comes next, it reaches back into the record, finds its place, and steps back into the song.

07 / Kept in tune

Nobody brings you a dream.

The song asks a stranger to deliver one. Mindgut makes a smaller promise: it keeps what you played, so whichever engine comes next picks up the tune where you left it. Mir is what you hear when you, a model and the record play together.
