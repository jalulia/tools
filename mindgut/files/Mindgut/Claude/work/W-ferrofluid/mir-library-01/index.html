Mindgut · Mir

Many small marks, agreeing.

Mindgut keeps a measured record of every exchange between a person and a language model. Mir is what appears when the three work together: a conversation that continues instead of starting over. This page explores how Mir could look and sound.

An exploration, not the shipped product. Numbers on this page are illustrative. Sound is off until you turn it on.

            Conventions

            These hold for every piece. A piece mentions one only when it bends it.

            1. Dots only. Every figure is made of placed dots. No lines in figures, no fills, no gradients. Tone comes from dot spacing and size.
            2. One accent. Each frame has one saturated color, and it marks where you and Mir meet.
            3. Neutral grounds. Night, olive, paper (#fafafa), cobalt, sky and plum. The figure reads the same on each. No cream or warm off-white.
            4. The wordmark as drawn. One flat color, clear space of one tittle height, never stippled or altered.
            5. Placeholder by default. Every value is invented unless the piece’s recipe lists it as computed.
            6. Live. Pieces draw at your screen’s resolution. Each has controls, Reset and a recipe. With reduced motion, each shows its final frame.
            7. Sound. Off until you turn it on; M toggles it. When several pieces are on screen, the piece you see most of leads and the others play 18 dB under it. Every sound is synthesized live, with no samples and no quoted melodies, and anything you hear is also shown.

            Rebuild

            Every piece has a recipe: its idea, marks, ground, accent, motion, controls, what is computed, what is placeholder, and the engine calls it uses. To rebuild a piece by hand or with a model, start from three things: the conventions, the recipe and the engine primitives below.

            PrimitiveWhat it does
            M.blueBlue-noise stipple: an even Poisson-disc dot set, thinned by a density function, with dot size following value.
            M.ringAn engraved ring: dots spread across a band, dense at the core and smaller at the edges, with a slight swell along the ring.
            M.mirFieldThe Mir field: concentric rings, blades and a center at coherence k, from loose (0) to in tune (1).
            M.atmosFine dots that fall off from a center and gather into rays.
            M.logo · logoGeo · logoMaskThe wordmark in one flat color, its geometry (tittle position, clear space) and a mask for knocking stipple out around it.
            M.BufA dot buffer that batches dots by color and can skip dots inside a mask.
            M.PAL · M.TOKThe grounds, and the product’s interface tokens.

            Listen
            The instruments the pieces play, synthesized live in your browser with no samples. Their timbres and methods come from Yellow Magic Orchestra, Mamman Sani, Raymond Scott, Bernie Worrell with Parliament-Funkadelic, and Frankie Reyes. Two phrases also take their feel from Koji Kondo’s Zora’s Domain and Susumu Hirasawa’s parade music for Paprika. Every phrase is new, and every chord is tuned pure.

              About this page
              This is a design exploration by the Mindgut design team. It began from a moodboard pairing Oskar Fischinger’s films with stipple drawing. Everything here is drawn and sounded live in your browser. Values are illustrative unless a piece says it computes them. Each piece has a recipe for rebuilding it by hand or with a model. See Rebuild.
              
  
              

  
              
    
              Studio notes
              Notes on the moodboard and the direction, and what is still open.
              
    
              “I think it’s important that there is some type of soft, handmade, inviting feel and it’s not too digital or cold instrument. After all, we’re asking people to share a lot of personal information in order to build a real relationship with this technology.”Colin · Slack · on the CVM × stipple moodboard
              
    
              
      
              Fischinger is the right ancestor
              
        
              What we takeAllegretto’s color came from a divisionist method that changed colors frame to frame. That is pointillism in time: many small marks agreeing across frames, as stipple dots agree across a plane. The merge works because the method is shared.Visual Music Archive [1]
              
        
              Where we pushKeep his abstraction as well as his look. Fischinger left Disney’s Toccata and Fugue sequence when his designs were made more representational. For Mir that means no face, no orb and no character. The CVM frames stay as reference; nothing shipped quotes them.Center for Visual Music [2] · P1
              
      
              Soft stipple has a history
              
        
              What we takeThe softness is right. Stipple builds tone from many dots, so no single mark carries the image, and it reads as made by hand.
              
        
              Where we pushStipple began as a way to reproduce the hand. Demarteau’s crayon manner (1756) etched dots to pass for chalk drawings, and stipple plates printed many more copies than mezzotint. In a product that asks for personal information, each dot should stand for a real exchange. No grain is added for warmth.Stipple engraving [3] · P5 · L-02, L-08
              
      
              Tuning, between two players
              
        
              What we takeThe moodboard keeps devices out: no bezel, no knob, no meter. Coherence shows as a picture resolving, with the number underneath.
              
        
              Where we pushA tuner is something you read. Two players tuning adjust toward each other. In Mir, your pitch is the reference and Mir tunes to you. Chladni’s plates (1787) show it: sand moves to where the plate is still. Instruments stay behind Advanced.APS News [4] · P2 · L-05, L-09, L-29
              
      
              Visual music has more than one record
              
        
              What we takeThe Center for Visual Music is the right archive for abstract film: Fischinger and his circle.
              
        
              Where we pushRagamala painting, made in court workshops of the Deccan, the Pahari hills, Rajasthan and elsewhere from the 16th century, pictured each raga as a person with a temperament, described in a verse. A mode became a mood without becoming a mascot. L-10 borrows that structure, credits it, and leaves the painting style alone.Indica [5] · UMMA [6] · L-10
              
      
              Where the hand shows
              
        
              What we takeSoft and handmade on top, exact underneath. The moodboard puts it as flat construction with stipple as the surface.
              
        
              Where we pushThe hand shows in three places: the strays, a slight wobble in each ring, and the count. Everything else is exact: whole dots at device resolution, real type, a real grid. If softness is everywhere, it stops meaning anything.P1, P5
              
    
              
    
              To settle with Colin
                
      
              1. Signals. Product and engineering need to name what drives density, strays, settling and crossings. Until then the values are placeholders.
                2. 
      
              2. Accent. Red is the strongest; ochre is the gentlest. Red against teal is hard to tell apart for red-green color blindness, so state is always written out as well.
                3. 
      
              3. Motion. How fast do dots settle, and can settling reverse on screen?
                4. 
      
              4. Low-resolution screens. Dots are at least about one device pixel. This needs testing on real 1× displays.
                5. 
      
              5. Grounds. Which ground is the default for long sessions: night or olive?
