# Mindgut — creative brief

**Create a visual world for Mindgut that feels authored, alive, and worth spending time in.** Show how that world becomes useful software, a compelling website, and recognizable communication. The presentation must demonstrate the design—not argue for work the viewer cannot see.

### What we're designing for

Mindgut is the harness. Mir is the AI partner people work with inside it. Model choice, accumulated context, memory, and connections across conversations are central to the proposition. Their precise capabilities and limits need technical confirmation; proposed interactions must remain distinguishable from existing functionality.

The human value is being able to return to unfinished thinking, discover a relevant connection, and develop something further without repeatedly rebuilding the context.

PFD informs the investigation. It is not consumer-facing copy or decorative scientific theatre.

### What people should feel

- **Recognition:** something of my thinking is present here.
- **Curiosity:** there may be something useful I haven't noticed.
- **Agency:** I can inspect, question, correct, and choose.
- **Attachment:** this is distinctive software I get to know and make mine.
- **Clarity:** I understand what happened and what I can do next.

These feelings must come from behavior, composition, and detail—not adjectives in the deck.

### Creative direction

Build from Julia's interest in words as visual material, layered context, personal software, and tactile digital craft.

The references contribute different things:

- **Julia's MG board:** expressive typography, accumulated marks, unexpected relationships, texture, and a richly inhabited world.
- **Chris's Mindgut board:** instrument precision, distinctive controls, software character, and personalization.
- **Chia Amisola's Instagram reference:** discovery through interaction; text and windows as places to explore; intimacy within a digital environment.

Study the mechanisms behind their appeal. Do not average their palettes or reproduce their surfaces.

The garden references are exploratory. **Flowers, roots, and growth are not the default explanation for memory.** Likewise, hardware references do not justify filling the interface with knobs, meters, or invented telemetry.

### Design requirements

**Identity**
Preserve the supplied Mindgut robot and wordmark while exploring their use. Develop recognizable composition, typography, motion, and material behavior around them. No tagline, palette, or proposed direction is settled.

**Visual craft**
Clean means disciplined hierarchy and readable working space. Allow concentrated richness where it earns attention. Use convincing materials, optical detail, and texture with a purpose. Color must have conviction; no cream fallback, sage substitute, or default muted purple.

If letters form an image, construct it deliberately through scale, density, layering, and movement. Avoid a one-pass ASCII filter. Avoid generic AI illustrations, abstract knots, and ornamental "memory" networks.

**UI and UX**
Make the conversation comfortable over long sessions and different kinds of work. Show source inspection, correction, context selection, and model switching through concrete interactions. Personalization should change the experience meaningfully, beyond recoloring a standard dashboard.

**HELIX**
Explore how people find, recognize, connect, and revisit material. Give spatial views an actual retrieval purpose. Make provenance, uncertainty, and changes understandable. A beautiful map that cannot help someone locate a thought is insufficient.

**Motion**
Choreograph attention and explain transitions: something returns, a relationship becomes visible, a selection carries forward. Design pacing, interruption, resting states, and reduced motion. Ambient movement alone is not an interaction concept.

**Website, social, advertising**
Express the same identity in compositions suited to each medium. Use specific situations and culturally aware placements. Show finished examples rather than lists of campaign ideas.

### Presentation standard

A confident visual sequence with sparse, necessary copy. Each section should make one clear contribution. Show the interface at usable scale, motion in context, and applications that demonstrate the system's range.

No internal instructions, delegation notes, meeting recaps, invented ownership, or self-congratulation. No wall of text explaining that the design is thoughtful.

### Working method and release bar

1. Revisit the references closely before designing.
2. Develop a coherent visual thesis and a small set of high-fidelity studies.
3. Resolve a hero composition, a working conversation, and a HELIX interaction before extending the system.
4. Critique what is actually rendered: distinctiveness, clarity, material quality, motion, and usefulness.
5. Refine and carry the resolved language through the presentation.
6. Inspect the complete result at desktop and mobile sizes. Resolve the viewing workflow before calling the work finished.

**Reject any pass that could belong to another AI product after changing the logo. Reject any visual whose explanation is more compelling than the visual itself.**
