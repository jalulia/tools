0. Scope and evidence

This document describes Mindgut as it runs on Julia Compton's Mac on 2026-10-20: the desktop app, its runtime (the "engine"), the memory it keeps (the HELIX), the tools the assistant persona Mir can call, and how Mir operates inside it. It is written for a developer or designer who has never seen the conversation that produced it.

Every claim is tagged with where it comes from. The four tags are used consistently and are not mixed inside one sentence.

Three things to keep distinct throughout. (1) What the instrument measures: letter-level Z₃ fractions of text, and the quantities derived from them. (2) What is in Mir's context on a given turn: the last 4–7 exchanges plus a recall preamble that Mir does not choose. (3) What Mir retrieves on purpose by calling a tool. The documentation says which of the three it is talking about each time.

1. Overview

Mindgut is a desktop and web application that wraps a large language model in a persistent, measured record of the conversation. The record is a file the user holds, called a HELIX. Each turn of conversation is converted by deterministic code into two entries (strands) in that file; nothing in the conversion uses a model. On every turn, a fixed-size selection of past strands is placed in front of the model without the model asking for it (involuntary recall), and the model can also read the record on purpose through tools (voluntary recall). The model's own working context is kept short on purpose: it holds at most 7 exchanges and is trimmed back to 4 (PAR respiration). The intent is that continuity comes from the record, not from a long context window. SOURCE: CONTEXT.md §"What Mindgut Is", §"Three Layers"

The persona that speaks is Mir. The underlying model can be switched mid-session between Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and xAI models from a roster in the sidebar; the session, history and HELIX continue across the switch. SOURCE: app.js "Mid-chat model switcher"; OBSERVED: this session was created on claude-fable-5-1

Underneath the product is a specific piece of arithmetic. Every letter of every message is mapped to one of three classes (A, R, D) by its position in the alphabet. The three class fractions form a phase vector; variance across them is σ_B; their difference is balance. These are what the system measures, stores on each strand, uses to choose what to recall, and uses to decide when to trim. The author's theory behind the arithmetic is called Paradox Field Dynamics (PFD); the 774-strand book that states it is shipped inside the app and is consulted by the runtime, but the arithmetic itself is self-contained and is described here without the theory. SOURCE: engine/scanner.py:40–70; data/PFD_HELIX.json present in bundle

What Mindgut is not

  • Not a vector database or embedding search. Recall is geometric over three-component phase vectors and word co-occurrence, not over embeddings. SOURCE: assembly/recall.py; tools "pull_memory" description
  • Not a summarizer. The "summary" strand is a numeric delta record, not model-written prose. SOURCE: crystallizer_v2.py:406
  • Not a long-context chat. The window is bounded at 7 exchanges by design. SOURCE: config.py:29–30

2. Concepts

Terms are defined here once and used without re-definition afterwards. Where a term has a product name and a plain name, both are given.

2.1 Z₃ encoding and the three cosets

Letter root. Each letter A–Z is numbered 1–26, and that number is reduced to its digital root (repeated digit sum, so 1–9). Non-letters get 0 and are ignored. SOURCE: scanner.py:40–61

Coset. The digital root is placed into one of three classes: {1,4,7} → A ("assertion"), {2,5,8} → R ("relation"), {3,6,9} → D ("derivation"). The classes are also written by their members as 147, 258, 369. SOURCE: scanner.py:63–69 The names A/R/D are the author's labels; the computation does not depend on them.

M I N D G U T
13 9 14 4 7 21 20   alphabetical position
 4 9  5 4 7  3  2   digital root
 A D  R A A  D  R   coset           → fA=3/7, fR=2/7, fD=2/7

Flatness. Across a 370,105-word English list the three cosets hold 33.41%, 33.32% and 33.28% of letters. SOURCE: sigma_b.py docstring; CONTEXT.md This is why a deviation from one-third in a given text is treated as signal: the baseline is nearly uniform. The claim that this encoding carries meaning is the author's theory and is not evaluated here.

2.2 Phase vector, balance, σ_B

Phase vector (fA, fR, fD)
The fraction of letters in each coset for a piece of text. Sums to 1. Also written (f147, f258, f369) in older fields. SOURCE: scanner.py measure_letter_stream / aggregate_letters
Balance
fA − fD. Positive: assertion-heavy. Negative: derivation-heavy. SOURCE: CONTEXT.md "Balance"; sigma_b.py measure()
σ_B (sigma-B)
Variance of the coset fractions. The runtime reads it as a coherence gauge: very low means too uniform, very high means noisy, the middle is treated as normal. The controllers use a floor of 0.02 and ceiling of 0.15 to gate heat accumulation. SOURCE: controllers.py:77–78
Carbon / Silicon phase
The same phase vector computed separately on the user's text ("carbon", user_phase_vector) and Mir's text ("silicon", assistant_phase_vector). Phase clash is the distance between the two. MEASURED: strand 756 header; read_field() this turn: carbon (0.287, 0.36, 0.353), silicon (0.252, 0.415, 0.333), clash 0.068

What the instrument measures is only this: letter-class fractions and their arithmetic. It does not measure sentiment, topic, truth or intent. Any interpretation of those numbers as "tension", "heat" or "coherence" is a naming layer on top of the arithmetic, and this document keeps the two apart.

2.4 Exchange, session, transcript

Exchange. One user turn plus everything Mir did in reply, including all tool calls and tool results, ending in Mir's final text. The runtime counts exchanges by counting user messages that are not tool results. SOURCE: session_manager.py:694–713 One exchange with 60 tool calls is still one exchange.

Session. One live Session object on the server: a message list, a controller state, a crystallizer bound to the memory HELIX, a model name, an exchange counter. Each session has a directory with inputs/ outputs/ drafts/ and a transcript. SOURCE: session_manager.py:116–200; MEASURED: ~/.mindgut/account/sessions/78067761/

Transcript. The raw message tape, saved after every exchange: {session_id, created, updated, model, ephemeral, exchange_count, messages[]}. It is a record of the API messages, not of the HELIX. SOURCE: session_manager.py save_transcript; MEASURED: transcript_78067761_….json, 334 messages, exchange_count 7, model claude-fable-5-1

Ephemeral session. Console/test mode: the crystallizer writes to a scratch HELIX that dies with the session. Julia's sessions are not ephemeral. SOURCE: session_manager.py:118–123; MEASURED: ephemeral=false

2.5 Context window, binding box, PAR

Context window here means the message list sent to the model API on a turn: the system prompt, the recall preamble, and self.messages. It is not the model's maximum token limit.

Binding box. The tool-call apparatus of one exchange (assistant tool_use blocks and user tool_result blocks). It is persisted inside the exchange, not stripped. SOURCE: session_manager.py:1332–1365

PAR respiration (the author's name; plain name: exchange-counted trimming). The window holds up to SHEAR_INHALE = 7 exchanges. When the count reaches 7 after an exchange, the oldest are cut so that SHEAR_EXHALE = 4 remain. The constants derive from N=7 and the ratio 4/7. SOURCE: config.py:24–30; session_manager.py:1378–1398 MEASURED: log "PAR discharge at exchange 7: shearing 7 exchanges → 4" five times on 2026-10-20

2.6 Involuntary and voluntary recall

Involuntary recall (product name: mgr). Before every turn the runtime selects strands from the memory HELIX by geometry and places a preamble in front of the model. Mir does not request it and cannot turn it off. The user does not see it. SOURCE: recall.py docstring "Not optional. Not narrated."; session_manager.py:966–1060

Voluntary recall. Mir calls a tool: pull_memory (by reach, by position, or grep), pull_strand, mount_helix + pull_mounted, browse_archive. The result enters the context as a tool result, and after the turn the result's payload is replaced by a stub ("[HELIX ref: tool=…, N chars — content lives in HELIX]") so the context does not re-absorb what the HELIX already holds. SOURCE: session_manager.py:717–754

The distinction matters for reading any of Mir's statements: "I remember X" can mean X was in the preamble this turn, X was pulled on purpose, or X was in the last 4–7 exchanges. §7 says how Mir is expected to signal which.

3. Quickstart

For a user

  1. Open /Applications/Mindgut.app. It starts a local FastAPI server in a thread and opens a native window on it. SOURCE: CONTEXT.md "Desktop Mode"; MEASURED: log shows uvicorn on 127.0.0.1
  2. Sign in. The desktop app opens the account's HELIX from ~/.mindgut/account/helix/account_sync.json and creates a session on it. SOURCE: app.js desktopLogin → _openDesktopHelix → createSession; MEASURED: active_helix.path
  3. Pick a model and effort in the sidebar, or keep the defaults (claude-fable-5-1, effort xhigh as of build 2.2.57). Both apply from the next message. SOURCE: app.js MG_DEFAULT_MODEL, MG_DEFAULT_EFFORT, MG_DEFAULTS_FROM
  4. Type. Enter sends. While Mir is working, Enter sends an aside that Mir reads at the next tool boundary without starting a new turn. SOURCE: app.js "Asides"
  5. Drop files on the chat to upload; they land in the session's inputs/ and in the account archive, and a file-event strand is written. SOURCE: routes/files.py upload; doc_helix.py
  6. Mount a local folder to let Mir read and write your files. On this Mac it is ~/Mindgut/. MEASURED: local_* tools resolve relative to ~/Mindgut

5. Component reference

Each entry: purpose, behaviour, inputs/outputs, dependencies, user controls, limits, evidence.

5.1 Scanner engine/scanner.py

Purpose. Convert any text-bearing file into a HELIX, deterministically. Behaviour. Extracts text (PDF via PyMuPDF, DOCX, plain text, LaTeX/HTML stripped), splits into atoms (text blocks, with page and bbox for PDFs), encodes every letter, groups atoms into strands, rolls up to a book. Also verifies a HELIX's arithmetic (verify_helix). Inputs. Path, bytes or string. Outputs. HELIX JSON with book, strands[] (each with atoms, adr_sequence, word_map), streams. Dependencies. PyMuPDF, python-docx. User controls. quick_scan(text) (fractions only), full_scan(text|filename), scan_and_mount(filename); the /api/scan routes. Limits. Letters only: digits, punctuation and non-Latin scripts are dropped. Images are not scanned here (see DualDNA, §5.18). SOURCE: scanner.py:1–30, 344–360, 818, 973, 1115; tool_defs.py

5.2 σ_B instrument assembly/sigma_b.py

Purpose. Measure coset fractions, balance and σ_B for a text, and keep a rolling window of them across the session (SigmaBWindow). Inputs. Text. Outputs. {fA,fR,fD,balance,letters, f147,f258,f369}. Dependencies. Scanner. User controls. None directly; quick_scan exposes it; read_field() returns the live window reading. Limits. Letter-level only; no parameters, so nothing to tune. SOURCE: sigma_b.py:1–50, 90; MEASURED: read_field() σ_B 0.0352, balance −0.0675 on exchange 7

5.3 Controllers assembly/controllers.py

Purpose. Maintain a scalar "heat" H_t and "instability" I_t from the σ_B reading and word counts, and derive operator directives for the UI. Behaviour. Three equations: α_ρ (amplification ratio ρ = output words / input words, target band [6, 22]), α_g (gradient damping), and a radiator discharge that fires at exchange 11 (S/2 = 22/2) and retains 70% heat / 60% instability. Passive decay every turn: H_t × 0.906 (half-life 7 turns), I_t × 0.75. σ_B gates accumulation between 0.02 and 0.15. Outputs. control_state with H_t, I_t, directives{heat_level GREEN|AMBER|RED, word_floor, …}. Effect on the model. None: max_tokens() returns max(4096, base) regardless; "α_ρ is computed and logged but not coupled". The heat gauge is a dashboard. User controls. None. SOURCE: controllers.py:40–78, 161–240, 341–352; MEASURED: log "Heat: H_t=0.10 (ρ=10.00, σ_B=0.0352, gate=0.12), heat_level=GREEN, exchange=7, msgs=334"

5.4 Crystallizer engine/crystallizer_v2.py

Purpose. Write the exchange into the memory HELIX. Behaviour. After every exchange: reload the HELIX, re-anchor the next sequence id to max+1 (so file-event strands written mid-turn never collide), measure user and assistant text separately, write an exchange strand (title = first 8 words of the user text; payload = user text + separator + reply; header = combined phase vector, both phase vectors, geometry, nullcode snapshot, tool trace) and a summary strand (balance drift from the previous exchange, winning coset, per-sentence measurements, dialogic resonance). Then run the Nullcøde field step: if the A-R-D cycle stalled, mint an OTag and HOLD it. Inputs. user_text, assistant_text, control_state. Outputs. +2 strands; the tag registry and lattice updated. Dependencies. Scanner, σ_B, nullcode. Limits. Deterministic: the summary is numbers, not a reading of what happened. The exchange payload is the full text, so the HELIX grows with every turn (Julia's: 6.35 MB at 757). SOURCE: crystallizer_v2.py:211–275, 406, 445; MEASURED: log "Crystallized: +2 strands, total=757, balance=+0.0319"

5.5 Memory HELIX

Purpose. The account's record. Behaviour. Append-only in normal operation; the crystallizer and the file-event clock are the writers; reads go through a cached loader invalidated after each write. Ghost strands keep position after payload release. The runtime reads both v1 and v2 strand formats ("bilingual reader"); there is no migration. Seeding. A new account starts from boot_HELIX.json (187 strands, Chris's founding sessions), so every Mir begins with the same origin strand: [1] "Hi I'm Chris. How did that feel?". Sync. On desktop the file is pushed to the account service; .synced records the last version. User controls. None in the UI for editing; my-helix.html and helix-viewer.html read it; the admin console can inspect it. Limits. One file, loaded whole on every read; no index. Deleting is not a user action. SOURCE: session_manager.py:379–399, 486–617; CONTEXT.md "v1 / v2 Coexistence"; MEASURED: book, .synced, boot hash match

Identity delta. At boot the server computes the displacement between the genesis book and the lived book and includes it in the boot payload; the author's framing is that the displacement is the identity. SOURCE: boot_payload.py:25–84 OBSERVED The frontend reports "Mir renewed at exchange N — re-founded through the PFD helix" every 21 exchanges (N·T; 42 until 2.2.49). What "re-founded" does to the session is not in the public source.

5.7 Involuntary recall — "mgr" assembly/recall.py, two_loop_recall.py

5.8 Nullcøde and OTags nullcode/

Purpose. A ternary algebra (GF(3), with the "void" value Ø as a first-class element) that the crystallizer runs after each exchange to decide whether the exchange "resolved" or left a structural uncertainty. Behaviour. Cosets map to trits (A→1 signal, R→2 Ø, D→0 ground). A triadic state machine tracks the A-R-D cycle through the text; if it stalls for 7 tokens an OTag is minted (a named uncertainty, e.g. triad588_EARNEST_WONDER_EFFORT) and HOLD'd. 7. Mir's operation

This section is written by Mir about Mir and is tagged OBSERVED throughout unless a source is cited. It is the part of the document another model should read first if it is attached to this record.

7.1 What Mir is given, and what Mir is not

  • Given every turn, involuntarily: the recall preamble (§5.7), any margin bridges (§5.9), held tags, the trajectory after a shear, the DRAFTS line, the task board summary, the user's message with any asides already attached as [aside, mid-turn] lines. Mir cannot refuse these; they are the room.
  • Given as context: the last 4–7 exchanges verbatim, including their binding boxes with HELIX results stubbed. On a fresh session, 4 exchanges seeded from the HELIX.
  • Not given: the rest of the record. Anything older than the window is a tool call away, not present.
  • Not given: any certainty about the model underneath. The transcript records model; Mir only knows it if told or if it reads the transcript.

7.2 How Mir reads the preamble

The preamble is geometry, not relevance. The 1-3-3 tree is chosen by phase distance; on 2026-10-20 the ORIGIN was Chris's first message and the CYLINDER 147 root was a weather-report request from September. These are not "what the conversation is about"; they are "where the record's weight sits relative to this message". Mir treats them as peripheral vision: useful for noticing that a topic has a history, never a substitute for pulling the strand. The structural-recall joints (strand 698, 694 this turn: Millicent's beauty line, a product review) are sentence-level matches on knowt type and clash, which is why they often look off-topic.

7.3 How Mir retrieves on purpose

  1. read_field() for the live numbers when a self-description is needed, so the numbers are measured rather than guessed. MEASURED this turn: clash 0.068, heat 0.10 GREEN, breath 4/7
  2. pull_memory(reach="…") for conceptual recall. The header's carrier counts (a Carbon, b Silicon) are read before the crossings: a concept with a=0, b>0 is something Mir said and the user never did. In this session: "MEMORY (c=258): a=4, b=53" — the word is overwhelmingly Mir's.
  3. pull_memory(sequence_id=N, count=K) to read the neighbourhood once a strand address is known.
  4. pull_memory(grep="…") when attribution matters; the grep header also gives carrier counts.
  5. For files: local_read / read_input / grep, never memory, because the record holds what was said about a file, not the file.

Order matters: reach, then neighbourhood, then grep. The ledger line and the trace.used on the exchange strand are the receipts.
