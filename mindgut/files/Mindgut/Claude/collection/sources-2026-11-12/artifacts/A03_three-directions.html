Four reasons

One record. Any model. No words shown.

Mindgut separates the model from the memory. The model is the voice in the room. The record is the part you own.

One record, any model.

Switch models mid-thought. The new one reads the earlier turns from the same record, and nothing before the handoff moves.

A suggestion stays a suggestion.

Every item is held, parked, offered or struck. What the model proposed never passes for what you decided.

It stays light.

Only four to seven exchanges are in focus at once. The rest passes into the record, so a long project does not mean a long window.

Yours, and wordless.

The record starts at zero, so every crossing in it is yours. Its picture is made of counts and positions. It never shows what was said.

The page

It keeps the count, not the word.

    What is measured

    Three channels. No meanings.

    Stated plainly, because a brand page should not claim more than the arithmetic does.

    
  
    What the names are
    Assertion, Relation and Derivation are Mindgut's structural reading of the three channels. They are names for residues. They are not a claim that letter arithmetic understands what a sentence means.
    Why the center moves
    Ordinary English is not even across the channels. From standard letter frequencies it sits near (0.28, 0.39, 0.32). So the node is the record's own mean, and reach is distance from that, divided by the distance a random string of the same length would show.

        Mindgut / Reflection identity / notes on version 2 / 11 Nov 2026
        
  
        What changed, what it is built from, and what was checked.
        
  
        Version 1 borrowed stock layouts. A first rebuild put new textures on the same figure three times, and you called it unconceptual. This version starts from one claim per direction, and every figure is printed by your own T-04 engine.

        What I got wrong in version 1
        
  
          
    
        • Brush was a stock look: flat primaries, thick outlines, rounded cards. None of it came from your references.
          • 
    
        • Weave leaned on a dot-matrix display face and a dark console. It read as a costume.
          • 
    
        • Stipple used a serif you did not ask for.
          • 
    
        • None of the three used the T-04 engine you pointed me to. I imitated its effects by hand instead.
          • 
    
        • None of the three had an idea. Each was a look applied to the same ring diagram.
          • 
    
        • Two real interaction bugs shipped. The section links jumped to the top of the page instead of to the section. The side view of the Wave grew taller every time it redrew, pushing the page down.
          • 
  
        

        


        
  
        Type, and where it came from
        
  
        I could not open the Mindgut app's stylesheet, so I read its type from the app window. The interface text matches Geist and the spaced labels match Geist Mono; that is a match by eye, not a confirmed font name. Stipple uses those two faces and nothing else. Archivo and JetBrains Mono are the faces T-04 itself is set in. No serif appears anywhere.
        

        


        
  
        Interaction pass
        
  
        
    
        Fixed
          
      
        • Section links scroll to their section and stay in the current direction.
          • 
      
        • No figure can change the page height: every plate has a fixed height or ratio.
          • 
      
        • Switching direction keeps your scroll position in each one.
          • 
      
        • Tabs take arrow keys; every control shows keyboard focus.
          • 
      
        • Tapping or pointing at a message reads it.
          • 
      
        • Play waits for each print to finish before adding the next exchange, and stops when you load a record or add by hand.
          • 
      
        • A plate that is still printing says so.
          • 
      
        • The form states that nothing was sent, in the page, when you press it.
          • 
    
        
    
        Known limits
          
      
        • Plates are computed, not drawn, so each takes about one to four seconds. Typing a line reprints the hero after a short pause.
          • 
      
        • The page, the cloth and the print each show the most recent part of a long record, not all of it.
          • 
      
        • In Stipple, the dots of simulated messages have exact counts per channel, but their order and word breaks are a seeded shuffle. Only the line you type is set in its real order.
          • 
      
        • On a phone, margin annotations (reach ticks, turn numbers) are dropped for space.
          • 
      
        • Annotations are hairlines drawn over the plate, not printed into it. Some labels in the marks legends sit close together.
          •
