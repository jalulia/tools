# fidelity.md — craft standard

**Version** V01 · 2026-10-19 · Consolidated from: creative-hifi-frontend, composing-computational-material-systems, material-systems-direction, frontend-design, brand-world, headless-render, proof-cleanup-upscale skills; Julia's encyclopedia (`jalulia.github.io/tools`) chrome and style contracts; the Ø Operating Spec, Impossible Brand Toolkit, Techniques & Templates, Ki, Mode Mode, Mad Money Tycoon, WØRKHUB and Coffee/POP sessions; the Mindgut brief. Rules are stated once, as rules. The machine-readable form is §14.

The bar is high. A pass that would survive with another company's logo is a fail. A pass whose explanation is better than its picture is a fail.

---

## 0 · The bar

1. **Logo-swap test.** If the work could belong to another product after changing the logo, reject it.
2. **Explanation test.** If the explanation is more compelling than the visual, reject the visual.
3. **Removal test.** For every layer, operator, texture, motion and word: what specifically collapses if it is removed? "Less cool" is not an answer. Cut it.
4. **Render test.** Nothing is judged from code. Screenshot it, look, list faults per element, patch, re-render. Two full passes minimum; three is normal.
5. **Recognition test.** Would the reference's designer recognize their piece? Would Julia recognize her thinking?
6. **Design is of the content.** No house style. Type, color, layout and motion are decided from the content every time. The calibration references in §12 are bars to clear, not defaults to apply.
7. **Show, don't tell.** A working proof beats a description of one. Feelings come from behavior, composition and detail, never from adjectives.

## 2 · Material systems (computed pixels)

### 2.1 Stack vs system
A **stack** is effects placed beside each other. A **system** is operations wired to shared causes, doing distinct jobs, resolving in a load-bearing order, read as one thing. **Compound causality, singular read.** Fewer parts, more coherence. Access to effects is one-liners now; the scarce skill is why an operator exists, what drives it, what it separates, when it stops.

### 2.2 Primary read first
One sentence naming what the viewer encounters — the material condition, not the techniques. "A single blue optical body you look through," not "transmission + distortion + absorption." No sentence → no idea → do not touch parameters. "Immersive," "ethereal," "dynamic," "cinematic" are not reads.

### 2.3 The five tests
1. **Shared cause** — ≥2 visible consequences derive from one field/buffer/event (depth → blur + atmosphere + absorption + scale; velocity → form + grain direction + streak + drift; emission → bloom + exposure; pointer/scroll/audio → amplitude of the coupled set).
2. **Distinct jobs** — each operator does something no other does. Three operators all "making it moodier" is one job three times; cut two.
3. **Order dependence** — reordering changes the result, or the operators are adjacent, not composed.
4. **Single read** — one proposition, not a demo reel.
5. **Removal** — each layer names what collapses without it.

### 2.4 Seven layers (skip the ones not in play, and say so)
SOURCE (coords, image, geometry, depth, normals, velocity, audio, pointer, scroll, text, time) → STRUCTURE (shaping functions, thresholds, SDFs, domain warp, repetition, cellular, fBM) → MATERIAL RESPONSE (transmission, absorption, reflection, emission, roughness, density, relief) → IMAGE FORMATION (camera, depth, one key light, exposure, focal hierarchy) → SCREEN-SPACE (blur, bloom, edges, quantize, dither, grain, aberration, remaps, grade) → TEMPORAL (state, envelope, feedback, cadence, hold, rest) → GRAPHIC COMPOSITION (type, mark, air, framing).
Production constraints (budget, DPR, fallback, reduced motion, legibility) apply across the chain, not as a final QA step.

### 2.5 Field grammar
- Everything is `f(position, time) → value`. The shape of the function is the design.
- Vocabulary: `step`, `smoothstep(a,b,x)` (edge softness lives in `b−a`), `pow` (response curve), `fract`/`mod` (repetition), `abs`/`sign` (fold), `sin`/`cos`, `exp`/`log` (falloff, absorption), `min`/`max`/`clamp` (SDF booleans), `mix`.
- An edge is where a continuous value crosses a level: `smoothstep(t−w, t+w, field)`. Tie `w` to pixel footprint (`fwidth`) for resolution-stable edges.
- Domain choice is creative (0..1, centered −1..1, polar, world). Change domain before shaping.
- Domain warp `uv += noise(uv)` is the single most important organic move. Warping input ≠ distorting output.
- Noise is a **source of structure that drives something**, never a grain overlay for busyness. `fbm(uv + fbm(uv))` is the workhorse for living fields; octaves = detail/cost, lacunarity/gain = character.
- Keep shaping and coloring as separate stages. The brightest, most saturated point of the ramp is the focal point — place it on purpose.

### 2.6 Material before post
Decide what the structure is made of before reaching for screen-space. Transmission needs a background to relate to; absorption accumulates color through thickness (Beer–Lambert) — "blue glass," not "a blue rectangle"; one sharp specular sells a material better than any bloom; emission is the only legitimate driver of bloom; varying roughness separates depth better than uniform blur; a normal/relief pass is what makes refraction read as a surface instead of a UV wobble.
**Fluted-glass test:** sine-displaced UVs = filter. Refracted UVs across flutes + relief shading + a hair of chromatic separation + one advected field driving curvature, strength, blur radius, absorption and drift = body.
**NPR is legitimate** when stylized marks derive from measured scene quantities (depth, normals, luminance, curvature) — "measure physically, render expressively."

### 2.7 Pass order (orders that change the image)
- Dither **before** quantize (perturb then snap hides banding). Quantize-then-dither is noise on bands.
- Refraction at the **sampling** stage; stretch transitions may live at the **composite** stage. Decide which.
- Geometry/UV distortion → material/lighting → screen-space (blur/bloom/edges) → tonal grade → grain/dither **last**.
- Extract the bloom mask **before** any scene blur.
- Edge-detect on clean depth/normal buffers **before** posterizing the fill.
State the order and the reason in every build note. No reason = finding.

### 2.8 Color space
Light math in linear, display in sRGB. Gradients and mixes in OKLab/OKLCH (midpoints stay bright and on-hue; a brand ramp with a bridge color is interpolated in OKLab so the bridge reads). Premultiplied alpha for transparent/emissive compositing. Most "muddy gradient / dead midtone" complaints are color-space errors, not taste.

### 2.9 Operators → job → driver → overuse
| Operator | Job | Driver | Overuse tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian blur | push back, reduce acuity | depth/importance field | whole-frame blur flattens; softness reads as depth only if something stays sharp |
| Disk/bokeh blur | plausible defocus with shaped highlights | circle of confusion from depth | heavy bokeh on a flat scene = camera cosplay |
| Progressive/directional blur | sharp→soft along an axis (honest frosted header) | position gradient | dreamy wash with no axis |
| Radial/zoom blur | rush toward/away | focal center + energy | instant "epic" |
| Fluted refraction | glass body with relief | field for flute direction/spacing | sine wobble without relief |
| Polar / kaleidoscope / mirror | remap structure into tunnels, mandalas, doubling | structure worth folding | folds nothing into nothing; six-fold looks designed for one second |
| Stretch/squeeze | motion streak, elastic transition | velocity/anchor | elastic everything |
| Bloom | expand **emitted** light | emissive mask | global bloom on midtones = haze |
| Exposure/tone map | HDR→display, roll off highlights | scene luminance; once, late, right space | crushing to fake contrast |
| Vignette | steer inward | focal center (not geometric middle) | dark ring instead of hierarchy |
| Quantize/posterize | palette reduction, print/retro foundation | palette + level count | banding without dither |
| Dither | trade spatial for tonal resolution | threshold pattern, coordinated with quantize | grit with nothing behind it |
| Halftone / CRT / pixel grid | impose a medium | cell size vs content | nostalgia with no reason |
| Grain | unify, tactile surface, break banding | low, luminance-aware, gated to body | crutch; static grain over motion |
| Chromatic aberration | lens character at edges | distance from center / refraction strength | RGB split everywhere |
| Edge detect | ink/outline NPR | depth+normal+luma buffers | outlining everything |
| Color grade chain | last 10% | constant or slow field | three ops all "moodier" |

### 2.10 Reduce before finalizing
1. Remove anything whose only defense is richness, polish, atmosphere or trend. 2. Merge operations doing the same perceptual job. 3. Protect the read, the focal structure and the causal links while simplifying implementation. Do not add an operator because a library makes it convenient. A single well-shaped field may be enough.

## 3 · Surface and print physics (generated, never faked)

### 3.1 Non-negotiables
1. **No boilerplate drop shadows.** `0 20px 60px rgba(0,0,0,.5)` is a tell. Shadows = contact + short directional ambient, one light angle per scene, tinted by the receiving surface (cool on lavender, warm on kraft); occlusion only where geometry causes it.
2. **No gradient standing in for a photograph.** Draw the scene in grayscale, then reproduce it through a print process (halftone, dither, duotone, grain).
3. **No vector clip-art hardware.** Metal is brushed per-pixel on canvas (environment banding + micro-streak grain + cast shadow + pressure darkening on the paper).
4. **No unmasked type that should be cropped.** Oversize inside `overflow:hidden`; crop on the axes the reference crops.
5. **No effect without a cause.** One toner-coverage noise punches every ink; one line screen renders the photo and the ink-starved letter; one light direction casts every shadow. Static artifacts get static filters — a print does not animate.
6. **A screen specimen gets no fake grain.** Grain fakes a print that never happened. Grain belongs to a reproduction process or to a body, never to chrome.
7. **Hand-drawn marks read as crisp, nuanced, natural ink.** Displacement-wobble ink is weak. Ink is a filled spine-and-width stroke; weight loads where forms crowd; no shadow — nuance from construction, not filters.
8. **No decorative crop marks, registration affectations, or ornament without a job.**

### 3.2 Engines (full recipes in §13)
Grain-gradient fields (paint at 1/8–1/10 scale, upscale — the upscale **is** the blur — then per-pixel grain; bodies are rotated elliptical **belts**, never chains of blobs); Bayer-8 ordered dither with ramps and fBm ridgelines; procedural photographs (leaf litter via 2-octave light field, drawer/architecture scenes with chunky value structure) run through a 45° dot screen or duotone; luminance-melt type; spectral ribbons (faceted hue keyframes + diagonal refraction offset, grain ≤ 7); bokeh interiors; one film overlay above the type layer so field and type share one emulsion; paper tooth tile (multiply, ~.5) so ink sits **in** the paper.

### 3.3 Print physics (SVG filters, defined once)
- **Ink chain** = feed-jitter displacement → toner pinhole punch. Calibrate for sparse **pinpricks** (threshold ×0.74–0.78, hole frequency 0.35–0.55). Lower thresholds = moth-eaten distressed font = wrong.
- Tool signatures with their own seeds: `brushInk` (wet wobble + dry gaps), `stampInk` (starved rubber stamp; borders break correctly), `markerInk` (waver only; markers don't starve).
- **Riso misregistration** is a second **run**: own seed, sloppier scale, offset ~6–7px/4–5px, rotate ~.4–.5°, **under** the main pass so it surfaces through pinholes. Never a text-shadow copy.
- **Ink-starved letterforms**: pattern fill at the **same pitch** as nearby halftone (the coupling is the point) + ink chain + a solid squeegee catch at the baseline.
- **Blind emboss/deboss**: text in exactly the substrate color; opposing light/dark 1px shadows.
- Static seeds for printed artifacts; animate the seed only for live/photocopy-shiver contexts, and scale displacement with viewport.

### 3.4 Layer doctrine
**Press layer** (display type, imagery) ← ink chain. **Hand layer** (marker ovals, arrows, bubbles, signatures) ← crisp or markerInk only, always outside the press filters. **Object layer** (stickers, tape, clips, plastic) ← crisp + physical shadow; vinyl never takes print filters. Annotations are siblings, not children, of filtered elements.

### 3.5 Hardware vocabulary (vary it — one punch style everywhere is a tell)
Standard binder holes (stage shows through, 1px bottom lip) · planner micro-punch · tractor-feed strips with perforation rule · blind perforation (nearly imperceptible) · torn deckle (clip-path, ~46 jittered points) · die-line notches · stab-binding stitches · kiss-cut stickers (white halo stroke under fill, optional peel corner) · plastic sleeve (rgba body ~.045, 1.4px border, sheen polygons, turbulence-displaced wrinkles, faint veil over contents).

### 3.6 Shadow systems
- Dark stage: `0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,.08) inset, 2px 3px 5px rgba(0,0,0,.62), 8px 14px 30px rgba(0,0,0,.36)`
- Light stage: `0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,.35) inset, 0 1px 2px rgba(35,35,52,.30), 2px 4px 9px rgba(35,35,52,.18)`
- Flat/papercut: `0 1px 1.5px rgba(0,0,0,.5), 5px 7px 0 rgba(0,0,0,.22)` → `10px 14px 0` while dragged
- Occlusion strips are narrow linear gradients placed where the geometry is, plus a 1px lit edge on the occluder. Light stages read better as a radial falloff panel than a flat fill.

### 3.7 Finishing raster proofs (print)
Two-stage loop: clean at working resolution (two light passes beat one aggressive one), upscale with a detail-preserving method to 300 ppi at the target physical size, clean again at full size. Guardrails: text byte-for-byte identical (non-Latin glyphs especially), colors unchanged, composition unchanged, sharp at 100%. "Restore"/"reimagine" prompts redraw type — never use them.

## 4 · Typography

1. Type is a layout element, not a label on a layout: justified vertical columns (`textLength` + `lengthAdjust="spacingAndGlyphs"`), edge-to-edge headlines (characters flexed `space-between`), arcs on shallow `textPath` (leave headroom for diacritics — viewBoxes clip accents), cropped display type, mirrored wordmarks, glow stacks sharing one constant across lines.
2. If letters form an image, construct it deliberately through scale, density, layering and movement. Never a one-pass ASCII filter.
3. Type follows structure or it decorates. Place type on the routes, hinges and open regions the field creates. If it could sit anywhere, it is decoration. Sample type color from the image or use the one accent; never a hue the field does not contain. Do not warp type just because the image is warped.
4. Roles: **serif = what a person wrote; mono = what the machine reports; sans does the work.** Mono only for actual code, readouts, parameters, machine labels — never as a decorative label face (mono is overused). Serif as an accent for numbers and math variables when the sans carries everything else.
5. No default-generic faces as a reflex (Inter, Roboto, Arial, system stacks, Space Grotesk). Faces are chosen for the content; all are self-hosted woff2 with real fallback stacks matched for width so nothing reflows.
6. Leading and tracking are confident and declared. Display tracking tight; small caps/kickers in mono at a size that still passes 4.5:1.
7. The brand mark is a **signature** (~34px class), never a headline. Clear space and opacity rules are hard constraints.
8. Hairline rule: text never runs up to a 1px rule; symmetric horizontal padding in every cell of a hairline grid.
9. Small functional text never enters a degradation filter; legibility outranks texture.
10. Copy is sparse, plain and direct. No LLM speak, no filler, no cute/obscure/verbose, no self-congratulation, no "the design is thoughtful." Prose that explains belongs in a machine-readable appendix, not on the page.

## 5 · Color

1. **Conviction.** Dominant color with a sharp accent beats an evenly distributed, timid palette. No cream fallback, no sage substitute, no default muted purple, no purple-on-white gradient.
2. **Work in ramps, not hexes.** Interpolate in OKLab (midpoint L* ≈ 0.55 for a two-pole ramp). Connective stops are derived in OKLab from the poles, not picked.
3. **Fields open and close on black** (or the declared ground) so an accent reads as a flare, not a rainbow band. Five equal segments, ~0.75 handover, no hue shoulder was the measured io hero contract — copy measured constants, do not re-derive them.
4. **Chrome is silent.** A single neutral ink ramp; no hue in chrome; the one accent means "interactive." A neutral bench (mat) is the viewing condition for a color specimen — the specimen is the only color on the sheet.
5. **Contrast floors.** Text ≥ 4.5:1 (ink-3 class ≈ 4.6:1 is the floor for anything that is a word; lighter values are non-text only: ticks, spines, hatch). Small white type on a bright blue fails — move to the darker sibling.
6. **Never encode meaning in color alone**, especially across a spectrum doing expressive work. Palette values can change between themes; category identity and label wording cannot.
7. Rejected palettes on record: "basic bright klein + electric orange"; "dusty corpo blue + pastel peach"; "grad-size 0.88 all-handover" fields that wander pale stops to center. Black is ground and ink, white is page, energy colors at full strength; pastels are connective stops only, never surfaces or type.

## 6 · Layout and composition

1. **Clean = disciplined hierarchy and readable working space.** Concentrated richness where it earns attention. Airy columns and hairline dividers over heavy bordered boxes. No aggressive grid paper; textures kept very light.
2. **Negative space is a decision.** Substantial air (~40%+) lets the focal event read. Filling the frame is the habit; leaving it is the craft.
3. **One focal event, one signal.** Align the cues (brightest, sharpest, most saturated, highest contrast) on one place, or stage a deliberate primary/secondary. Disagreeing cues = busy with no subject.
4. **Forced-light contract** for documents and tools: paper and bench are the only grounds; the page never goes dark, only the specimen may; stage ground declared, never inherited; focus rings by contrast, not hue; the hatch is the one ornamental gesture and it marks an admission of failure.
5. **Fixed rail / side nav** for long documents; entry identity in the header (kicker + title); no expository lede; related-entries block over redundant title blocks; track log stays beside the plate.
6. **Plates and specimens at 1:1.** The rail width is derived so the mat interior lands at true size; a "shown 0.96×" drawdown is a bug.
7. **Hairline grids**: `gap:1px` on a line color, symmetric cell padding, exact 1px rules, integer tracks.
8. **Frame is not content.** Model the design register (how it looks) separately from what it is.
9. **Interface at usable scale** in any presentation; motion shown in context; applications that demonstrate range, not lists of ideas.
10. **Every displayed quantity** carries measure, unit, period, population and source. A neat label can still make a false claim. A dashed line has one meaning per view. Use redundant encodings (shape + label + color), never color alone.
11. **No wireframe-level screens passing as design.** No copy-heavy sections. Visual first; text only where a picture cannot do the job.

## 7 · Motion

1. **Motion is composition.** What is the subject of the change, and what holds still? Everything moving = nothing reads.
2. **Envelope, not loop.** Specify attack / hold / decay / rest. "Builds ~2s, holds ~1s, decays ~4s into dark, rests" is a composition; "loops" is not. A hard cut reads as a loop seam.
3. **Two speeds.** Material/field drifts slowly and continuously (multi-second cycles); interface transitions are precise and short (120–400ms, eased, no spring, no bounce). Never mix: no bouncy material, no drifting UI.
4. **State vs animation.** `f(t)` for ambient reversible motion; carried state (ping-pong buffer / compute) for anything that accumulates, remembers or evolves — feedback, advection, erosion. Mind decay factors and reset.
5. **Interaction modulates the shared driver** so the whole body responds, not one bolted-on parallax layer. One master intensity, not a dashboard of sliders.
6. **Cadence.** A very slow envelope under fast motion so ambient pieces don't read as a 2-second GIF.
7. **Reduced motion is authored.** Freeze the field at the composed frame; long-running motion is pausable; essential information never depends on motion. Designing the still frame usually improves the whole piece.
8. **Choreograph attention and explain transitions**: something returns, a relationship becomes visible, a selection carries forward. Ambient movement alone is not an interaction concept.

## 8 · Interface and product fidelity (Mindgut)

1. Comfortable over long sessions and across kinds of work: generous measure, stable chrome, quiet ground, no telemetry theater.
2. Show source inspection, correction, context selection and model switching as **concrete interactions**, each at usable scale.
3. Personalization changes the experience meaningfully — not a recolored dashboard.
4. HELIX: spatial views must help someone **locate a thought**. Provenance, uncertainty and change are visible states with their own encodings. A beautiful map that cannot retrieve is insufficient.
5. Proposed interactions must remain distinguishable from existing functionality; capability claims need technical confirmation before they appear as behavior.
6. Flowers, roots and growth are not the default explanation for memory. Hardware references do not justify knobs, meters or invented telemetry. No generic AI illustration, abstract knots, or ornamental "memory networks."
7. Preserve the supplied robot and wordmark; develop composition, typography, motion and material behavior around them. Nothing (tagline, palette, direction) is settled until it is logged as a decision.
8. Views change emphasis, not geography: selection, zoom, interval and open document persist across views. Two views may be compared; four bright flow fields is a screensaver.
9. Visibility and evidence confidence are different variables; a disputed item gets an explicit status, never visual corruption.

## 9 · Front-end engineering

1. **Single self-contained HTML** per piece: markup, styles, script and fonts in one file; no fetch, no CDN, no `contentDocument` tricks. Measured constants named at the top.
2. **Canvas sizing from explicit pixel dimensions** passed in; never `getBoundingClientRect` for canvas size (host timing yields blank canvases). Paint at 1.5–2× for retina; clamp DPR ≤ 2.
3. **`innerHTML +=` destroys appended canvases.** Set static HTML first, then `appendChild` canvases.
4. **Rebuild on resize**: every plate reruns its builder; debounce ~300ms, threshold ~70px. All geometry derives from a scale unit `u = W / baseWidth`.
5. **Studies are `<section>`s** with ids (screenshot harnesses target them); `data-shot` isolates figures.
6. **Interactive paper**: pointer capture, `left/top` deltas (preserves rotation), z-bump on pickup, deeper shadow while lifted, `touch-action:none`, `user-select:none`.
7. **Fonts**: self-hosted woff2, subset, `font-display:swap`, fallback stacks width-matched. A fragment declares its own faces; undefined `var(--f-*)` renders the wrong face with no console error.
8. **Color scheme**: `color-scheme:light` on `:root`, `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">`, explicit `background` on `html` and `body`. Dark-by-design pieces declare `dark` and set every background explicitly. Never rely on propagation (transparent backgrounds render as dark canvas on iOS).
9. **WebGL/WebGPU**: feature-detect; WebGL2 fallback; below that a composed still that preserves the read. Headless rendering under SwiftShader needs `--use-gl=angle --use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader --ignore-gpu-blocklist`.
10. **Performance budget stated**: 16.7ms frame; expensive knobs are blur taps, raymarch steps, fBM octaves, full-res multipass, overdraw; cheap wins are half/quarter-res post upsampled, precomputed static fields, early-outs, one shared driver buffer. No CPU readback in the hot loop.
11. **Edges**: `smoothstep` widths tied to `fwidth`; hard `step()` shimmers in motion.
12. **Accessibility is composition**: contrast on a moving ground means reserving calm space or a real panel; `prefers-reduced-motion`; pausable motion; keyboard reachable; meaning never in color alone.
13. **Data-driven, no-build where possible**; manifests are allowlists; derived cues are computed, never hand-authored; a validation step fails the build on drift.
14. **Edit the source, not the output.** Hand-patching a generated artifact is reverted by the next build.
15. **Deterministic visuals** for review: `--force-color-profile=srgb`, reduced-motion for resting state, seeded PRNG for textures.

## 10 · Process (the loop)

1. **Revisit references closely** before designing. Decompose each: substrate, reproduction process, type system (nearest available faces, flagged), hardware, layout skeleton measured off the image (sample colors, note fractional positions). Distinguish observation from inference; never claim code, parameters or pass counts that are not visible.
2. **Primary read → seven layers → coupling plan → pass order → lane → reduction** (the six moves), proportionate to the ask. Lead with the read, never with the technique list.
3. **Visual thesis + a small set of high-fidelity studies.** Resolve a hero composition, a working conversation and one HELIX interaction before extending the system.
4. **Build in stages.** Single file; one `<section>` per study; quiet gallery shell with mono captions naming the techniques.
5. **Critique what is actually rendered**: distinctiveness, clarity, material quality, motion, usefulness. Texture reads as the process or as "an effect"? Anything colliding, overflowing, floating? Leading/tracking confident, crops masked, accents alive? Fix lists per study; apply; re-render; present only when the loop converges.
6. **Refine and carry the resolved language** through the presentation. Each section makes one contribution; confident visual sequence, sparse necessary copy.
7. **Inspect the complete result at desktop and mobile.** Resolve the viewing workflow before calling anything finished.
8. **Fresh-eyes review for high-stakes work**: a separate pass/agent that did not build it. Self-review from inside the build is the weakest check.
9. **Expedience when the target is named**: name the target or reference → change the source → render once → push. Contact sheets only when asked.
10. **Deliver incrementally and early.** Julia has been left with zero work when a session ran out. Save the first usable draft before refining.
11. **Deliverables are visual**: finished examples, not lists of ideas; no internal instructions, delegation notes, meeting recaps or invented ownership in the presentation.

## 11 · Reference discipline

Every reference carries one status — **canonical · directional · historical · technical_reference · known_failure · excluded** — plus `primary_read`, `use_as_reference_for`, `do_not_inherit_automatically`, `open_questions`. Do not average statuses together; inherit mechanisms selectively; name contradictions instead of smoothing them into a generic style. Known failures are kept as correction references (paired decisions: accepted + rejected + reason). Study the mechanism behind a reference's appeal; never average palettes or reproduce surfaces.

### 13.2 Grain-gradient field
Paint at 1/8–1/10 scale; bodies are rotated elliptical belts (radial gradient held to .72 then falling to transparent, `scale(1, ry/rx)`); upscale with smoothing on (the upscale is the blur); `grain(ctx,W,H,55)`. Film overlay canvas above the type layer: random pixels, `alpha ≈ strength*(0.5+rand*0.5)`, strength 10–26.

### 13.3 Ordered dither poster
Half-res canvas + `image-rendering:pixelated`. Per pixel: ramp position `t`, `c = pal[frac(t*(n-1)) > BAYER8[(y&7)*8+(x&7)] ? i+1 : i]`. Ridgelines `H*(base + a*fbm(x*0.01)) + |noise(x*0.03)-.5|*spike + jitter ≤ H*0.006`. Sun `t += glow*glow*k`.

### 13.4 Procedural photograph → print
Leaf litter: 2-octave light field drives tone and size; asymmetric double-quadratic leaves; three depth layers via `ctx.filter = blur(2.2 / 0.9 / 0)`; midribs on large sharp leaves; ~30% get a darkened half. Finish: S-curve `(v-128)*1.22+134`, grain ±15, soft Bayer q=12, radial vignette. Halftone: 45° grid, `r = (1-lum)*cell*0.62`, ink on paper color. Duotone: `t=(lum-.5)*1.35+.62` lerped dark→light. Small features die under halftone — go chunky.

### 13.5 Luminance melt
White word on black, rotate in radians; vertical decayed max per column `acc[x]=max(src, acc[x]*dec[x])`, `dec[x]=exp(-1/L(x))`, L from noise; horizontal wander growing with depth; colorize `pow(v,1.25)` on a 7-stop hot ramp; bloom via self-draw `blur(16px)` α.55 screen + `blur(48px)` α.35; rotated dot screen strongest in darks `k = 1-0.62*(1-lum)`; radial edge vignette.

### 13.6 Spectral ribbons
Per column: hue-keyframe ramp with smoothstep between stops; diagonal refraction = top samples the ramp ~0.035 behind the bottom; `h(x)=H*(0.74+0.26*smooth(noise))` waists; vertical alpha 0→1→1→0 with 10% feathers; two blur passes fuse columns; grain ≤ 7.

### 13.7 Paper tooth
One cached 180px tile, per-pixel v 234–255 + ~54 faint horizontal fiber strokes; appended as a final element per sheet, `mix-blend-mode:multiply; opacity:.5; z-index:9`.

### 13.9 Metal that is not clip art
Cast shadow first (blur 7 wide + blur 2.5 tight, offset with the scene light) → pressure darkening on the paper → body clipped to a rounded rect: 7-stop environment banding across, `h*2` random horizontal micro-streaks at α .03–.08 alternating light/dark, end-cap gradient along → dark rim stroke + one crisp specular at 30% width. Clamps and rings after, with their own small shadows.

### 13.10 Blind emboss
`color: <substrate>; text-shadow: 1px 1px 1px rgba(255,255,255,.16), -1px -1px 1px rgba(0,0,0,.5)` (swap signs for emboss vs deboss).
