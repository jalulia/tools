The Cave Rig

A platformer whose levels are real shadows. Light a wall with a projector, put objects in the beam, and a green square named Plato walks on whatever dark shape lands there. Turn the table and the level rebuilds under his feet.

Six moves

01

Read the trick

Three layers share one wall: light, shadow, sprite. The projector is the lamp and the screen at once. Objects block its light, and the blocked area is the level. A camera reads the wall back, and the game keeps one fact per pixel: dark or lit. Plato is physics on that bitmap. Nothing in the code knows what a block is.

Plato is drawn with projector light, and the physics keeps him out of solid pixels, so he can never stand where the projector can’t reach. He is visible by construction. inferred

Field note

A shadow is a projection: 3D to 2D, lossy, many objects collapsed into one outline. Information design makes the same move. Name one projection you made recently. What did it throw away, and who was standing on it?

03

Close the loop

Camera → warp → luminance → threshold → mask → physics → projector. One frame through all of it, about 30 times a second. These are the live stages of the bench above:

Never project anything dark. The camera can’t tell a drawn dark pixel from a real shadow, so dark UI becomes floor. Keep every projected pixel brighter than the threshold.

Past roughly a tenth of a second of lag, Plato trails the shadow he stands on. estimate Tune by eye.

Field note

The game measures darkness and calls it ground. What does your current instrument actually measure, and what do you say it measures? Write both.

04

Calibrate

Camera pixels and projector pixels disagree about where everything is. A homography fixes it: project four dots, click them in the camera image, solve an 8×8 linear system, and every camera pixel maps to a projector pixel.

Recalibrate when the camera or projector moves. The turntable moves freely. It’s content.

To try it here: switch to Your footage, load a photo of a wall taken at an angle, press Pin 4 corners, and click the corners in order top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left.

Field note

Calibration is agreement between two views of one wall. Which two views of a current project are out of alignment? Name the four points you’d pin.

05

Sculpt levels

OperatorWhat it does to the level
Rotate the tableFloors become walls. The level rewrites continuously.
Bring the lamp closerMagnification is Lz / (Lz − Pz). Closer lamp, bigger level.
Raise the lampEverything drops on the wall. Tall pieces stop reaching the top.
StackTowers, ledges, overhangs.
Lean a dowelA ramp, or a beam to balance on.
Your handA live platform. Or a crusher.

Crushing is a design decision. When a shadow grows into Plato, this engine lifts him up to 16 px, so a rising shadow works as an elevator. Respawn him instead and you have a different genre.

Field note

You shape the level by moving things the player can’t see. What’s the turntable in your practice: the thing you rotate offstage that rewrites someone else’s ground?

06

Turn it on yourself

Plato’s prisoners take shadows for reality. In this game the shadow is the only reality the player has, and it holds weight.

Your practice walks into an unfamiliar domain, refuses its surface, and builds an instrument to reach the structure underneath. This rig is that move made physical. The operator holds the structure. The player lives on the surface. The camera is the instrument between them, and it only ever sees the surface.

Field note

When is the surface the right thing to stand on? Name a case where refusing it would have cost you.
