FX-01

Stipple ramp

Tone made of whole dots. Value sets how many sites print (FM) or how big each dot is (AM); a blue-noise rank decides which sites, so sparse areas thin evenly.

Recipe

  1. Lay a site lattice at the pitch (CSS px). Jitter each site inside its cell.
  2. Read the value field at the site: linear ramp, radial, or noise — eased from → to by the dropoff curve.
  3. Screen: FM keeps a site when its rank < value; AM keeps every site and sets radius ∝ √value. The FM↔AM dial interpolates.
  4. Rank comes from a 64 × 64 void-and-cluster tile (blue), a hash (white), or Bayer 8 × 8 (ordered).
  5. Rasterize each kept dot analytically (7 vertex radii, AA by distance) into a coverage layer, then mix ink into the ground per pixel in the chosen color space. Ink B overprints by the overprint rule.
  6. The mask is sampled at the site, not the pixel: dots are whole or absent, never sliced by the edge.

Limits

ParamRangeWhat happens
pitch< 2 pxsites merge into flat tone at 1× — the stipple stops reading as marks
dot radius> ½ pitchdots overlap into a solid; FM loses its range
selectionwhiteclumps and voids at low density — the original jittered-hash look
screenAM at pitch < 4 pxradius steps quantize to the pixel grid; banding

FX-02

Ink leaving paper

A solid that runs out. Coverage eases off across a warped edge; inside the transition it resolves to binary speckle (blue-noise threshold, optionally streaked along the stroke), with starved pinholes and film mottle in the body.

Recipe

  1. Distance to the edge line along the stroke direction (CSS px), displaced by fbm warp.
  2. Coverage = dropoff curve over the transition width.
  3. Speckle: perturb coverage by a clump noise (clump size, stretched along the stroke by streak), then threshold it per device pixel against the blue-noise rank. Speckle 0 → continuous tone, 1 → pure binary.
  4. Starve: clumped pinholes, likelier where coverage is thin. Mottle: low-frequency film thickness, taken off coverage.
  5. Mask × coverage × ink density, mixed into the ground per pixel in the chosen color space.

Limits

ParamRangeWhat happens
transition< 4 pxreads as a hard vector edge — no speckle band to see
clump size> 8 pxclumps read as blotches, not ink
speckle< .3the edge becomes a gradient — the "AI gradient" failure
streak> 6marks read as scanlines unless the angle is off-axis

FX-03

Paper tooth

White stock that is not a screen. On white, tooth can only take light away, so it is built as depth: a height field shaded by flat value or a raking light, plus laid fibers and pits, all as an OKLab L offset with an optional tint in the pits.

Recipe

  1. Height field = fbm at the tooth scale; octaves and persistence set the spectrum (how fast detail drops off).
  2. Distribution curve reshapes height (more valleys, or more peaks). Rake mixes flat value with relief lit from the light angle (finite-difference gradient).
  3. Fibers: curved strokes at a count per 100 × 100 px, laid near the grain angle with spread; pits: small dots. Both rasterized into separate coverage channels.
  4. Depth = amplitude × shade (+ fibers, pits), optionally stronger toward the plate edges by the falloff curve.
  5. Shade: L −= depth in OKLab (a/b pulled toward the tint by depth) — or multiply in linear / sRGB, for comparison.

Limits

ParamRangeWhat happens
amplitude> .06 Lreads as dirt, not stock
tooth scale< 1 pxcollapses into per-pixel noise — digital grain, not paper
fibers> 30 / 100 px²felt, not paper
shade in sRGB—the same depth looks ~2× stronger than in linear; keep oklab for predictable steps

FX-04

Weave

Cloth, built as cloth: warp and weft threads with their own counts, a real interlacing pattern, slubbed thread width, cylindrical shading along every float, under-threads in shadow, whole accent threads and worn floats.

Recipe

  1. Warp columns and weft rows at their own counts (threads per 100 CSS px); each thread gets a jittered center, a thickness, a dye-lot L offset, and accent membership.
  2. Interlacing pattern decides which thread is on top at each crossing; a float is the run of crossings where it stays on top.
  3. Thread half-width = fill × pitch / 2, modulated along its length by slub noise. AA coverage from distance to the centerline.
  4. Shading in OKLab L: across the thread (cylinder) and along the float (dives under at both ends); the under-thread sits in shadow.
  5. Wear thins and fades floats with a probability that rises along the wear direction by its curve; a worn float shows more of the thread beneath and the ground through the gap.

Limits

ParamRangeWhat happens
count> 40 /100 px at 1×threads under 2.5 px alias against the display — moiré
fill< .5reads as a grid of strokes, not cloth
fill> .97gutters close; the weave flattens to a check
accent rate> .15accents stop being accents — a plaid

Template contract

Make another set

The sheet does not know what an effect draws. Copy this folder, replace set.js with your own effects and usages, run node build-sheet.mjs (bakes the static read, set.json and template.json), then register it in _shared/entries.js with section:'templates'.

Rules the sheet enforces by construction: one params object per effect; every length in CSS px; density dials change counts, not zoom; inks in OKLCH, chroma reduced at constant L/h when out of sRGB; partial coverage mixed by an explicit space; masks sample at the mark, so marks are whole or absent.

fx.jsshared core · noise, 64² blue-noise rank (void-and-cluster), ease curves, OKLab/OKLCH + gamut, mix and overprint rules, randomized mask, canvas I/O
set.jsthis set · effects (params, recipe, limits, run) and usages — the only file a new set replaces
sheet.js · sheet.cssgeneric runtime · rack, blocks, dials (limit zones hatched on the track, default tick, OKLCH gradient tracks, ease graphs, mix strip), loupe, views, usage gallery, queue
set.json · template.jsonmachine-readable spec of the set and of the contract
index.htmlbuild output · static read baked in
