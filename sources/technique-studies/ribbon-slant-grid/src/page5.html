The coil lands on the word.

The ribbon stays ST-22’s band. Only its constraints change, in nib widths: x-height, ascender, descender, nib angle, slant, stroke pitch. Seen from the front, the coil is a sine wave. Its crests and troughs are the x-height and the baseline, and the wordmark’s own front view says which crests rise and which troughs drop. Three lockups then land it on the real wordmark: the ribbon is never bent into letters.

The numbers

Measured values are marked as measured. Values taken from a calligraphy source say which. Anything this study chose says derived.

  • Measured · ST-22 p10 defaultsST-22 already has a hand: a band 180 px wide on a coil 594 px across is an x-height of 3.30 nib widths, nib at 0°, with a pitch of 0.44 nib widths a turn that opens toward the sweep.
  • Measured · wordmark front viewThe wordmark’s stems are 45 units and its x-height 128: 2.84 nib widths, upright, with ascenders at 1.00 and descenders at 0.76. Inside m, n and u the stems repeat every 81.5 to 82.5 units, 1.81 to 1.83 nib widths.
  • Cited · Textura Quadrata · derived comparisonTextura Quadrata is written about 5 nib widths high with counters and letter spaces of about one nib width. At a 40° nib a vertical stroke is cos 40° = 0.77 wide, so stroke plus counter is 1.77. The wordmark’s stem rhythm, 1.82, sits beside it.
  • Derived · exactA helix of pitch p on a coil of diameter X, seen from the front, leans by its lead angle. Here the cylinder is sheared by that angle, so the near strokes stand on the slant lines and the far strokes become the diagonal hairlines. With variable pitch the shear is taken per sample.lead = atan(p / (π · X)) · x′ = x + (mid − y) · (tan σ + dx/dψ ÷ amplitude)
  • Measured · ascenders and descenders from the front viewFor each of the 13 downstrokes, the ink extent in a ±5-unit column of the wordmark: i and d reach 45 units above the x-height, t 32, and both g strokes 34 below the baseline. That skyline is a field over x, so a crest that passes through the d’s slot rises while the coil turns.downstroke k at ψ = π/2 + 2πk · crest before k = top(k) · trough after k = bottom(k)
  • Derived · spheresST-22 has twelve spheres; thirteen downstrokes leave twelve counters. Each sphere sits in one, on the axis, at 0.36 nib widths.

Grids from other records

The Latin guide sheet is one way to rule a page for writing. These sit beside it; none is the original.

Northern Europe, 12th–16th c.

Minims and the picket fence

In formal blackletter the minims reach the regularity of a picket fence, relieved only by hairlines from the nib’s corner (Britannica). Textura, Rotunda, Schwabacher and Fraktur are the four usual families. Blokland (Leiden, 2016) argues that early textura and roman type shared standardized widths in units, a grid inside the type itself.

Britannica · Blokland 2016

United States, 19th–20th c.

Slant lines

Pointed-pen guide sheets rule slant lines at 52° (Spencerian) or 55° (Copperplate) from the baseline. Spencerian adds a second, connective slant of about 30°. A 1903 US patent for a penmanship chart rules unit parallelograms at about 50°, with connectives at about 25°.

Spencerian · US 723,338

China · and the Arabic proportioned scripts

Nine palaces, rice grid, the dot

Chinese practice sheets divide each character square into nine (the nine-palace grid, 九宮格), four (the field grid, 田字格) or eight with diagonals (the rice-character grid, 米字格). One study compares the nine-palace grid to painters’ transfer grids and Tang map grids. The proportioned Arabic scripts measure letters in rhombic dots, the mark of the pen’s own width. The rice-character grid is the guide here when the coil is seen down its axis.

Leiden 2020, ch. 4 · The Met
