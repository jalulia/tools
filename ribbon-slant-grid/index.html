Ribbon study · ST-22 airbrush ribbon → the mindgut wordmark and mark V11 · 2026-11-27

The ribbon becomes the m. The camera finds the word.

From ST-22’s still to the mindgut wordmark. The band interpolates to an m in three overturns, then flows onto the letter m itself, along the m’s own skeleton, in one of three ways: Ductus, Write or Press. It takes the mark’s ink and hands over to the real m with no visible change. Parallax keeps the ribbon unchanged and finds the word by viewpoint. The wordmark itself is never morphed or recolored. The Mindgut 2.2 mark studies are at the end.

REGS
Registration0 · 0 · 1
Interpolates every sample from ST-22 (1.85 turns, radius 297, band 180) to the m master (3 turns, radius 64, band 45, axis yawed 11.7° so the front view is a cycloid: cusped feet, round overturns). Turns, radius, width and yaw move together.
FORM
Ribbon m to letter m0 · 0 · 1
Every sample moves to its landmark on the m’s own skeleton (each turn of the ribbon has a stroke to land on): depth falls away, width goes from the band’s 45 to the letter’s 45 and 34, and the band turns face-up, rolling through edge-on where it has to turn over.
PHAS
Turn, time-driven0 · 0 · 1
The coil’s turn about its axis, driven by time rather than set by hand (hidden, as a hidden axis is in OpenType). Stroke slots stay fixed, so every whole turn registers again.
LITE
Helicesfollows REGS
ST-22’s three light helices become the skyline (ascenders and descenders read from the wordmark’s front view), the x-height line and the baseline.
SPHR
Spheresfollows REGS
The twelve spheres land on ink only: the ten strokes outside the m, the tittle and the ®.
